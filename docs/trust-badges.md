official program pages, with what each costs, how long it takes, and whether hurulab qualifies.

## The two catches that matter most

**NVIDIA Inception explicitly excludes consulting and outsourced development firms.** hurulab
would have to apply as a company building its own AI product, not as an agency, and the product
must be visible on the site before applying. **If the website reads as an agency site, expect
rejection.** The fallback is NVIDIA Connect, which is free and explicitly open to service
providers, but may not include a public badge. You can be in one or the other, not both.

**Dell's partner enrolment requires at least one developer employed**, along with a business
registration number and a company website on a matching email domain.

## The eight, in short

**NVIDIA Inception.** Free, rolling admission, 2 to 4 weeks review. Apply at
programs.nvidia.com/phoenix/application with a three or four sentence product description.

**Dell Technologies partner.** Register at dell.my.site.com/PartnerEnrollment, Taiwan, solution
provider track. Approval quoted at 5 to 7 business days, authorised tier free. The harder **Dell
AI ecosystem certified** designation needs a real product validated against Dell AI Factory
reference architectures, through the Dell Taiwan partner team, 1 to 4 months of engineering.

**HPE technology partner.** Register at partner.hpe.com/web/prp. Entry tier historically free.
**HPE is unifying everything into Partner Ready Vantage on 1 November 2026, so reconfirm the
mechanics after that date.** Their AI badge, Unleash AI, is curated and effectively invitation
only: 6 to 18 months for a small unknown firm.

**AIEC, Taiwan's AI evaluation center.** aiec.org.tw, a Ministry of Digital Affairs initiative
with ITRI. **The most on-brand badge of the eight for a Taiwan AI company**, and testing is
currently free during the pilot. The catch: it evaluates a specific AI product or system, not
the company, so it needs a deployed system or an API endpoint. Contact AIECService@aiec.org.tw.

**ISO 27001.** Information security certification, valid three years with annual surveillance.
Route: a compliance platform (Vanta, Drata, Secureframe, roughly USD 10,000 a year) or a DIY
toolkit. Audits in Taiwan run NTD 200,000 to 400,000 through BSI, SGS or TUV Rheinland, in
Chinese or English. **ISO 42001, the AI management standard, is rarer and therefore more
differentiating for an AI consultancy, and much cheaper bolted onto an existing 27001 with the
same body. Do 27001 first, 42001 second.**

**SOC 2.** Not a certification, an attestation by a licensed CPA firm. Type II is the one buyers
take seriously. **Never say "SOC 2 certified": it is attested, not certified.** Doing this on the
same compliance platform as ISO 27001 is meaningfully cheaper than doing them apart.

**Google Developer Expert.** Individual recognition, not a company badge, and it belongs to the
person rather than to hurulab. A referral from a current expert or a Google employee is
mandatory to even get the form. No design category exists, so the candidate is an engineer.

**NVIDIA Cloud Validated. Skip.** It requires operating a commercial GPU cloud at data center
scale. The exact phrasing no longer appears on nvidia.com, so a competitor displaying it is
likely showing a legacy badge, or one outside its intended scope. **Worth knowing when selling
against them.**

## The reading of a competitor's badge wall

Of the eight badges on the competitor site this was researched against, only three cost real
money and effort. The vendor partner badges are largely free registrations. **For a Taiwan buyer
the strongest signals are the government-backed AIEC mark and the audited ones.**

hurulab can match the free tier in weeks, the AIEC mark as soon as there is a submittable
product, and the audited tier within a year on a modest budget.

**One open legal question, unanswered since 4 August 2026:** whether MoreHarvest's *credentials*,
meaning badges and certifications held by that company, can be shown under hurulab. The owner
ruled on 7 September that the *work* may be presented as hurulab's because it is the same
people, but a certification is issued to a legal entity and that is a different question. It
stays open.
