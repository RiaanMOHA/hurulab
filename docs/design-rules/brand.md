## 3. What hurulab is

**Settled: a consultancy.** Owner decision, 30 July 2026, reversing the 8 July choice of
"agency". Henry and BY have not been told.

The interviews disagreed, and the disagreement is recorded here rather than smoothed over,
because it says something the decision does not.

| Person | Their word for it |
|---|---|
| Riaan | "A new kind of agency combining AI, tech and design" |
| Henry | "A design and AI agency" |
| BY | "A design and AI agency" |
| Cal | "A consultancy partner" and separately, "I don't like the word agency, it feels too mercenary" |
| Pedro | "A technology partner" |

Source: Q2 and Q5, all five.

Three said agency, one said consultancy, one said partner. Worth noting that the one dissenter
gave a reason: Cal said "agency" feels too mercenary. That reason lines up with section 5,
where the buyer is a company already burned by an agency. Naming yourself the category your
buyer distrusts is the argument for the decision, not against it.

**Where the five actually agree**, and this is stronger than the label:

> Business, design and technology in one team that understands the business before it builds.

Riaan: "Business, design and tech in one team that understands the business and builds it
fast." Henry: "Business, design and technology in one team, and a proven method, not one off
experiments." Pedro: "Design and AI in one team. Most shops do one or the other, hurulab does
both deeply in the same small team." BY: "Strong design and real AI skill in the same team."
Cal: "Product management, engineering and design in one team." (Q2 and Q13, all five)

That is the only claim all five make unprompted, in their own words, to two different
questions. It is the foundation.

## 6. Where AI sits

Answered directly, and the answer is three to two.

**AI is a tool, not the product.** "A tool we use." (Q16, Cal) "AI is a tool behind the
scenes." (Q16, Henry) "A tool behind the scenes. AI makes the team faster and better, but
clients buy outcomes, not AI." (Q16, Pedro)

**AI skill is the differentiator.** "We actually know how to use AI, how to train people to
use it, and how to build with it, and we use design taste so it doesn't look like AI slop."
(Q16, Riaan) "We build software with AI and design the workflow, deciding for each part
whether AI or ordinary code is the right tool." (Q16, BY)

**Settled, 30 July 2026. AI does not lead. It explains.**

AI is never the promise. It is the answer to the one question the promise provokes: how can you
possibly build it before I pay for it. So it appears once, as mechanism, after the work and the
proof, and never in the hero.

Three reasons this is the decision and not a compromise. The single idea in section 7 mentions
capital and using the thing, and does not mention AI. The buyer in section 5 is already
"drowning in tools, no clear results", so leading with AI adds to what they are drowning in.
And "AI-first" in Taiwan is already occupied by a competitor running a content operation
nobody here can outspend, so the ground is taken (evidence, part 4).

Both interview positions survive this. AI is not what is sold, and competence with AI is what
makes the speed real. Pedro put it plainest, and it came from the developer: "clients buy
outcomes, not AI".

## 7. The single idea

Question 4 asked what hurulab stands for if it stands for one thing, and got five answers.

- Riaan: "Once we are done we become useless, because we have given you everything to continue
  on your own, not just the product but the tools and training to keep doing it."
- Cal: "Outcomes over process."
- Henry: "A highly efficient method that helps companies get more out of their capital, their
  money, time and labour."
- BY: "Technology made human."
- Pedro: "Craft."

**Settled, 30 July 2026.** The single idea is two of these joined, chosen by the owner:

> **Companies waste capital because they buy software they have never used. So you use it
> before you pay for it.**

Henry's answer supplies the reason and Riaan's lead promise supplies the mechanism. This
pairing is better evidenced than any single Q4 answer: capital waste is the only idea two
people reached independently and unprompted, both naming it at Q6, and "use it before you buy
it" is the only claim confirmed unoccupied in Taiwan by direct search in both languages
(evidence, part 4).

**What this means for the design.** Sober and demonstrative rather than persuasive. Numbers
forward. Working things in preference to pictures of things. The page should behave rather
than describe.

**Settled, 30 July 2026: the site is itself the first demo.** The visitor uses something real
on the page. This is the only form where the promise and the artifact agree, and it is the
strongest available answer to the credibility problem, because there has never been a cold win
and the site's job today is to convince someone who arrived through an introduction.

The failure mode to design against is a gimmick: an interaction that demonstrates nothing about
whether hurulab can build the visitor's actual product. Whatever the visitor uses has to be
evidence, not decoration. If it does not survive the question "what did that prove", it is
wrong.

**On "we make ourselves useless".** This is Riaan's own answer to Q4 and Riaan is the owner of
this project, so it carries weight. It is not the single idea, but it is not withdrawn either. It is a stated intention, not an observed result. No
client has yet run alone after a handover, and no interview claims one has. It can be
published as what hurulab is trying to do. It cannot be published as something that has
happened.

The previous version called this "a marketing idea rather than something observed". That was
wrong about where it came from. It is the founder-designer's answer to the central question.
It is still unproven.

## 10. What can be proved

All of these are in `docs/evidence.md`, part 3, which kept only the claims citing a file, a
commit count or a date.

- **jadegia storefront.** Kickoff 28 May 2026, two complete brand directions demonstrated
  12 days later on 9 June, client chose a hybrid of the two at the demo. Jade at or above
  NT$200,000 shows "available by appointment" and cannot be added to the bag, so the commerce
  model cannot be misconfigured. (evidence, jadegia)
- **The 3D house, Ozu-1.** 76 commits across 8 merged pull requests, 22 April to 22 May 2026,
  verified against `git log`. Rebuilt from a blueprint at 400 dpi and 933 interior
  photographs. A source-of-truth hierarchy was set once and cited by every later room build:
  blueprint beats photos, beats room maps, beats code comments, beats memory, and nothing is
  eyeballed. (evidence, Ozu-1)
- **The haramizu landing page.** 121 commits across 25 pull requests in about 20 days, one
  feature per pull request. Live 26 March 2026, maintained in-house by the client with no
  vendor lock-in. (evidence, haramizu)
- **The kozue fund site.** Nine days, 30 March to 7 April 2026. 75 commits, 27 pull requests,
  all merged. Note that two older source files say 76; 75 is the verified git figure.
  (evidence, kozue)
- **The value-add prototype.** 28 production step components. One locked copy source feeds both
  the iPad app and the PDF, so the pitch and the leave-behind cannot drift apart. Apple's own
  interface guidance was overruled on three named points with the reason recorded each time.
  (evidence, value-add prototype)
- **Work thrown away.** A finished page-transition effect deleted because jadegia read cleaner
  without it. Frosted glass killed across all 20 steps of the pitch and the depth rebuilt from
  a 1px border and a shadow. A splash screen killed once it stopped earning its place.

## 11. What cannot be claimed

Each of these is a limit the record itself sets.

- **No measured client outcome.** No analytics on any client site, and the case studies say so
  in their own gap sections.
- **No cold win.** "So far it's been leveraging existing connections through Henry." (Q12, Cal)
  Every engagement came through someone already known.
- **No funding result from the pitch.** "No external investor has seen the finished deck. It
  has been shown internally only." (evidence, value-add prototype)
- **No client has run alone.** The "we make ourselves useless" idea has no instance behind it.
- **No client quote on record.** jadegia approved the flagship direction and nobody wrote down
  why.
- **The 2.5 year platform figure is soft.** It is a midpoint between two numbers given in the
  same session. Ed ran that project and should pull the real number from release history
  before it is said out loud.
- **No invented case studies.** The 29 July page carried three written to be plausible. Real
  ones exist. Use those.

**The rules those limits set.** The list above is what is true. This is what it forbids on a
page. These are constraints, not preferences.

- **Do not claim a measured outcome.** Naming a measure is allowed. Claiming a result is not.
- **Do not publish an invented case study.** Use the real ones.
- **Do not show a placeholder client logo.** A row reading "client one, client two" advertises
  that the answer is empty. Say the names are not yet ours to publish, or say nothing.
- **Do not claim a cold win.** There has not been one.
- **Do not lead with "we make ourselves useless".** No instance exists. It may be stated as an
  intention, never as a result.

## 15. The visual identity

**Restarted from a blank page on 30 July 2026, then rebuilt the same day** from
`hurulab-codebase`, the owner's Lovable build, whose stated visual reference is squareblack.com.

**This section owns nothing.** The system now lives in its own files, one per topic:
[language.md](language.md), [color.md](color.md), [type.md](type.md), [mark.md](mark.md),
[layout.md](layout.md), [motion.md](motion.md), [breakpoints.md](breakpoints.md) and
[copy.md](copy.md). Start at [README.md](README.md).

The typefaces settled 31 July 2026: Geist for headings, IBM Plex Sans for body, owned by
[type.md](type.md). The accent color is settled as purple, hue 318, re-approved 31 July 2026
against indigo and red. The mark is settled as `*hurulab`, the typeface's own asterisk, with
only its motion still to be designed ([mark.md](mark.md) section 4).

**Released in the restart, and not reinstated.** Recorded so nothing returns by accident.

- **The palettes.** The 6 July warm greige and amber, the 8 July cold neutrals, and the
  undocumented palette on the 29 July page. The current neutrals come from the Lovable build,
  not from any of these.
- **The typefaces.** Schibsted Grotesk, Instrument Sans and Sometype Mono. So is the ban on
  serifs and italics.
- **The spacing rules.** The 24px maximum gap and the fixed-pixel rule. Superseded entirely by
  the rem system in [breakpoints.md](breakpoints.md), which has no fixed pixels.
- **The mark.** The drawn paperclip, `logo-old.png`, is not the logo. Neither is the type-set
  wordmark from the 10 July brief. The mark is now `*hurulab` and is owned by
  [mark.md](mark.md).
