# Part 1. The funnel and pricing document

**Source.** HL-SOP-2026-001 v1.1, the CEO's master one-pager, marked internal use. Reproduced

Five stages · two contracts · one credit bridge · three pricing options.

### S0 · Filter first: three gates before any demo

- Log inquiries same day; 15-30 min intake call within 3 days.
- Gates: (1) concrete operational pain, (2) they've spent money on it, (3) decision-maker attends the demo in person.
- Passed leads: also decide which industry template to pre-load for the demo.

*all 3 gates pass →*

### S1 · Interview-as-demo: one meeting, 60 minutes

> **DEAD, owner ruling 7 September 2026. There is no free live demo.** The stage below is
> reproduced because this file is the record of the CEO's document, not because it is policy.
> Nothing is built for a prospect before they pay. The replacement discovery process is coming
> from the owner and will be recorded when it arrives.


- Two operators, no exceptions: Henry interviews, Delta builds; build starts at minute 15, not after.
- Min 30-40: methodology talk as buffer; min 40, read the signal: present, or pivot to fallback.
- Map every screen to their words; live only, no code, no links; they take home a screenshot PDF.
- Final 5 min: the discovery pitch; proposal next day, follow-ups day 3/7, nurture at day 14.

*client agrees to pay →*

### S2 · Paid discovery: figure out what to build

- The line: "the demo took an hour because we've done it many times; knowing what to build for you is the hard part."
- Fixed price, fixed 1-2 weeks; NDA signed now, real data enters here.
- Won't pay for discovery → walk; if it shouldn't be built → deliver the report, keep the fee, say so honestly.

*findings report ships →*

### S3 · Scope and quote: the three-option menu

Discovery report becomes the contract appendix; every quote offers three options:

- **A, subscription** (default pitch): setup fee + monthly retainer, we run operations and model swaps.
- **B, buyout**: setup × 2.5-3, code and rights transfer, no retainer; methodology and templates stay ours; 90-day warranty then per-incident billing; model swaps not included.
- **C, subscribe-then-buy**: buyout option after 12 months, price = B minus 50% of retainer paid, the safety valve for lock-in-averse clients.

B is priced to make A look good, not to sell B; in negotiation, scope down, never price down.

*both sides agree →*

### S4 · Sign and write the predictions down

- Contract: scope appendix, payment milestones, discovery credit clause.
- On signing day, freeze predictions (timeline, effort, margin), grade at close (GTL).

---

### How the money works (§S2.5 essentials)

- Two contracts: small discovery agreement (1-2 pages) paid first; big build contract later, never negotiate the big one with someone who hasn't paid.
- Discovery fee = 5-15% of the build value, 100% upfront.
- Build contract signed within 90 days → discovery fee credits in full against the setup fee (never the retainer; expires after 90 days).
- Three outcomes, fee never refunded: they proceed → credit applies; we say no → fee earned, honest advice delivered; they walk → report is theirs, methodology stays ours.
- Build contract: define the milestones first, then set payments accordingly, each payment tied to an accepted milestone (credit netted out of the first payment); retainer from go-live.

---

### Three principles never to forget

1. Polished but incomplete: mock data, single slice, labelled gaps; no matter how good the room feels, the artefact never leaves.
2. Understanding their needs is billable: discovery is our most valuable expertise, not a cost of sales.
3. The asset library is the compounding engine: file components the same day; the thicker the library, the better the next 30-minute build.

`trust-reliability-efficiency`
trust, reliability, efficiency, this is the product we sell; ai is just our method

We are not selling ai tools. Models are commodities we route between. What we sell is the thing enterprises cannot buy anywhere else: the ability to trust ai, rely on it, and afford it, with receipts. The cycle below is how each of the three is manufactured, step by step, and why all three get measurably better every round.

### `trust`
a glass box, not a black box

Every claim clicks back to its source. Every prediction is frozen before we act and graded after. The system can never lie about whether it was right.

### `reliability`
structural, not probabilistic

Facts live in a ledger, never in a model. Anything with one correct answer goes to code. Accounts don't hallucinate, because accounts never pass through a model.

### `efficiency`
every cent has a receipt

Every task routes to the cheapest engine that answers correctly. Every cost is logged. The routing itself is graded, so cost per outcome falls every round.

# Part 3. How eight other firms sell a staged engagement

**History, not truth.** These are other companies' published prices and promises, studied
3 September 2026 with every page visited and quoted live. None of it is a hurulab decision and
nothing here may be presented as a hurulab claim. The prices were live on 3 September 2026 and
are recorded as what the market shows, not as a guide to what hurulab should charge.

## 1. What the pages actually publish

| Firm | First step | Its published price |
|---|---|---|
| A Cultivated Mindset | Discovery Sprint, 2 weeks | Starting at $7,500 |
| Crux Digits | Audit | €2,500, credited toward the build |
| Brival | Code audit, fixed diagnostic | Starts at $5K, never credited |
| Gradion | Audit producing a business case | Published |
| eflury | Automation audit | Published |
| The AI Consultancy | Diagnostic Audit | Published, in a flat table |
| LunoMotion | Five-phase arc | Hidden, quoted on the call |
| Boom Consulting | Fixed-fee discovery sprint | Hidden, quoted on the call |

**Pages doing all three things at once, a true ordered progression, every step priced, and an
ownership promise on the discovery artifact, are genuinely rare.** The sequence-strongest pages
mostly hide their numbers.

---

## 2. The four mechanics worth knowing

**The first step produces the next step's price.** A Cultivated Mindset's discovery sprint
delivers "a scoped roadmap, a working technical spike, and a clear price for the next phase."
The rung is not credited forward; it manufactures the quote.

**Or the first step is credited forward.** Crux Digits and eflury credit the audit fee against
the build. This is what part 1 above already proposes for hurulab, at 5 to 15% of build value
credited within 90 days.

**Or it is deliberately never credited, and that is the pitch.** Brival, and this is the
sharpest sentence in the whole study:

> "Fixed because a diagnosis shouldn't be a negotiation: the price is set before you commit,
> and it stands alone, never credited toward follow-on work, because **a diagnosis discounted
> by the cure stops being a diagnosis.**"

**The walk-away is stated plainly on the page, not in fine print.** Four of the eight:

> "No obligation to proceed. The audit stands on its own as a deliverable." (Gradion)
> "If automation does not pay off in your case, the report says so, in writing, before you have
> spent anything on a build." (eflury)
> "You leave with enough to take the project anywhere, including to someone else if we mutually
> decide Boom Consulting isn't the right fit." (Boom)

---

## 3. Where the ownership promise sits, which is the finding for hurulab

The firms that attach ownership to the **first paid step** put the line inside that step's
deliverable list:

> "All of it is yours, to use as you see fit, even if we don't move to build together." (Boom,
> last bullet of Discovery's deliverables)
> "All written, all yours, usable with us or with anyone else." (eflury)
> "A clear roadmap, yours to keep." (Crux Digits, on the €2,500 audit card)

LunoMotion's strongest ownership language sits at the **bottom** of the page and describes the
finished build, not the discovery artifact.

**The consequence, if hurulab's promise is ownership from day one:** it belongs inside step
one's deliverables at the top of the page, not deferred to the last step. **This is currently
unresolved**: whether the client keeps the discovery output from day one or "for the interim"
is contradiction four of the five in `docs/evidence.md` part 11, and the CEO has not ruled.

---

## 4. Two failure modes

**Instalments disguised as a ladder.** Weblogic.ie shows "four phases" that are stages of a
single €7,995 build with staged invoicing: "design sign-off triggers the second 30% invoice."
You do not buy phase one, decide, then buy phase two. You buy the whole thing and it is
delivered in phases. **Readers can tell.**

**A flat table with no arrow of causation.** The AI Consultancy lists six packages at equal
visual weight; only one sentence, "the Diagnostic Audit is the required first step", makes it a
sequence at all. Without it, the ladder reads as a menu.

**A ladder stops being a progression the moment** finishing a rung is not a real decision point
with its own price and its own walk-away, or the steps sit side by side as interchangeable
options rather than in a caused order.

---

## 5. Two ways to draw it that nobody tried

Kept because they are the only untested ideas in the study, and because they suit hurulab's own
position rather than being borrowed from a page.

**The accumulating ownership trail.** Every example draws time, durations and weeks, or scope,
bigger deliverables. **None draws what the client already owns growing at each rung.** A
vertical staircase where each passed step leaves a "yours now" marker behind it, the roadmap
after step one, the prototype after step two, so the reader watches their own pile grow
regardless of whether they buy the next rung. It reframes each purchase as buying something you
keep rather than renting access to a process.

**The fork drawn at every rung.** None of the eight drew, at each step, the explicit choice:
stop here and this is exactly what you walk away with, or continue and this is what is added.
Boom gestures at it in a separate "scope variations" section rather than in the ladder itself.
Building the exit into every rung makes the walk-away **visible rather than asserted.**

Both are drawings, not decisions. What hurulab charges and what the client keeps are owner and
CEO questions, unresolved, recorded in `docs/evidence.md` part 11.
