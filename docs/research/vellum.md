One note before the report: the site I studied is \*\*Vellum.ai\*\*, a subscription AI personal-assistant product. It is \*not\* the five-step, pay-as-you-go consulting engagement you described selling. So treat this as a teardown of a reference site whose mechanics you might borrow — not as a description of your own model. Where your model and theirs diverge (steps, ownership, "paid before next begins"), I've flagged it. Everything quoted is copied verbatim from the pages.

\#\# Business model

What's sold: a subscription to a personal AI assistant ("Personal Intelligence"), priced monthly, plus metered "credits" for usage. Someone becomes a paying customer by signing up free, then upgrading to a paid monthly package. There is a genuine free tier and the reader is pushed to \*use the real thing before paying\* — every primary button routes to \`/account/signup\`, i.e. into the product itself, not a sales call.

The free tier / smallest first step is stated on the pricing page:

\> Start free.Your first tasks are on us.

\> Set up your assistant in minutes and start getting things done.

The smallest paid step is the "Mighty" package at $30/month. The paid tiers, verbatim:

\> Mighty — More capacity for consistent use. — $30 /month — Small computer, 10 GB storage, $25 in credits included

\> Super — Do more, think less about the how. — $100 /month — Medium computer, 30 GB storage, $45 in credits included, Assistant email and subdomain

\> Ultra — Your second brain, running full speed. — $200 /month — Large computer, 60 GB storage, $115 in credits included, Assistant email and subdomain

\> Custom Plan — Build a plan that fits exactly how you work.

Where money first appears and how far in: on the \*\*homepage\*\*, money never appears at all — there is no price anywhere on it. Money first appears only when you navigate to the dedicated Pricing page, and even there the very first thing you see is "Start free," with the first dollar figure ($30, Mighty) sitting \*below\* the fold under the free CTA. So the pricing reveal is deliberately delayed and softened.

How this differs from your model: theirs is a recurring subscription with metered credits and no visible price on the main page. Yours is a sequential, one-off-per-step engagement with prices shown openly. The one mechanic worth stealing is that they let the reader \*use the real thing first\* — your equivalent is a genuinely deliverable, client-owned paid discovery step as the smallest first commitment.

The main objection the page is built against is \*"AI assistants don't actually work / don't really know me."\* It answers this in two ways. First with a relationship-over-time argument on the Product page:

Second, "Make AI finally work for you" concedes the objection openly — the word "finally" admits prior tools failed.

Promise about ownership / what the customer keeps: the closest the site comes is a \*\*data-ownership\*\* promise, not an IP/deliverable-ownership promise. Quoted in full (Product page):

\> Your life isn't training data.

\> Whether you self-host or use the managed platform, your data is yours. Conversations reach the LLM for responses, but are never used for training. Telemetry is off by default. The value flows to you.

And on the pricing page, in the Self-hosting block:

\> Vellum assistant is open source. Deploy it on your own hardware, a VPS, or a private cloud. You control your data, your keys, and your uptime SLA. No platform fee, no computer tier charges.

Where it sits and how much room it gets: the ownership language lives \*deep\* — near the bottom of the Product page and in a secondary block below all paid tiers on Pricing. It gets modest room (one short paragraph each) and is never the headline. This is the biggest gap versus your model: \*\*your "everything made in paid discovery belongs to the client from day one" is a much stronger, more central ownership promise than anything here, and you should put it far higher than they put theirs.\*\*

\#\# Sequence

Yes — the site shows an explicit staged process, most clearly on the \*\*Product page\*\*, which frames onboarding as a three-day arc:

\> You raise them.They grow with you.

\> Most people say it takes about three days before the relationship clicks.

Three phases, each named with text:

\> DAY 1: Stranger — Your assistant just met you. They're eager but clueless. Give them context — your style, role, tools, all of your preferences.

\> DAY 2: Acquaintance — They're starting to pick up patterns. They'll get things half-right and ask the right questions. Correct them when they're off.

\> DAY 3: Colleague — They finish your thought before you do. They act before you ask. You stop managing them and start relying on them.

Orientation and weight: it reads as a vertical, stacked progression (Day 1 → 2 → 3), equal in size, with no single phase dominating — progression is drawn by the escalating labels (Stranger → Acquaintance → Colleague) and by the tone shifting from "clueless" to "you start relying on them." The \*\*peak\*\* of the sequence is Day 3, "They finish your thought before you do" — the moment of full reliance.

On the pricing page there is \*also\* a sequence of sorts — the tier ladder Mighty → Super → Ultra → Custom — but it's a value ladder, not a customer journey. Note: this is a three-stage \*maturation\* model, not a five-step \*paid-gate\* model like yours. Their steps are not individually purchased and don't carry ownership at each gate. \*\*If you want a sequence like this, borrow the naming and escalation feel (each step has an identity and a payoff), but you'll need to draw the "paid, delivered, and owned before the next begins" gating yourself — nothing here shows that.\*\*

\#\# Layout

Composition is section-by-section, each a near-full-height band, mostly center-aligned with a large display heading at the top of the band and supporting copy/CTA beneath. What dominates and how the eye is led: a single large serif heading anchors each section top-center; the eye drops from heading → short subhead → interactive proof element (a chat card, a scrolling log, a comparison table) → a button, then into the next band. So the vertical rhythm is heading-then-evidence-then-ask, repeated.

How sections are bounded from each other: mainly by full-bleed background color changes — the hero and content bands are light, the "always on" band is a strong magenta/pink, the final CTA is near-black, and cards sit on soft rounded containers. The color flips act as chapter breaks.

Dense vs open: the "I handle everything you do" section is the \*\*dense\*\* one (a rapidly scrolling multi-line skill log plus overlaid cards plus a row of tool tabs), and the comparison table is dense with rows. The hero, the three-day phases, and the final "Make AI finally work for you" CTA are the \*\*open\*\*, high-whitespace moments.

Imagery and its job: the imagery is not photography — it's a pink egg/blob \*\*mascot\*\* (appears mid-scroll as two egg halves that resolve into the character) plus product-UI simulations (chat bubbles, skill logs, permission prompts, the comparison table, app/service icons like Gmail, Slack, Notion, Reddit). The job of the imagery is \*demonstration\* — it shows the product doing believable work rather than decorating. The mascot's job is warmth/personality, softening an otherwise capability-heavy argument.

\#\# Motion

This is the richest part of the site. Distinct pieces of motion and triggers:

Continuous / load-triggered: the hero role word cycles on a loop roughly every \~2 seconds ("Marketer." → "Health Coach." → "Finance…" → "Chief of…" → "Personal AI."), swapping the green word while "Morning, I'm your" stays fixed. The hero also auto-plays a scripted "assistant working" sequence on load (checking a source, then replying). In the "I handle everything you do" section, a terminal-style skill log scrolls continuously and endlessly (the SKILL·EM01…TR08 lines loop). The egg mascot animates in as two halves that come together.

Scroll-triggered reveals: sections animate in as they enter the viewport. The measured easing is \`transform 0.9s cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.3, 0.64, 1)\` — note the \`1.3\`, which means elements \*\*overshoot slightly and settle back\*\*, i.e. a soft spring rather than a linear slide. Duration \~0.8–0.9s. So motion feels \*slow-ish and floating with a gentle settle\*, not fast and mechanical.

Do elements in a section arrive together or one after another: \*\*one after another\*\* — staggered. I found a \`0.15s\` transition-delay in the reveal chain, so grouped items (cards, table rows, phase blocks) cascade in sequence with roughly a 150ms gap between siblings, top-to-bottom / in reading order. How far and from which direction: they translate a modest distance upward into place (a short rise \+ fade), not a long or sideways travel.

Hover behavior: the primary "GET STARTED" button uses a snappy \`transform 0.12s, box-shadow 0.12s\` — a quick lift/press with a shadow change on hover (fast and tactile, deliberately contrasting the slow section reveals). Cards use \`background 0.3s, transform 0.3s, box-shadow 0.3s\` — a gentler 300ms lift/highlight on hover. Links (e.g. "I want this →") carry an arrow that reads as a directional affordance.

Interactive (click-triggered) motion: the "always on" tabs (In Slack / pocket / meetings), the tool tabs under "I handle everything," and the "Compare with" dropdown all swap content on selection.

Where motion carries meaning rather than decoration: two places. The rotating hero role word \*is\* the value proposition — motion is literally saying "I can be any of these roles for you." And the three-day Stranger→Acquaintance→Colleague reveal, animating in sequence, \*enacts\* the "they grow with you" claim through progression. The overshoot easing also subtly signals "alive/responsive," reinforcing the assistant-with-personality framing.

\#\# On a phone

What stacks and in what order: everything collapses to a single centered column, and the section order is preserved exactly (hero → always-on → I-handle-everything → 10x → compare → plugins → final CTA). The argument does not reorder on mobile.

What changes shape rather than just narrowing: the hero headline rewraps across more lines ("Morning, / I'm your / Personal AI."), the top nav collapses from inline links into a hamburger menu, and multi-column card/tab rows restack into vertical lists. The comparison table narrows but keeps its row structure.

Is motion reduced: no. \`prefers-reduced-motion\` was not matched in the test and the site does not force-disable animation at mobile width — the rotating word and scroll reveals still run on the phone layout. (Worth noting as a possible accessibility consideration: they don't appear to honor a reduced-motion preference, which you may want to do better on your own page.)

\#\# Judgment

The strongest single thing this site does: it \*\*demonstrates the product instead of describing it.\*\* Almost every section is a live or simulated piece of the assistant actually working (triaging 47 emails, rescheduling a meeting, briefing a Series B prospect), so the reader believes the capability before being asked to pay.

The one decision that makes it feel unlike a generic SaaS page: \*\*speaking in the assistant's first person and framing it as a relationship you raise over three days\*\* ("You raise them. They grow with you." / Stranger → Acquaintance → Colleague). Generic SaaS says "our platform enables X." This says "I'll handle it," and gives the product a character. That, plus the rotating role word and the mascot, is the personality no template ships with.

The sentence doing the most work:

\> I learn your voice, your priorities, and your routines. Then I handle the rest.

It compresses the entire promise (it adapts to \*you\*, then removes work) into one line.

The three things to take into your own page, and why:

First, \*\*show the real thing / smallest-first-step before the ask.\*\* They route every CTA into the product and give a genuine free first step. Your analog is leading with the client-owned paid \*discovery\* step as a low-risk entry — let the reader see the actual deliverable logic before the full engagement. This is your strongest structural borrow.

Second, \*\*the named, escalating sequence with a clear peak.\*\* Their Day 1→3 arc gives momentum and a payoff moment. Your five paid steps are already a natural sequence; name each step with an identity and an outcome, escalate them, and let the last one be the visible peak. Take the \*feeling\* of progression, not their content.

Third, \*\*motion that carries meaning: staggered, overshoot-eased reveals and a snappy CTA.\*\* The \~0.9s spring reveal with 0.15s stagger makes the page feel alive and guides the eye one item at a time, while the 0.12s button hover stays tactile. This is a cheap, high-impact way to make forward movement felt — and it directly serves a stepwise argument like yours (each step can animate in as the reader arrives at it).

What I would not copy: \*\*burying the ownership promise and hiding price on the main page.\*\* Their data-ownership line sits deep on the Product page and their prices appear only on a separate Pricing page below a "Start free" fold. For your model that would be self-sabotage: "made in paid discovery belongs to the client from day one" and "prices shown openly" are your two sharpest differentiators, so they belong high and unmissable — the opposite of Vellum's choice to soften and delay both. I'd also skip shipping animation to users who've asked for reduced motion, which this site appears not to respect.
