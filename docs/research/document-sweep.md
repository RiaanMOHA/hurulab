The five documents: `hurulab-meeting-20260812-v1.md` and `-v2.md` (two records of the

## 1. Where the documents agree

Seven agreements, each held by at least three of the five documents.

1. **The client's fear leads, never the firm's credentials.** Both meeting notes say every
   competitor talks about itself, and the hurulab angle is "this is your problem, this is
   about you" (v1) and "address the client's fear first, not the agency's credentials" (v2).
   The positioning document scores four competitor heroes as supply-side for exactly this
   failure. gtos is structured entirely around the client's three fears.

2. **The buyer cannot judge what they are buying, and the answer is to make the work
   judgeable instead of asking for trust.** The vision sentence appears identically in v1 and
   the positioning document: "no business should have to take a technical decision on trust
   alone." v2's headline candidates all restate it. gtos calls it the glass box: "nothing
   asks to be taken on faith." This is the strongest single convergence in the corpus.

3. **Discovery is the single entry point, and the process itself is the product.** v2 says it
   outright. funnel-pricing builds the whole funnel on it and names understanding-their-needs
   as billable expertise. v1's website direction explains the onboarding steps. v2 adds the
   sharper form: hurulab sells a development process, not just software delivery.

4. **Walking away is a respected outcome.** v1: "make the right decision without spending too
   much, even if that decision is to walk away." v2: a high-confidence decision "even if they
   don't continue." funnel-pricing: deliver the report, keep the fee, say so honestly. gtos:
   the advisor flags ideas the data does not support rather than flattering them.

5. **AI is the method, never the product.** v1: "product is expertise and process, not AI as
   a product," and avoid "AI blindness" because every competitor says AI. gtos: "we are not
   selling ai tools. Models are commodities we route between." This matches the settled
   30 July decision in `brand.md` section 6, reached before either document existed.

6. **Every decision comes with something the client can see and a number they recognise.**
   The positioning document makes it one of its two rules. v1 makes cost transparency the
   central differentiator, down to tokens, models and server costs in one statement. gtos
   gives it the machinery: the router log, the receipts, the metered spend with a circuit
   breaker.

7. **The target is established Taiwan businesses with steady revenue and no internal
   technical team.** v1 records this as the agreed target of the 12 August meeting, and it is
   the positioning document's middle ICP almost word for word.

## 2. Where the documents disagree

1. **Two ICPs are still alive in the same meeting.** v1 records the target as agreed
   (established family businesses) and then lists "reconcile ICP into a single agreed
   profile" as an open next step. gtos pulls the other way: it is written for enterprises,
   with CRM, finance functions and closed-source API bills, which is v1's other, competing
   ICP. The specific ICP in the positioning document (owner-led, sells offline, opening a
   digital channel) is a third, narrower cut that nobody has ruled on.

2. **Two different competitors.** v1 records both views without resolving them: competing
   against Taiwan dev shops, or competing against AI token cost opacity. The positioning
   document scores the dev shops; gtos answers the token opacity. Which fight the website
   picks decides what the process section argues.

3. **Three pricing pictures, none reconciled.** funnel-pricing: fixed price and fixed weeks,
   the discovery fee at 5 to 15% of build value, never hourly. v1: show an hourly rate and
   the process on the website, a floor of roughly $3k for a week, flat rate per role,
   case-by-case early on. And `docs/evidence.md` part 6 already disagrees with funnel-pricing
   in five recorded places. The five standing contradictions (discovery pricing, discovery
   length, negotiability, what the client keeps, whether the live demo exists) remain
   unresolved, and v1 adds a third voice rather than settling them. No adjudication is
   currently scheduled: the CEO demo step left the plan on 13 August.

4. **Three different demos.** funnel-pricing S1 is the interview-as-demo with a live build in
   sixty minutes. The 10 August meeting record (evidence part 6) describes two calls and
   never mentions building live. v1 proposes a third thing: the AI cost transparency demo,
   two sliders and a utility tracker, plus an interactive text-box website concept to be
   prototyped. Which demo exists is already contradiction five; the set has grown.

5. **The name in the room was not hurulab.** v1 twice attributes the work to Moreharvest
   ("Moreharvest angle," "vibe coding vs. Moreharvest"). Every other document says hurulab.
   Whether this is a note-taking artifact or something real, only the owner knows; it is
   flagged, not interpreted.

6. **The headline favorite predates the strike.** v2's next step is to test "you're about to
   spend serious money on technology you can't judge" as the favorite headline. On 13 August
   the owner struck the todo's variant of that line from the plan. The documents still carry
   the favorite; the site currently carries no set headline.

## 3. The actual positioning and value proposition, as the documents converge

Found in the documents, not invented. The pieces sit in different files, but they answer each
other and no document contradicts the assembled shape.

- **The vision:** no business should have to take a technical decision on trust alone.
  (v1 and the positioning document, verbatim in both)
- **Who it is for:** established Taiwan businesses with steady revenue and no internal
  technical team, whose next step needs something built and there is nobody to build it.
  (v1's agreed target; the positioning document's middle ICP)
- **The pain that leads:** you are about to spend serious money on something you cannot
  judge, and you fear being burned again. (v2's headline framing; v1's emotional core;
  the fear catalog in gtos)
- **The value proposition:** hurulab makes the decision judgeable instead of asking to be
  trusted. A paid discovery is the entry point and is itself the product: the client leaves
  with usable, measurable evidence, something they can see and a number they recognise, and
  reaches a high-confidence decision even if that decision is to walk away. (v2; the
  positioning document's two rules; funnel-pricing's walk-away honesty)
- **The mechanism, told once, after the promise:** trust, reliability and efficiency
  manufactured by the cycle, with receipts; AI as the method, never the product. (gtos; v1)

**How this sits against the settled single idea.** `brand.md` section 7 says: companies waste
capital because they buy software they have never used, so you use it before you pay for it.
The converged positioning keeps the same buyer and the same fear but moves the promise from
*use it first* to *judge it first*: evidence and a number, rather than hands on the artifact,
become the thing the client gets before committing. That is a real shift in emphasis, it is
consistent across the new documents, and it is not yet a decision. Whether the single idea in
`brand.md` gets amended is the owner's call, through the evidence rule, not this file's.
