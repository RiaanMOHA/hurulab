I studied the AJ\&Smart Partners page (ajsmart.com/partners) as it argues, not as it looks. Here is the report.

\#\# Business model

What is sold is described in the "WHAT WE DO" section:

\> Becoming one of our Partners is kind of like having temporary marketing and growth-focused cofounders, without needing to give away equity or deal with the complexity of hiring C-level team members.

\> And what does that actually mean? Very practically speaking: we work directly with our Partners through strategy sessions, calls, workshops, text messages, and regular catch-ups (in-person or remote) to help them build funnels into their business to make more money in a more predictable and enjoyable way.

The period varies by offer: the top engagement runs "12-18 months" and the mid engagement is "2 full days (and maybe nights if needed\!)". What the seller is on the hook for is stated as a threshold of impact rather than a guarantee: for the 2-day option, "We will only offer you this option if we think it will have more than a €200,000 impact on your business," and for the Partnership, "only offer it if we believe we can make the company at least €1,000,000 (profit or revenue) more than the previous year."

How they get paid: fixed fees are shown for two tiers (a "From €100,000" floor and a hidden number behind "Book a Call"), with the top tier explicitly not equity — "without needing to give away equity." There is no retainer, revenue share, or equity language. On carrying risk themselves: the page frames selectivity as their filter rather than a money-back promise; they do not state they refund or work for free if targets miss. The risk they name is reputational/selection ("we will only offer you this option if…").

The smallest first step is a discovery call, repeated as the entry point:

\> Book a discovery call with our team and let's chat\!

There is no described paid discovery deliverable, no pricing on that first step, and nothing about it crediting toward what follows — the smallest paid step shown is the Group Mastermind (price not present) or the 2-Day Growth Jam. Anything paid before the main engagement: not present as a described, priced, credited discovery product.

\#\# Judgment

The strongest single thing this page does is refuse to sell hours. It reframes an expensive engagement as buying access and outcomes, and then makes the price feel like a fraction of a promised return before the number ever appears — so €100,000 lands as "cheap relative to €200,000+ of impact," and the seven-figure tier lands as a privilege you must qualify for.

The one sentence doing the most work:

\> You're not buying our hours, you're buying access to experience, advisory, know-how, and insights based on a proven track-record.

What to steal for an expensive, staged engagement with prices shown openly: (1) put proof high and price last, and earn the number with track record and a reframe before revealing it; (2) anchor each price against a stated, larger return in the same breath ("we only offer this if we think it'll have \>€200,000 impact"); (3) use selection as scarcity ("only 3 companies per year") so the buyer feels chosen rather than sold to; (4) show real, named clients with concrete figures; (5) name the categories you are not ("Not an Agency. Not Investors.") to define yourself by contrast without attacking anyone.

What I would not copy: the prices are only half-open — one tier is a real number ("From €100,000") but the other two hide behind "Learn more" and "Book a Call," which contradicts a genuinely open-pricing goal; if your promise is that prices are shown openly, show all three. I'd also drop the reliance on unverifiable dollar claims inside testimonials as the load-bearing proof, and I'd add a real "who this is not for / what we don't do" admission, which this page lacks. The offers are also presented as parallel choices with no described path from the cheap entry to the expensive tier — for a staged engagement you'd want that progression spelled out.

What it gets away with because of who they are: AJ\&Smart is a well-known brand with a genuinely large audience and famous clients (the Sprint / Make Time authors), so they can post a €100k floor with almost no deliverable detail, hide the top price entirely, and gate access to "3 companies per year" — and have it read as confidence rather than evasion. A less-known company doing the same (vague scope, hidden numbers, hard scarcity) would read as overpriced and cagey; they'd need far more concrete deliverables, guarantees, and third-party proof to earn the same benefit of the doubt.
