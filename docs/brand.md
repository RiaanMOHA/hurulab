## 3. What hurulab is

**A consultancy, and more than that.** Owner decision 30 July 2026, reversing the 8 July choice
of "agency", restated 7 September: consultancy is right as far as it goes and does not cover
everything hurulab does. **The owner is supplying the fuller definition next session.** Until
then the word is consultancy and no page stretches it further.

The interviews disagreed, and the disagreement is recorded here rather than smoothed over,
because it says something the decision does not.

| Person | Their word for it |
|---|---|
| Riaan | "A new kind of agency combining AI, tech and design" |
| Henry | "A design and AI agency" |
| BY | "A design and AI agency" |
| Cal | "A consultancy partner" and separately, "I don't like the word agency, it feels too mercenary" |
| Pedro | "A technology partner" |

Source: Q2 and Q5, all five.

Three said agency, one said consultancy, one said partner. Worth noting that the one dissenter
gave a reason: Cal said "agency" feels too mercenary. That reason lines up with section 5,
where the buyer is a company already burned by an agency. Naming yourself the category your
buyer distrusts is the argument for the decision, not against it.

**Where the five actually agree**, and this is stronger than the label:

> Business, design and technology in one team that understands the business before it builds.

Riaan: "Business, design and tech in one team that understands the business and builds it
fast." Henry: "Business, design and technology in one team, and a proven method, not one off
experiments." Pedro: "Design and AI in one team. Most shops do one or the other, hurulab does
both deeply in the same small team." BY: "Strong design and real AI skill in the same team."
Cal: "Product management, engineering and design in one team." (Q2 and Q13, all five)

That is the only claim all five make unprompted, in their own words, to two different
questions. It is the foundation.

## 6. Where AI sits

Answered directly, and the answer is three to two.

**AI is a tool, not the product.** "A tool we use." (Q16, Cal) "AI is a tool behind the
scenes." (Q16, Henry) "A tool behind the scenes. AI makes the team faster and better, but
clients buy outcomes, not AI." (Q16, Pedro)

**AI skill is the differentiator.** "We actually know how to use AI, how to train people to
use it, and how to build with it, and we use design taste so it doesn't look like AI slop."
(Q16, Riaan) "We build software with AI and design the workflow, deciding for each part
whether AI or ordinary code is the right tool." (Q16, BY)

**Settled, 30 July 2026. AI does not lead. It explains.**

AI is never the promise. It is the answer to the one question the promise provokes: how can you
possibly build it before I pay for it. So it appears once, as mechanism, after the work and the
proof, and never in the hero.

Three reasons this is the decision and not a compromise. The single idea in section 7 mentions
capital and using the thing, and does not mention AI. The buyer in section 5 is already
"drowning in tools, no clear results", so leading with AI adds to what they are drowning in.
And "AI-first" in Taiwan is already occupied by a competitor running a content operation
nobody here can outspend, so the ground is taken (evidence, part 4).

Both interview positions survive this. AI is not what is sold, and competence with AI is what
makes the speed real. Pedro put it plainest, and it came from the developer: "clients buy
outcomes, not AI".

## 7. The single idea

Question 4 asked what hurulab stands for if it stands for one thing, and got five answers.

- Riaan: "Once we are done we become useless, because we have given you everything to continue
  on your own, not just the product but the tools and training to keep doing it."
- Cal: "Outcomes over process."
- Henry: "A highly efficient method that helps companies get more out of their capital, their
  money, time and labour."
- BY: "Technology made human."
- Pedro: "Craft."

**Settled, 7 September 2026, in the owner's own words.** This is the **subtitle** on the live
landing page, and it is the single idea. A headline sits above it and a full page around it,
both held by the owner and not recorded here yet:

> **At hurulab, we know AI. Let us sit with you for 1-2 weeks to see how you work. We'll help
> you understand how AI can relieve your biggest headaches. If you like what we find, we'll
> build it. And if you don't need AI, we'll tell you that, too.**

**What it commits hurulab to, and each part is a decision:**

- **One to two weeks, sitting with the client**, watching how they actually work. Not a call,
  not a workshop. This is the discovery engagement.
- **The output is understanding, not software.** What the client gets first is knowing where AI
  would relieve a real headache.
- **The build is conditional and comes second.** "If you like what we find."
- **The honest stop is part of the offer, stated out loud.** "If you don't need AI, we'll tell
  you that, too." This is the one thing in the corpus no competitor claims.

**On "we know AI" opening it.** [Section 6](#6-where-ai-sits) says AI does not lead and never
appears in the hero. **Owner ruling, 7 September 2026: this line stands and section 6 stands.**
"We know AI" is a statement of competence, not an AI-led promise; the promise is the two weeks
and the honest stop. The distinction is the rule: hurulab may say it knows AI, and may not sell
AI as the outcome.

## 11. What cannot be claimed

Each of these is a limit the record itself sets.

- **No measured client outcome.** No analytics on any client site, and the case studies say so
  in their own gap sections.
- **No cold win.** "So far it's been leveraging existing connections through Henry." (Q12, Cal)
  Every engagement came through someone already known.
- **No funding result from the pitch.** "No external investor has seen the finished deck. It
  has been shown internally only." (evidence, value-add prototype)
- **No client has run alone.** The "we make ourselves useless" idea has no instance behind it.
- **No client quote on record.** jadegia approved the flagship direction and nobody wrote down
  why.
- **The 2.5 year platform figure is soft.** It is a midpoint between two numbers given in the
  same session. Ed ran that project and should pull the real number from release history
  before it is said out loud.
- **No invented case studies.** The 29 July page carried three written to be plausible. Real
  ones exist. Use those.

**The rules those limits set.** The list above is what is true. This is what it forbids on a
page. These are constraints, not preferences.

- **Do not claim a measured outcome.** Naming a measure is allowed. Claiming a result is not.
- **Do not publish an invented case study.** Use the real ones.
- **Do not show a placeholder client logo.** A row reading "client one, client two" advertises
  that the answer is empty. Say the names are not yet ours to publish, or say nothing.
- **Do not claim a cold win.** There has not been one.
- **"We make ourselves useless" is forbidden.** Owner ruling, reconfirmed 7 September 2026. Not
  as a claim, not as an intention, not in any rewording. See section 7.
- **There is no free live demo, and it is never offered.** Owner ruling, 5 August 2026,
  restated 7 September: building the client's problem live and free, before any payment, is
  not what hurulab does. It does not appear on a page, in an email or in a proposal. The
  sources disagree about whether such a step ever existed, which was contradiction five in `docs/evidence.md` part 11; **the owner's ruling settles what may be offered regardless.**

## 15a. Meaning accrues, it is not designed in

Extracted 7 September 2026 from the logo research before it was deleted, and kept because it
is a brand principle rather than a logo one.

**Paul Rand:** a logo takes its meaning from the quality of the thing it represents, not the
other way around. Literal subject matter barely matters, and Mercedes is not automotive.
**Ivan Chermayeff:** it must work without explanation. The Chase octagon and the National
Geographic rectangle carried no literal meaning and became iconic through decades of
consistent use.

**The consequence for hurulab:** meaning belongs in the brief, where it steers what is
appropriate and specific. The viewer is never asked to decode it. A name, a mark or a line
that has to be explained to work is not yet doing its job, and no amount of explanation on the
page fixes it.
