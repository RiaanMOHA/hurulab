# hurulab evidence

The one place the primary evidence lives. Everything in `docs/design-rules/brand.md` cites
this file.

Assembled 30 July 2026, replacing a 74-file `research/` folder that was a verbatim copy of
`/Users/riaan/Desktop/hurulab/`. That original is untouched and is still the archive of record.
This file carries what survived the evidence rule in `CLAUDE.md`.

**Three parts, in strength order.**

1. **The interviews.** Five people, thirty questions, verbatim. Nothing condensed. This is the
   strongest source in the project and it is reproduced whole, on purpose.
2. **Knowns and bets.** The owner's own split between what is proven and what is still a bet,
   verbatim from a Miro board export.
3. **Project facts.** Condensed from thirteen case studies. Only claims citing a file, a commit
   count or a date survived. Every story passage was dropped.

**What was deliberately left out.** The market and competitor research (five documents, none of
which cited an interview) is research about the Taiwan market, not about hurulab. It is not
evidence for anything on the site and is not reproduced here. It remains on the Desktop.

# Part 2. Knowns and bets

Verbatim from the owner's Miro board export. The board itself:
https://miro.com/app/board/uXjVH7rsHaY=/

## Proven

The owner's words, unedited.

- Our own experience hiring consultancies was poor.
- Jadegia came to us because the alternatives were over-sized with long delivery tails.
- Inkslap came to us because their previous partners were led by weak process that produced lacklustre delivery.
- Jadegia's business goal is stated as growing 20% of their revenue from their online storefront.
- Our rapid-prototype approach wowed both Jadegia and Inkslap: a workable rebuild in days, not months.
- With Jadegia, Dada and Jiuyang, reaching an international audience is a real, stated client need: Jadegia's overseas storefront, Dada's Chinese-only site opened up to global investors, and Jiuyang's push into Japan. Internationalisation is a lever, not the lead; the measurable result is still to be proven.
- Referrals already compound: one existing relationship brought two paid projects at once (Dada and Jiuyang), the exact flywheel our growth engine depends on.

## Still a bet

What needs testing. Also the owner's words.

- **No cold win:** all four clients (Jadegia, Inkslap, Dada, Jiuyang) came through existing, Taiwan or Taiwanese-connected relationships, not this positioning. Cold willingness to pay, and any win outside that network, is the first thing we need to test.
- **Build-depth proof is thin:** Jadegia is design heavy with some custom development. Complex-system track record has been internal only with no outcomes.
- **We can't yet measure the outcomes,** neither revenue nor audience. We will need Jadegia to disclose their business results and analytics.

# Part 3. Project facts

Condensed from thirteen case studies. A claim survived only if it cited a file, a commit count
or a date. Every "Pixar seven-step" story passage was dropped. Those passages describe a
composite, invented client and one of them was misread as history on 30 July 2026, which is

## The clients

Four. All named by the owner in the Miro board export.

| Client | On record |
|---|---|
| jadegia | A jade house in Taipei 101, founded 1932. Came because the alternatives were oversized with long delivery tails. Stated business goal: growing 20 percent of revenue through the online storefront |
| Inkslap | Came because their previous partners were led by weak process that produced lacklustre delivery |
| Dada | A family firm founded in Tokyo in 2018 by Taiwanese investors. Invests its own capital and also brokers. Needs a Chinese-only site opened to global investors |
| Jiuyang | Pushing into Japan |

MoreHarvest is a separate company and a client. The value-add prototype, the map, the 3D house
and the product hub were built for it, and jadegia arrived through that relationship.

## jadegia storefront

- Kickoff 28 May 2026. Two complete brand directions, warm flagship and cold luxury, designed
  and presented as live testable Shopify theme variants twelve days later on 9 June.
- At that demo the client chose a hybrid themselves: flagship branding and colors with the
  luxury layout and navigation.
- A behavioral floor defining how buttons, spacing, focus and motion behave on every device was
  agreed before a single screen was drawn.
- Jade at or above NT$200,000 shows "available by appointment" and cannot be added to the bag,
  so the commerce model cannot be misconfigured.
- Layout specs carry no color, font or copy, so nothing gets invented downstream.
- A finished page-transition effect was deleted because the storefront read cleaner without it.
- A prior agency delivered a visual style deck in February 2026 and was replaced. Their name and
  the reason are not documented.

## Ozu-1, the 3D house

- 76 commits across 8 merged pull requests, 22 April to 22 May 2026. Verified against `git log`.
- Rebuilt from a blueprint at 400 dpi and 933 interior photographs, one room at a time.
- A source-of-truth hierarchy was set once and cited by every later room build: blueprint beats
  photos, beats room maps, beats code comments, beats memory. Nothing is eyeballed.

## haramizu landing page

- 121 commits across 25 pull requests over about 20 days, one feature per pull request.
- Live 26 March 2026. Static HTML the client maintains in-house, no vendor lock-in.
- A third of the page was cut, the splash screen removed once it stopped earning its place, and
  the timeline connector rebuilt eight times.

## kozue fund site

- Nine days, 30 March to 7 April 2026. 75 commits, 27 pull requests, all merged.
- Two older source files say 76 commits. 75 is the verified git figure.
- The brief "make a nicer deck" was reframed into "an investor must reach the IRR table without
  passing the pitch", and that reframe set the whole panel structure.

## The value-add prototype

- 28 production step components, grown from 20 as the story architecture was added.
- Roughly 360 content strings in one locked source of truth, with all 8 source conflicts decided
  by a human, feeding both the iPad app and the downloadable PDF so they cannot drift apart.
- Apple's own interface guidance was overruled on three named points with the reason recorded
  each time: no frosted glass, REM and Noto Sans JP instead of San Francisco, and #F9F9F9
  instead of true black.
- Frosted glass was killed across all 20 steps and the depth rebuilt from a 1px border and a
  shadow.
- The working rule, in writing: "do not round rgba(255,255,255,0.72) to 0.7. The prototype is
  the spec."

## gktk

- The team refused the deck format itself and built a briefing instrument instead. One rule set
  at the start: this is a private briefing, not a sales deck.
- One step went through ten rejected versions before the rule that stuck was written down: the
  animation is the content.

## The record itself

- 74 handoff journals carrying context between sessions.
- Five AI audits came back wrong and were sent back until every claim was observed rather than
  assumed.
- A hard rule that nothing is added beyond what the spec or the client asked for.

## Limits the case studies set on themselves

Each of these is written into the source files by their own authors.

- **No external investor has seen the finished value-add deck.** Shown internally only. No
  funding win to claim.
- **No citable investor moment on gktk.** The "shown to investors, money moved" outcome rests
  only on an interview. No recorded quote, named investor, date, venue or figure.
- **No recorded investor reaction on kozue.** Not in any file, not confirmed in interview.
- **No hard figure on cost or build hours anywhere.** The "months and six figures the old way"
  counterfactual is an honest estimate, not an invoice.
- **The 2.5 year platform rebuild figure is soft.** A midpoint between two numbers given in one
  session. Ed ran that project and can pull the real one from release history.
- **No analytics on any client site.** No outcome has ever been measured.

## One process lesson worth keeping

The first Dada brief described them as a company that "sells Tokyo property", taken from a Slack
summary. Research proved otherwise. The note left in the handoff of 16 July 2026: do not
describe a client from a Slack summary when the evidence is one search away.

# Part 4. The two market facts the brand rests on

The competitor and market research is otherwise excluded from this file, because it is research
about the Taiwan market rather than about hurulab and none of it cites an interview. Two
findings are the exception, because decisions in `docs/design-rules/brand.md` rest on them
directly. Both are reproduced with their own caveats intact.

**1. The demo-first motion is unoccupied, and searching for it turns up scam warnings.**

> "Nobody in Taiwan, Hong Kong, or Singapore sells the motion Hurulab is built on. No agency
> found in any tier lets the client use their own working product before committing. Confirmed
> by direct search in both languages, on 先試用再付款, 滿意再付款, and equivalents, which
> surfaced only SaaS free trials and scam warnings. The caveat that matters: this market has
> learned that 'free first' means scam. 免費的最貴 is a standing warning in local buyer guides.
> So the teaser demo must be framed as invitation-only work on the prospect's real brand, never
> as a free website offer."

The second half of that finding is a constraint on the lead promise, not a footnote to it. "Use
it before you buy it" and "free website" must never be readable as the same offer.

**2. "AI-first" in Taiwan is already occupied.**

Tenten.co, Taipei, founded 2010, self-describes as an AI-first agency, is fully bilingual, and
runs the largest content operation in the market: a developer blog, an SEO blog, a Webflow
community forum, newsletters, and a claimed 300,000-follower social audience. That follower
claim is recorded in the source as unverified. Their client logos include ASUS and FarEasTone.

The research's own conclusion: do not fight Tenten on content volume or logo walls. Fight on
the demo-first motion, which they do not sell.

**Everything else about the market stays out of this project.** Five documents on the Desktop
cover competitors, market sizing, go-to-market, launch sequencing and international benchmarks.
None of them cites an interview. Do not lift claims about hurulab from any of them.
