# Copy archive

**What this is.** Client-facing copy that was written, put on a page, and in most cases judged.
Kept because the wording exists nowhere else: `docs/evidence.md` and `docs/pricing.md` carry
the mechanics of the offer, not the sentences a client would read.

**Dated, and history, not truth.** Every entry carries the date it was written and the verdict
if one was given. Nothing here is approved copy. Where a line here and a rule in
`docs/design-rules/` disagree, the rule wins, and several lines below are known to break
current rules; each is marked.

**The evidence rule still governs.** A line surviving here does not make its claim true. Before
reusing any of it, check the claim against `docs/evidence.md`.

## 3. The cost argument, 12 August 2026

From `proposal-c-the-money.html`, on why a sprint costs less than not having one.

> **Do it twice, or do it once.**
>
> Build it, then fix it. Two efforts, and the second one is the expensive one.
>
> One sprint, then build it. You know what to build before anyone builds it.

> The second effort is the expensive one. The sprint exists so there is no second effort.

On the honest cost of building it yourself:

> The cost does not sit still. It is spend you cannot budget for a year ahead.
>
> Fixing takes longer than building. Most of the time goes after the first version works.
>
> Customer data raises the floor. If it holds payments or personal details, the standard is not
> negotiable.
>
> A simple idea may not need us at all. We will say so.

The four entry problems, which is how the page opened:

> **Operations.** The work itself takes longer and costs more than it should.
> **Sales and marketing.** You cannot see which effort brings the money in.
> **Data and reporting.** The numbers exist, and nobody trusts them.
> **Customer experience.** You lose people at a point you cannot name.

## 5. The three fears, 12 August 2026

From `proposal-d-the-moment.html`, which put the CEO's own document into a reader's words. The
mechanics are `docs/pricing.md` part 2; these are the sentences.

> **These tools do not understand how my business actually runs.** You have seen the demos.
> None of them knew the operation.
>
> **I could burn a year's AI budget in a month, and I cannot see where it went.** An open tab at
> someone else's bar.
>
> **Tokens keep getting cheaper. Should I just wait?** Waiting looks free, so waiting looks
> sensible.

The answer to the third, which is the strongest argument in the CEO's document:

> Tokens will get cheaper. That is why you should not wait. When electricity got cheap, the
> winners were not the ones who waited. They were the ones who already had the wiring.

> Nobody wins by owning electricity. The value is in three other things: the wiring, the meter,
> and the discipline.

On trust:

> **A glass box, not a black box.** Every claim clicks back to its source. Every prediction is
> frozen before we act, and graded after. The system cannot lie about whether it was right.

> **Facts live in a ledger, never in a model.** Anything with one correct answer goes to code.
> Accounts do not hallucinate, because accounts never pass through a model.

The moat question, which asks the reader something rather than telling them:

> A competitor copies everything you do tomorrow. **What do they still not have?**
>
> A reputation · A way of working · The relationships · Honestly, not much
>
> Whatever it is, it probably is not written down. Written down, it outlives the person holding
> it. That is the work.

**No answer reads as a rejection.** "Honestly, not much, today you mostly compete on price"
routes to finding the edge, never to a decline. Owner ruling, 12 August 2026.
