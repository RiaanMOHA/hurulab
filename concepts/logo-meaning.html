Five meanings for the mark.

Each proposal starts from something the interviews or the evidence actually say, and draws that one idea as its own mark, each with its own shape. The typeface asterisk stands until you choose. Sources read as in the brand foundation: (Q7, Riaan) is question 7, answered by Riaan.

1Three lines, one point

The asterisk is not six arms. It is three whole lines crossing at a single point: business, design and technology in one team. The mark we already have gains its meaning without changing shape, because an asterisk has always been three strokes.

Source: the one claim all five people make unprompted, to two different questions: "business, design and technology in one team that understands the business before it builds." (Q2 and Q13, all five)

hurulab

2The footnote

In writing, an asterisk marks a claim that can be checked: it points at the source. hurulab as the asterisk on its industry's promises, for a buyer who has been sold hype before. The mark says: this claim carries its evidence.

Source: "they fear being fooled, sold hype and made to waste money again." (Q11, BY) And the design consequence of the single idea: "sober and demonstrative rather than persuasive, numbers forward." (brand.md section 7)

hurulab

3Use it first

Two of the same mark: the light one is the working thing you use before you pay, the solid one is the thing you then own. The demo comes first and the purchase follows it, never the other way around.

Source: the single idea, settled 30 July 2026: "companies waste capital because they buy software they have never used. So you use it before you pay for it." (brand.md section 7, joining Q6, Henry to Riaan's lead promise)

hurulab

4The handover

One arm of the mark leaves it and becomes an arrow: everything made is handed on. The work, the tools and the training go out the door with the client. This is a stated intention, not yet an observed result, and the mark wears it as a promise.

Source: "once we are done we become useless, because we have given you everything to continue on your own, not just the product but the tools and training to keep doing it." (Q4, Riaan) Publishable as intention only. (brand.md sections 7 and 11)

hurulab

5The business at the center

A point first, then everything radiating from it. The client's business sits at the center of the mark, and nothing is built that does not grow out of it: the opposite of taking the order and building.

Source: "why so much capital is wasted, why vendors just take the order without understanding the business." (Q6, Riaan) "They just take the order and build." (Q6, Cal) And the shared claim: "one team that understands the business before it builds." (Q2 and Q13, all five)
