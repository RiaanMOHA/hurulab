# Hurulab go-to-market strategy

*Document 3 of 3. Written July 3, 2026. Builds on the positioning (document 1) and the market research (document 2).*

## The core sales motion: demo first

The whole brand says "use the thing, not pictures of the thing," so the sales process must be the first proof of it.

**The teaser demo.** For a named, qualified prospect, spend one to two days building a live, working redesign of one piece of their actual business: their homepage, their best product's page, or a tappable version of their pitch. Send it as a single link with one sentence: "we made this for you; open it on your phone." No deck, no proposal, no meeting request.

Why this works for this buyer specifically:

- It is proof in the only form the research says this buyer trusts: something tangible they can touch, before any relationship of obligation exists.
- It gives face instead of taking it. It never says "your site is bad"; it shows what their business deserves and lets the gap speak silently.
- It is nearly free for Hurulab and impossible for competitors to match, because their economics require billing a team from day one. The teaser demo is the moat performing in public.
- It filters. An owner who does not respond to their own business made beautiful was never going to buy.

Rules: never more than two days on a teaser; always the prospect's real brand and real content (extracted the same way Jadegia's was); always mobile-first because this buyer will open it on a phone; and always end the demo with one tappable moment of surprise, per Gallo, so it gets shown to someone else.

Precedent: this is a pilot-first motion, the services equivalent of product-led growth, and it mirrors what already worked internally: the Moha hub and the 3D walkthrough were both unrequested demos that converted leadership on sight.

## The referral engine: how trust actually travels here

The research is unambiguous: this market buys through introductions, not outreach. Build the engine deliberately.

**1. Lighthouse clients.** Jadegia is the first. The playbook per lighthouse: capture launch numbers and one recorded owner sentence, produce the case story in the Pixar structure, then ask for exactly two introductions to peer owners. Two is a small enough ask to honor and large enough to compound: 1 client, then 3, then 9 within roughly 18 months if the pattern holds.

**2. Named network entry points in Taiwan.**

- 二代大學 (NGC, the second-generation university) under the national SME association (全國中小企業總會): the flagship platform for family business successors, with 150+ successor alumni and a mentor bench of listed-company founders. This is a room full of exactly the buyer trigger (succession) described in the research. Sources: https://www.ngc.org.tw/ and https://www.facebook.com/ngc.org.tw/
- 台灣數位企業總會 (TDEA, Taiwan digital enterprise association): grew out of the二代大學 lineage, focused on succession plus digital transformation, runs successor communities on the theme of innovation, upgrade, transformation, succession. Source: https://sunrisemedium.com/p/391/tdea
- N世代學苑 (NexTech academy) under the MOEA startup portal: runs successor courses on exactly the founder-successor communication problem. A guest talk on "modernizing without confronting the founder" fits their curriculum precisely. Source: https://startup.sme.gov.tw/ntacademy/
- General chambers (工商協進會, regional chambers, industry guilds for jewelry, tea, and food): secondary, slower, still worth one relationship each.

The entry offer to these networks is never a sales pitch. It is a talk plus a live demo: "how a 94-year-old jade house chose its new website by using it," delivered in the storytelling format the positioning document defines. Successors bring their parents' problems to speakers they trust.

**3. The cross-border referral lane.** The Taiwanese real estate firm already reusing GKTK material, and the MoreHarvest investor network, are warm channels into exactly the capital-adjacent businesses (property, investment, private banking-adjacent services) where interactive pitches sell.

## Segment priorities: hunt by symptom, in this order

The symptom is always the same: an excellent business wearing an embarrassing digital face. Hunt it in these verticals, in order:

1. **Heritage luxury retail (jewelry, jade, watches, antiques, tea, tailoring).** The Jadegia story transfers with zero translation. Taipei 101, Zhongshan, and Dihua street addresses with decade-old websites can be qualified from a desk.
2. **Boutique property and investment firms pitching cross-border capital.** Four of five existing case studies live here; the interactive pitch and 3D walkthrough are unique inventory no agency quotes.
3. **Premium food, beverage, and hospitality with succession under way.** High face-value businesses, strong successor presence in the named networks.
4. **Family manufacturers selling B2B abroad.** Their websites are their factory tour now; the market research shows B2B site budgets of NT$150,000+ already normal here. Enter via successor networks, not cold.
5. **Clinics and professional practices.** Later; regulated, slower, but wealthy and image-sensitive.

## Subsidies as a door, not as revenue

Use government money to lower the perceived risk of the first yes, never to define the project:

- Taiwan: TCloud points (NT$30,000, fast approval) and the under-30-employee subsidy (up to NT$100,000) can fully or largely cover a small first engagement (a product page system, a pitch experience), making the demo-to-first-project step feel nearly free. Offer to handle the paperwork; the research shows this buyer values a guide through bureaucracy.
- Position against the documented 60 percent subsidy-failure rate directly: "most subsidized digital purchases changed nothing, because nobody with judgment supervised them; that supervision is what we are."
- Japan (ring 3): the IT introduction subsidy can cut client cost 50 to 75 percent, but requires vendor registration in Japanese; plan a local partner before actively selling there.

## Proof strategy: close the one gap everything depends on

Every case study currently ends before the applause. Fix this as a standing process, not a one-off:

1. **At Jadegia launch:** capture baseline and post-launch traffic, first online sale or appointment booked, and one recorded sentence from the owner. This becomes the new step 7 of the flagship story.
2. **Get the GKTK reuse on record:** one sentence from the Taiwanese real estate firm about why they keep using the material.
3. **Instrument every future project from day one:** agree at kickoff which two numbers will be captured at launch plus 90 days, and that a quote may be requested. Make it a contract clause, framed as "we document results the way we document decisions."
4. **Publish the method:** the written handoffs, the locked source-of-truth practice, and the AI-caught-guessing stories are publishable assets that competitors cannot fake quickly. One method article per month on the Hurulab site, each built in the Pixar structure, each ending with a live demo link.

## Pricing guidance

- **Anchor high, justify with certainty.** Stay at the top of the market (the Jadegia NT$900,000 band for full builds) and never discount on price; concede scope instead. The comparison sentence: "full-custom agencies charge this much for mockups in month two; you will be using your real store in week two."
- **Three-step ladder to lower the first-yes barrier:**
  1. Teaser demo: free, by invitation only, two days max.
  2. First working piece: NT$80,000 to 250,000 (subsidy-eligible band), two to three weeks, a complete usable thing, not a phase of a bigger thing.
  3. Full build: NT$600,000 to 1,500,000 depending on scope, always framed against the agency alternative's timeline and mockup process rather than its price.
- **Interactive pitches and 3D walkthroughs are premium inventory with no market comparison.** Price them by value against the deal they support (a 2 billion yen raise justifies far more than a website budget), floor NT$400,000.
- Review pricing every six months against the AI-native studio wave; the 2 to 4 year differentiation window in the market research means today's premium needs continuous proof to hold.

## The 12-month sequenced plan

**Months 1 to 3: proof and platform.**
- Ship the Hurulab site as a demonstration of the method (story-driven, tappable, fast).
- Capture Jadegia launch results and the GKTK reuse quote; publish both case stories.
- Build the qualified target list: 50 heritage businesses matching the symptom, desk-audited.

**Months 3 to 6: the demo engine.**
- Send two teaser demos per month to the best targets; goal, one new signed client per quarter minimum.
- Deliver one talk at 二代大學, TDEA, or N世代學苑, with a live demo as the centerpiece.
- Stand up the subsidy-handling offer as a documented service step.

**Months 6 to 9: compound.**
- Ask each satisfied client for two introductions; begin the referral chain deliberately.
- Second and third network talks; first published method articles collecting inbound.
- Instrumented results now exist from at least two projects; refresh both story documents with real step-7 endings.

**Months 9 to 12: ring 2 toes in the water.**
- Take the strongest case story to Hong Kong and Singapore through existing personal and client networks; one exploratory trip per market, referral-fed only.
- Assess Japan formally: subsidy vendor registration path, one Kumamoto-adjacent pilot beyond MoreHarvest.
- Year-end review: pipeline by source (referral, network talk, teaser demo, inbound), decide the year 2 weighting between deepening Taiwan and opening ring 2.

**Benchmarks to hold ourselves to:**
- month 6: at least 2 external (non-MoreHarvest) clients signed.
- month 9: at least 1 published case with real outcome numbers and a client quote.
- month 12: at least half of new pipeline arriving by referral, the signal that the trust engine has started running on its own.
- If any benchmark misses by more than a quarter, the correction is always the same: narrower targets, more teaser demos, not broader marketing.
