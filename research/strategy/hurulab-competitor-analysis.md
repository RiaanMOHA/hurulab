# Hurulab competitor analysis and positioning

*Document 4 of 4. Researched July 5, 2026. Builds on the positioning (document 1), market research (document 2), and go-to-market strategy (document 3). Five parallel research passes across roughly 150 sources, in traditional Chinese and English, with an adversarial verification pass on document 2's claims. Sources cited inline. Anything unverifiable is marked as such.*

## How to read this document

Part 1 profiles the named competitors, four categories, with a mini SWOT each. Part 2 reports how these firms promote themselves and win clients. Part 3 corrects document 2 where new verification contradicts it. Part 4 is the master SWOT for Hurulab. Part 5 is the positioning recommendation and the refined value proposition.

The single most important finding, stated up front: nobody in Taiwan, Hong Kong, or Singapore sells the motion Hurulab is built on. No agency found in any tier lets the client use their own working product before committing. The whitespace claimed in documents 1 to 3 is confirmed by direct search in both languages (searches on 先試用再付款, 滿意再付款, and equivalents surfaced only SaaS free trials and scam warnings). The caveat that matters: this market has learned that "free first" means scam (免費的最貴 is a standing warning in local buyer guides, https://www.awakening-design.com.tw/blog_detail/how-to-choose-quality-web-design/), so the teaser demo must be framed as invitation-only work on the prospect's real brand, never as a free website offer.

## Part 1: the competitive landscape

### Category A: integrated full-service agencies (design + engineering + consulting)

These are the firms a prospect would shortlist alongside Hurulab for a serious build.

**Tenten.co, Taipei. The most direct threat in Taiwan.**
Founded 2010, offices in Taipei, Manila, and the US, 300+ brands claimed (https://tenten.co/). Self-describes as an "AI-first agency": Webflow, Shopify Plus, headless CMS, design sprints, AI consulting, and generative engine optimization. Fully bilingual. The only agency in the study with public pricing: standard projects US$30,000 to 100,000, Webflow builds US$20,000 to 50,000, enterprise e-commerce US$50,000 to 100,000+ (https://tenten.co/pricing). Client logos include ASUS, ASE Holdings, MOXA, and FarEasTone. Runs the largest content operation in the market: a developer blog with vibe-coding essays, an SEO blog, a Webflow community forum, newsletters, and a claimed 300,000-follower social audience (claim unverified).
Mini SWOT. Strength: AI-first brand plus bilingual reach plus content volume nobody matches. Weakness: platform-dependent delivery (Webflow, Shopify templates as base), big-brand focus leaves the family SME under-served, portfolio case studies carry no outcome numbers. Opportunity for them: moving down-market with productized AI offers. Threat to them: their speed claims are tool-based, not method-based, so they are copyable. What Hurulab takes from this: do not fight Tenten on content volume or logo walls; fight on the demo-first motion and governance, which Tenten does not sell.

**Block studio 版塊設計, Taipei. The prestige benchmark.**
Founded 2015 by 李明, about 20 people, 400+ projects (https://blockstudio.tw/about/). Motion-driven brand websites for the Presidential Office, Kaohsiung Music Center, KEBUKE, and TDRI. Five Awwwards honorable mentions, seven Golden Pin marks. Chinese-first. No published pricing. The strongest demand signal in the market: their intake form states the earliest project slot is October 2026, a 15-month backlog (observed at https://blockstudio.tw/project/tsbb-officialwebsite).
Mini SWOT. Strength: award prestige, press, and a backlog that proves referral demand exceeds supply at the craft tier. Weakness: 15-month wait, design-object deliverables rather than business outcomes, no metrics anywhere. Opportunity for them: raising prices indefinitely. Threat to them: clients who need working software this quarter cannot wait. What Hurulab takes from this: Block studio's backlog is Hurulab's opening sentence to an impatient buyer, and their award-plus-community playbook (they founded the WebCRACK web trends group) is worth copying at small scale.

**AJA Creative 大予創意, Taipei.**
Founded 2009, self-described first and largest UX consultancy in Taiwan, 100+ clients, 300+ projects across finance, telecom, aviation, and medical (https://www.aja-creative.com/aja_before/zh_tw/about.html). Research through front-end delivery; deeper engineering is partnered. Site in zh-TW, zh-CN, and English. No published pricing, no findable design awards; visibility comes from conference speaking and community standing. Case study structure unverifiable (JavaScript site).
Mini SWOT. Strength: enterprise UX credibility and research depth. Weakness: process-heavy consulting model, deliverables end at design and front-end. Opportunity: enterprise AI redesign wave. Threat: AI compresses exactly the research-to-wireframe phase they bill for. For Hurulab: AJA validates that UX consulting sells in Taiwan, but their model is the "pictures before software" process Hurulab inverts.

**25sprout 新芽網路, Taipei.**
Founded 2012, "digital creative consultant", custom web apps plus their own SaaS products SurveyCake and BackStage (https://www.25sprout.com/). Chinese-first. No published pricing. Marketing is founder essays and product reputation; the agency arm has visibly given way to SaaS focus.
Mini SWOT. Strength: product credibility and engineering depth. Weakness: agency work is no longer the core business. Opportunity: SaaS growth. Threat: agency arm atrophies. For Hurulab: a competitor exiting the field, not entering it.

**Duotify 多奇數位, Taipei.**
Roughly 20 years old, 400+ enterprise clients, 800+ projects, Microsoft Gold Partner, Taiwan's leading Angular practice (https://www.duotify.com/). Clients include Lativ, 恆隆行 (Dyson Taiwan store), and LiteOn. Chinese-only. Marketing runs almost entirely on CTO Will 保哥's personal technical brand (18-time Microsoft MVP): blog, YouTube, courses, and a huge developer following, with training courses as the consulting funnel.
Mini SWOT. Strength: two decades of enterprise trust and a founder-brand lead machine. Weakness: engineering-led, design is secondary, Chinese-only, traditional delivery process. Opportunity: AI training demand. Threat: AI erodes billable developer hours, their core economics. For Hurulab: proof that a personal expert brand generates a services pipeline in Taiwan; Riaan's design-directs-AI story can run the same play in the design lane.

**5xRuby 五倍紅寶石, Taipei.**
Founded 2014, 25+ developers, Taiwan's largest Rails team, bilingual site, overseas clients including Shopmatic in Singapore (https://5xruby.com/en). Case studies follow problem, solution, measurable result (a logistics client's 10 to 20 monthly data-entry errors reduced to near zero). Training arm feeds the consulting funnel.
Mini SWOT. Strength: engineering reputation, bilingual delivery, and the rare metric-driven case study. Weakness: design is the weak leg; no brand or strategy offer. Opportunity: AI tooling adoption. Threat: same as Duotify, AI compresses billable hours. For Hurulab: the closest thing to a results-published competitor; their case format confirms metrics resonate with Taiwan B2B buyers.

**Goons 果思設計, Taipei.**
Founded 2012, bilingual, UI/UX plus app and system builds, metric-forward case studies: EasyCard Easy Wallet (5 million members, 4.8 stars), Hotai ChicTrip (2 million members) (https://www.goonsdesign.com/en).
Mini SWOT. Strength: consumer-app scale credentials with numbers displayed. Weakness: enterprise app niche, not SME web or commerce. Opportunity: fintech and mobility work. Threat: in-housing by their large clients. For Hurulab: rarely a head-to-head rival; a model for stat-block case studies.

**Rytass 八拍子, Taipei.**
Founded 2013, consulting plus brand strategy plus engineering under one roof; clients include the Taiwan Space Agency, Fubon, PTS, and the National Theater, and notably the TCloud redesign for the Ministry of Digital Affairs (https://tw.linkedin.com/company/rytass). Their marketing claim is retention: 62 percent of clients are multi-year partnerships. Low content output, referral-driven, strong in public sector.
Mini SWOT. Strength: the retention-as-proof message and public-sector trust. Weakness: near-invisible marketing; JavaScript site that cannot even be crawled. Opportunity: government AI projects. Threat: procurement cycles. For Hurulab: the closest structural mirror (consulting, design, engineering in one small firm), and evidence that retention numbers work as a trust message.

**Begonia 海棠設計, Taipei.** Founded 2005, UX consulting through build and paid post-launch operations, Chinese-first (https://begonia-design.com.tw/). Full-lifecycle offer, weak differentiation otherwise.

**Pineapple Web, New Taipei.** French-founded, trilingual (English, French, Chinese), design plus engineering plus CTO consulting, LINE official account, Clutch reviews with a metrics-rich testimonial (Habibi Macaron: traffic up 50 percent, conversion up 30 percent) (https://pineapple-web.com/). Small, expat-market focused. For Hurulab: the proof that a foreign-founded boutique can win Taiwan SME work with multilingual service and published results, exactly Hurulab's shape, just aimed at a different community.

### Category B: design-led consultancies and branding studios

Bito 甲蟲創意 (founded 2013, motion branding, Taiwan Tourism rebrand, Golden Horse ceremonies, Red Dot and iF, https://bito.tv/), JL Design (founded 2007, Taiwan's first motion graphics firm, Golden Horse and Golden Melody visual direction, https://jl.design/en), Whitelight Motion 白輻射影像 (founded 2016, Golden Melody 34, Red Dot, iF, Golden Pin, DFA, https://whitelightmotion.tv/about), Onion Design 洋蔥設計 (founded 1999, three Grammy nominations for packaging, https://oniondesign.com.tw/), UserXper 悠識數位 (founded 2007, UX research and audits, the strongest education-content funnel in Taiwan UX: courses, podcast, HPX community events, https://www.userxper.com/), and Plan b (sustainability strategy, CIT space, https://theplanb.cc/).

Shared pattern: founder celebrity, national prestige projects, international awards, earned editorial (Shopping Design, La Vie, VERSE), Behance and Instagram portfolios. None build software beyond front-end. None publish pricing. None publish outcome metrics. They compete with Hurulab for brand-identity budgets, never for working-software budgets. Collective mini SWOT. Strength: cultural prestige and awards. Weakness: deliverables stop at design; the client still needs someone to build. Opportunity: AI motion tooling. Threat: AI-native studios absorbing the build and then the design decision too. For Hurulab: these are partners or upstream referrers as plausibly as competitors.

### Category C: custom development houses

Trunk Studio 創科資訊 (Taichung, AWS builds, developer-community marketing, https://trunk-studio.com/), Titansoft (Singapore HQ, 201 to 500 staff, agile-culture content brand, mostly captive platform work, https://www.titansoft.com/en), WebComm 偉康科技 (founded 1998, listed, digital banking and FIDO security, claims 70 percent of Taiwan financial-sector clients, https://www.webcomm.com.tw/), TPIsoftware 昕力資訊 (founded 2005, about 600 staff, listed, enterprise middleware and conversational AI products, https://www.tpisoftware.com/en), SYSTEX 精誠資訊 (the enterprise ceiling: 5,000 staff, NT$43.9 billion revenue, https://tw.systex.com/), plus a long tail of app houses (JoinSoft, WishMobile, e-Giant).

Published market rates for this band: display apps NT$80,000 to 200,000, tool apps NT$200,000 to 500,000, e-commerce apps NT$300,000 to 800,000+ (https://www.pro360.com.tw/price/app_design); vendor-published bands run higher, MVP builds NT$800,000 to 1,200,000 and mid-size commerce apps NT$2 to 3 million (https://www.nss.com.tw, vendor-published, directional). Collective mini SWOT. Strength: engineering scale and enterprise compliance. Weakness: team economics require long timelines and big invoices; design bought in; mockup-approval process universal. Opportunity: enterprise AI integration. Threat: AI-assisted development undercuts their per-hour model hardest. For Hurulab: these firms explain why the Jadegia-class client gets quoted months; their pricing validates the NT$900,000 flagship band as market-normal for what was delivered.

### Category D: AI-native studios, the closest true competitors

**NerdTechnic 恩梯科技, Taichung.** Boutique, deliberately selective ("we do not take every client"). Ladder: custom systems, then AI added to existing systems, then "AI employees". Explicitly targets traditional Taiwanese SMEs whose data lives in "Excel, paper, and LINE groups". Traditional Chinese first with English and Japanese toggles. Publishes thought leadership on AI governance and even on designing a trial period for AI employees (https://nerdtechnic.com/news/ai-agent-poc-trial-period-strategy).
Mini SWOT. Strength: the same buyer, in their language, with governance content. Weakness: systems and automation focus, no design capability, no brand or storefront work. Opportunity: exactly Hurulab's. Threat: small capacity. This is the competitor to watch most closely in Taiwan; today they sell AI operations, not design-led builds, so the lanes differ, but the positioning language overlaps.

**戰國策 NSS Group, Taipei.** 25+ years, claims 30,000+ Taiwan SME customers from its hosting roots, now selling ChatGPT consulting and staged MVP-first AI development, with heavy subsidy-framed SEO content (https://www.nss.com.tw/chatgpt-consultant). Strength: enormous SME reach and subsidy literacy. Weakness: volume shop, no design credibility, no premium positioning. For Hurulab: they will reach the same buyers first through search; Hurulab wins the ones who want judgment, not volume.

**FraserTec 暉訊, Hong Kong. The closest analogue to Hurulab's whole model anywhere in Chinese-speaking APAC.**
"AI rapid development": enterprise software for Hong Kong SMEs in two weeks at web-development prices, claiming 70 percent cost savings; packaged builds (smart CRM in 10 to 14 days); a published process where AI-generated code passes through "professional refinement, expert review and optimization"; and open-source core apps prospects can try before committing to customization (https://www.frasertec.com/en/AIRapid). Bilingual, WhatsApp-first, SEO blog aimed at legacy-system SME owners. No named clients anywhere, anonymous testimonials only.
Mini SWOT. Strength: the speed-plus-human-review pitch, bilingual, SME-native. Weakness: no client proof, no design capability, try-first applies to their generic base product, not the prospect's own build. Opportunity: crossing to Taiwan. Threat to Hurulab: if this model lands in Taiwan first with a local face, the window shrinks. Watch them.

**SpeedMVPs, US and India, marketing into Singapore, Hong Kong, and Malaysia through programmatic city pages.** Production-ready AI MVP in 2 to 3 weeks, fixed price US$30,000 to 80,000, weekly live demos, full code ownership, PDPA and MAS FEAT compliance language localized per city (https://speedmvps.com/ai-development-agency-in/singapore). English only. For Hurulab: the template of the global wave document 2 predicted; they de-risk scope with weekly demos but still take payment first, and they cannot speak to a Taiwanese family owner in any sense of the word.

**Enterprise-tier context, not hireable for a family SME build:** iKala (enterprise AI transformation, educates the Taiwan market on vibe coding through its blog, https://ikala.ai/), Appier, APMIC (on-premise private LLMs, US$7 million series A, https://meet.bnext.com.tw/articles/view/53095), Profet AI (no-code AutoML for manufacturers, sells an AI lifecycle governance module called AILM, claims AI deployment within a week, https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20250701PR208.html), InfuseAI, Chimes AI. These firms prove governance language sells in Taiwan (Profet productized it) and set the vocabulary enterprise buyers will bring to conversations.

### The three questions that define the category, answered with evidence

Does anyone sell "test your own working product before you commit or pay"? No. FraserTec's try-first is their generic base product. SpeedMVPs' weekly demos happen inside a prepaid engagement. NerdTechnic's trial period is a content frame. Nobody found in Taiwan, Hong Kong, or Singapore offers the client's own build, usable, before commitment.

Does anyone sell documented AI supervision as a headline? Partially. FraserTec buries it in an FAQ, SpeedMVPs sells it as compliance architecture to startups, Profet AI productized it for factories. Nobody has made documented human supervision of AI the headline brand promise of a fast-build studio for SMEs.

Does anyone target traditional Chinese-speaking family businesses in their language? In their language, yes (NerdTechnic, NSS, FraserTec). As family businesses with succession framing, no. The 接班 (succession) framing is owned by government programs, including a dedicated MOEA subsidy program for second-generation manufacturing succession and digital transformation (https://www.sme.gov.tw/drsme/drsme/Plan/plan_more?id=61d38e728b924a4e910bf4d29a19c27e), and by generic 輔導 consultants, not by any AI studio. All three lanes remain open, and the succession subsidy program is an existing demand channel Hurulab can ride.

## Part 2: how these firms promote themselves and win clients

### The channel stack, observed

The standard stack is the firm's own site plus Facebook plus Instagram plus Behance. Facebook remains the default across every tier. Instagram is the portfolio teaser channel for design-led studios. Behance is the standard external portfolio (Block studio, Bito, JL Design, even mid-market CADIIS). Dribbble is essentially absent from Taiwan. LinkedIn appears only at internationally oriented firms (Tenten, Cheerway). LINE official accounts appear at the SME-market tier (CADIIS, ozchamp, Cheerway) and are absent at the prestige tier, which still runs intake by form and email. Motion studios use Vimeo, not YouTube.

### Directories and marketplaces

Clutch's Taiwan categories are thin and dominated by sponsored foreign agencies; no prestige Taiwan studio maintains a presence, and the first real local firms listed have single-digit review counts (observed at https://clutch.co/tw/web-designers). Clutch matters only for catching foreign buyers. HeyShow 黑秀網 survives as a legacy portfolio directory, not a lead engine. Freelance marketplaces (Tasker, PRO360) are price-shopping arenas that practitioners publicly describe as low-yield (https://hend.design/web-freelance/). TDRI matchmakes through programs like T22 rather than a directory. Shopify Experts status is a real niche channel (AkoCommerce, Tenten).

### Awards, and whether they convert

Golden Pin is the domestic anchor, and its winners page is literally titled "winning works and business matching" (得獎作品＆媒合洽商), meaning the award program itself converts wins into commercial introductions (https://www.goldenpin.org.tw/goldenpin/zh-TW/winners). iF and Red Dot are the standard international citations at the top tier. Awwwards marks the web-craft tier. Awards become sales collateral at every level, down to small shops keeping dedicated award pages. La Vie's Taiwan creative power 100 works as a media credential. Implication: one credible award submission per year is cheap positioning fuel, and Golden Pin comes with built-in matchmaking.

### Case study conventions, from pages fetched directly

Block studio (TSBB case): poetic narrative, tags, named team credits, full-bleed images, and an intake form with the published backlog. No metrics, no process, no client quotes. JL Design (TaiwanPlus): bilingual summary, mission, solution, film-style rolling credits for about 25 contributors. No business metrics. CADIIS (GlobalTek): short brand narrative, service checklist, screenshots. No metrics, no quotes. The only quantification in the market is forward-looking guarantees at the SEO tier (Cheerway: "5,000 impressions or 100 clicks within 30 days") and stat blocks at Goons and 5xRuby.

The conclusion for Hurulab is blunt: in the entire fetched sample, no Taiwanese agency publishes outcome numbers with client quotes in case studies. Document 3's proof strategy (instrument every project, capture two numbers at launch plus 90 days, one recorded owner sentence) would be unique in this market, not just better.

### Content marketing

The SEO pillar-content playbook (pricing guides, vendor checklists, subsidy explainers) is run by the mid-market (CADIIS, ozchamp, Cheerway, des13, frankknow), not by prestige studios, and it visibly works for capturing 網頁設計費用 search intent. Tenten runs the English equivalent at scale. The prestige tier barely blogs and blogs about craft. UserXper's education funnel (courses feeding enterprise consulting) and Duotify's founder-brand funnel are the two proven expert-authority models.

### How clients are actually won

Referral and repeat dominate. The best primary evidence found: an eight-year freelancer retrospective reporting roughly 70 percent long-term repeat clients, 20 percent referrals, 10 percent website inbound, with all marketplaces tried and abandoned (https://hend.design/web-freelance/). Block studio's 15-month backlog with no visible sales function says the same at the top tier. Government procurement (政府電子採購網) is a real channel that explains the prestige tier's public-sector portfolios. Cold outreach is consistently described as near-zero-yield in local sales literature, while LINE, with about 21 million monthly users in a population of 23 million, is where SME vendor conversations actually happen; SME-facing agencies list LINE consultation ahead of phone and email in their contact widgets (observed at ozchamp, CADIIS, webs.com.tw). Document 3's referral-engine design is therefore not just culturally justified; it matches how the market observably works.

## Part 3: corrections to document 2

The verification pass confirmed the macro picture and found three real errors plus several refinements. Document 2 should be amended.

**Error 1, TCloud. The NT$30,000 TCloud digital points program ended.** The official site states the point subsidy was exhausted and stopped on March 15, 2024 (https://tcloud.gov.tw/about). TCloud now operates as a SaaS catalog with occasional themed subsidies. The 2026 successor point-style programs cap at NT$20,000 per micro-business (https://metabiz.tw/smb-ai-digital-transformation-subsidy-guide-2026/). Every mention of TCloud NT$30,000 in documents 2 and 3 must be replaced.

**Error 2, the 60 percent failure statistic is not citable.** No MOEA survey, academic study, or government report was found behind the claim that over 60 percent of subsidized SMEs saw no measurable improvement. The figure circulates verbatim in vendor marketing without a source. The honest, citable version: PwC Taiwan's 2022 SME survey found 53.5 percent of digitally investing SMEs saw less than 5 percent revenue increase, and about 70 percent of SMEs use only basic digital tools (https://www.pwc.tw/zh/publications/topic-report/sme-digitalisation-survey-2022.html); the Legislative Yuan budget center has separately flagged weak ROI on SME digital transformation. The wedge argument survives fully, tools without judgment fail, but the sales script must use the PwC numbers, not the 60 percent line. A brand that promises audited honesty cannot lead with an unsourced statistic.

**Error 3, the under-30-employee subsidy is a training subsidy.** The NT$100,000 cap is real, but it reimburses NT$10,000 per trained employee for at least 12 hours of digital or AI training, optionally bundled with software (https://www.sme.gov.tw/30ai/). No 30 to 50 percent co-payment rule exists in it. The useful reframe: Hurulab's scope already includes client training at handover; this subsidy can fund exactly that component, which is a better story than a build discount anyway.

Refinements. Pricing tiers: direction confirmed, boundaries drifted; current guides put semi-custom at NT$80,000 to 250,000 and full custom at NT$300,000 to 1,000,000+ (https://cheerway.tw/website-design-cost-pricing-guide/), while standard e-commerce builds start lower than document 2 stated (NT$30,000 to 200,000 for platform-based builds, custom platforms above that). The Jadegia NT$900,000 band remains top-of-market and defensible against what category C charges for comparable scope. SBIR/SIIR: NT$3 million is the SIIR ceiling; SBIR phase 1 is NT$1.5 million and later phases go far higher. Macro stats: confirmed and now larger (1.715 million SMEs in 2024, 9.19 million employed, https://www.moea.gov.tw/Mns/populace/news/News.aspx?kind=1&menu_id=40&news_id=121491). Japan: the 12 trillion yen figure is from METI's 2018 DX report, not a current estimate, and has been criticized by Nikkei as misleading; the IT introduction subsidy was renamed the digital and AI adoption subsidy for 2026, max 4.5 million yen, coverage 50 to 80 percent (https://it-shien.smrj.go.jp/news/40013). Use with those qualifications.

## Part 4: the master SWOT for Hurulab

### Strengths

- The only demo-first motion in the market: the client uses their own working build before committing. Confirmed unique across Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Singapore in both languages.
- Documented AI governance as evidence, not claim: locked source-of-truth files (roughly 360 strings, 8 conflicts human-decided), 40 written handoffs on one project, five AI audits caught guessing and re-run. Competitors either bury supervision in FAQs or sell it as enterprise architecture; nobody can show a working record like this.
- Trilingual delivery (zh-TW, English, Japanese) inside one workflow, with a Kumamoto track record. Only Pineapple Web is comparably multilingual, and it aims at the expat market.
- One-seat economics: no developer payroll means the teaser demo costs nearly nothing, while every incumbent tier's economics require billing a team from day one.
- The NT$900,000 Jadegia precedent, priced at the top of the market for scope the full-custom tier delivers in triple the time.
- MoreHarvest's network and capital-adjacent referral lanes, which match how this market demonstrably buys (referral first).

### Weaknesses

- No published external proof yet. Every case is internal or in progress; no launch numbers, no client quotes on record. Against 20-year incumbents with 400-client walls, this is the gap that matters most, and document 3 already names the fix.
- One person is the product. Capacity ceiling, bus factor, and a hard limit of roughly 8 to 12 projects a year; a single stalled flagship blocks the whole proof strategy.
- Zero market presence in the channels this market actually uses: no Facebook or Instagram footprint, no Behance, no awards, no Chinese-language content, no LINE official account, no community standing.
- No engineering bench for what AI cannot yet do alone (deep integrations, regulated systems); Pedro is one gate, not a team.
- The premium price requires certainty the brand cannot yet prove publicly.

### Opportunities

- Publish outcome numbers with client quotes: literally no Taiwan agency does this in case studies. Document 3's instrumentation plan is a category-of-one move.
- Own the succession story: no AI studio uses 接班 framing, government money already flows through a dedicated succession-and-digital-transformation program, and the successor networks (二代大學, TDEA, N世代學苑) are assembled audiences with no incumbent vendor voice.
- Golden Pin's built-in business matching: one strong submission converts directly into commercial introductions.
- Block studio's 15-month backlog: the prestige tier is turning away or delaying exactly the demand Hurulab can serve in weeks.
- Training subsidy alignment: the under-30-employee program funds the training Hurulab already includes at handover.
- LINE official account as the SME contact channel, cheap and unclaimed by any premium design brand.
- The AI-skeptic conversion: every burned subsidy buyer (PwC: half of investors saw under 5 percent lift) is a prospect for supervised, outcome-checked delivery.

### Threats

- Tenten moves down-market with a productized AI offer; they have the brand, the bilingual reach, and the content machine to occupy "AI-first" in every search result.
- The FraserTec model crosses the strait: two-week AI builds with human-review language, sold to SMEs in Chinese, needs only a local face to land in Taipei.
- The global wave (SpeedMVPs pattern) adds Taiwan pages the moment the market looks lucrative; the 2 to 4 year window in document 2 is real and may be shorter.
- Subsidy churn: TCloud's death shows programs vanish mid-strategy; any pitch built on a specific subsidy has a shelf life.
- The "free first equals scam" reflex could misfire on the teaser demo if it is ever framed as a free website.
- AI tooling price compression from below (Wix ADI, AI site builders, RAB's 3-to-20-day WordPress builds) squeezes the middle of the market and will eventually pressure the premium tier's justification.

## Part 5: positioning recommendation and refined value proposition

### Where to position

The map has four occupied corners: prestige design objects (Block studio, Bito, JL), enterprise UX process (AJA, UserXper), engineering scale (Duotify, 5xRuby, category C), and AI-first volume (Tenten, NSS). The open position, confirmed empty by this research, is the intersection Hurulab already claims: working software the client uses before committing, governed AI with a written record, senior design judgment, sold in Chinese to family businesses in succession, at a premium justified by certainty. Documents 1 to 3 pointed here on reasoning; this document confirms it on evidence. Do not reposition. Sharpen.

Three sharpenings the evidence demands:

1. **Make published results the second moat.** The demo-first motion is the first moat, but FraserTec proves the speed language is already being copied regionally. The moat nobody is building: case studies with real numbers and recorded client sentences, in a market where the entire prestige tier publishes poetry and credits. Every Hurulab case story should end with two numbers and one quote, and that fact should itself be marketed: "we publish results; ask the others for theirs."
2. **Take the succession lane by name.** NerdTechnic owns "SMEs drowning in Excel and LINE groups", NSS owns subsidy search traffic. Nobody owns 接班. The positioning line for ring 1 sales: the neutral third party through whom the successor modernizes without confronting the founder, and the founder still makes the final call, because the live-variants process gives the founder the choice. This was already in document 2's psychology section; it should now be elevated to the primary ring 1 wedge because the competitive field there is confirmed empty.
3. **Reframe the teaser demo against the scam reflex.** Never "free". Always: invitation-only, built from your real brand, two days of our time because we choose you. Scarcity framing (a numbered handful of invitations per quarter) converts the market's free-means-scam suspicion into selectivity, and matches how Block studio's backlog and NerdTechnic's "we do not take every client" already signal quality in this market.

### The refined value proposition ladder

The three rungs from document 1 survive contact with the evidence, with these adjustments:

1. **Speed without a team**: unchanged, but now with a market fact attached: the best design studio in Taiwan is booking 15 months out; Hurulab delivers working software in the time Block studio's queue advances one month.
2. **You decide on real software, not pictures of software**: confirmed as the emotional core, and now provably exclusive: no agency in Taiwan, Hong Kong, or Singapore offers it. Say so, plainly: nobody else in this market lets you use your own product before you commit. We checked.
3. **Possible versus impossible**: unchanged (the value-add prototype story).
