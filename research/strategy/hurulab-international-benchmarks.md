# Hurulab international benchmarks

*Document 5. Researched July 5, 2026. Three sets benchmarked: established product studios, AI-native studios, and one-person premium studios. All sites fetched directly. Headlines quoted verbatim. This document feeds the brand strategy (document 7) and the launch roadmap (document 6).*

## Set 1: established product studios

The studios: Metalab, Ustwo, Work & Co (now part of Accenture Song), Instrument, Clay, Fantasy, BASIC/DEPT, Pentagram.

### The five patterns that separate them from typical agencies

1. **The headline states an identity, not a service list.** "We make interfaces" (Metalab). "Digital products people stick with" (Ustwo). "Lead by Design" (Fantasy). Six words or fewer, a worldview, and the work carries the rest.
2. **Fewer, deeper cases; curation as a flex.** Instrument shows 6 cases, Fantasy 8. Metalab's Slack entry is a logo and one sentence: "The rest is history." Nobody shows 40 projects.
3. **Outcomes quantified and front-loaded.** Ustwo puts "The impact" with big numbers before the process story (https://ustwo.com/work/rvo-health/). Work & Co eliminated case studies entirely: one metric sentence per client ("resulting in a 298% increase in average viewing time", https://work.co/clients). BASIC/DEPT has a formal results block ("25% increase in mobile revenue").
4. **The intake form is a qualifying instrument.** Metalab asks how you heard of them and what stage your company is; Pentagram gives you a partner's email and nothing else. Friction signals "we choose clients too." Clay does the opposite for colder traffic: public FAQ, phone number, engagement models named (time and materials, fixed fee, retainer).
5. **They productize their delivery model as marketing.** Fantasy publishes its actual AI pipeline in the LIV Golf case: Claude Code plus Figma MCP, AI review on every pull request, senior engineers owning every merge, shipped in 72 days, under the line "Built with AI, Owned by Humans" (https://fantasy.co/work/liv-golf). The process is the marketing asset.

### The three lessons that transfer to Hurulab

1. **The Fantasy play:** make the AI-directed pipeline the headline proof, anchored on a date and a number. Hurulab's version is stronger: the record already exists (handoffs, locked files, five caught audits).
2. **The Pentagram logic:** the owner is the creator and the primary contact for every client. Matches how Taiwanese family businesses buy: trust in a person, not a company.
3. **The Work & Co plus Ustwo format:** one outcome sentence per client, impact before process, a client quote from another owner, and BASIC/DEPT's "relationship" section describing what working together week by week feels like. Plus Clay's one transparency move: name the engagement models, because buyers fear open-ended billing more than high prices.

## Set 2: AI-native studios (2024 to 2026 wave)

The studios: Lazer Technologies, Every (consulting plus studio), AI-Native Agency, Creme Digital, Fractional AI, Rubric Labs, Sublayer, Parallel, AE Studio, plus R/GA and IDEO as rebrand context.

### What has become cliché

"AI-native", "weeks not months", "ship", "production not demos", "senior" as quality shorthand, stat bars and logo walls, "pause or cancel anytime", dark-mode glow gradients or bright Framer templates. The emerging premium word being claimed now: "taste" (Parallel's headline is "Win on Taste").

### What none of them say: the confirmed global whitespace

- **Demo before commit.** Nobody offers "use the working software before you sign." Closest: AI-Native Agency's phased pricing (discovery still costs US$5,000 to 8,000) and Creme's cheap fixed MVP (paid upfront). Unclaimed globally in this cohort.
- **Design-led governance.** Governance exists only at the compliance end (AI-Native Agency: NIST-aligned audit logs, eval harnesses, public contracts) and is absent from every design-led studio. Nobody combines premium design taste with documented AI supervision.
- **A named human supervisor of record.** The wave is anonymous teams or founder bios. Nobody says: one senior designer personally directs and signs off everything the AI produces, like an architect stamping drawings.
- **Honest provenance.** No studio shows clients what was AI-generated versus human-decided. Hurulab's audit trail is exactly this.

### Pricing model trends

Three tiers crystallizing: productized subscription at the low end (Creme: US$4,500 per two-week sprint, or US$9,500 fixed two-week MVP), phased fixed price with a contractual guarantee in the mid-market (AI-Native Agency: discovery US$5,000 to 8,000, build US$15,000 to 40,000 fixed, production by week 7 or half back, written into the contract), and opaque embedded enterprise at the top. Fixed price plus a guarantee is the mid-market convergence; hourly billing is universally positioned as the legacy model to attack.

### Best and worst brand moves observed

Best: AE Studio's "Scientists Who Ship" plus alignment research as a commercial trust asset; Every's proof by public artifact (GitHub stars, own products, daily writing); AI-Native Agency's radical verifiability (read the contract before the first call). Worst: Creme selling craft-at-speed with a typo ("Itirate") and copy pasted from another agency's site on its own homepage, the exact failure mode buyers fear; interchangeable headlines at the top end; NDA-veiled portfolios that corrode premium claims.

## Set 3: one-person premium studios

The operators: Designjoy (Brett Williams), MDS (Matt D. Smith), House of van Schneider, Offgrid (Fons Mans), Jordan Hughes, Rauno Freiberg, Dann Petty, BYQ Studio, Goodspeed Studio.

### The economics

Designjoy is the ceiling for pure subscription: US$4,995 a month, roughly 20 to 25 concurrent clients, reported US$1.2 to 2 million a year, about US$176 a month in tool costs (https://www.designjoy.co, https://www.starterstory.com/stories/design-joy-breakdown). Solo-with-a-name commands 2 to 5 times the commodity subscription price. Scope caps are the real secret: the queue (one request at a time), published exclusion lists, async only, pause-instead-of-refund billing, and productization. Every mature solo operator built a second, non-linear income leg (product, course, template).

### The trust problem, ranked by what works

1. The craft of their own site: universal, the site is the audition.
2. Named client and outcome proof: Offgrid's homepage is nine logos; Goodspeed leads with "$500M+ raised by our clients"; one heavyweight name outworks fifty case studies.
3. Solo-ness reframed as the benefit: "you'll work directly with me", no juniors, no handoffs.
4. Risk reversal and fixed pricing: 75 percent back in the first week, pause anytime.
5. Public knowledge artifacts: MDS open-sourced his service agreement on GitHub (https://github.com/mds/sa).
6. Institutional validation: talks, features, press.
7. Follower counts: platform-bound, least transferable.

### What transfers to Taiwan and what replaces the rest

Transfers fully: the craft of the own site (a flawless bilingual site is a category-breaking signal next to 15-year-old competitor sites), founder-direct dealing (老闆對老闆 is the native trust model; the account-manager layer is the alien thing), risk reversal and fixed fees, and knowledge artifacts. Does not transfer: personal brand on X, Dribbble validation, follower counts. Replacements: referral chains through accountants, associations, and suppliers; Golden Pin, iF, and Red Dot as the badges this audience recognizes; LINE official account and association talks as the content channels; a printed case book for face-to-face meetings; and one authority-figure testimonial from someone the client's world respects (a known second-generation owner beats any volume of peer praise). A plain-language public contract in Chinese, copying MDS's move, would be novel in the market.

### The three most instructive operators

Designjoy for the complete economic blueprint (his leverage was tooling; Hurulab's is AI). Fons Mans and Offgrid for the studio-brand wrapper: an openly independent designer who speaks as "we", designed his own typeface, and lets nine client names carry the pitch. MDS for longevity and the open-contract trust artifact.

## What this means for Hurulab, in one paragraph

The global evidence agrees with the Taiwan evidence: the demo-first motion is unclaimed anywhere, and the combination of design taste with documented AI governance is unclaimed anywhere. The brand should therefore be built like the best of each set: a six-word identity headline, six deep cases maximum with impact numbers first, the pipeline published as proof (the Fantasy play, but with a real audit trail), the founder as named supervisor of record (the Pentagram logic), engagement models named in public (the Clay move), solo-ness sold as the feature (the Designjoy move), and a studio-brand wrapper so one person can carry studio prices (the Offgrid move). Avoid the cliché stack: "AI-native", "weeks not months", glow gradients, and unverifiable superlatives.
