Hurulab stakeholder answers

Name and role: Cal, product manager (PM).

1. What Hurulab is: A consultancy partner that solves other companies' business problems with product management, engineering and design in one team.
2. Plain words version: A mixture of helping businesses with their goals and turning ideas into products. I wouldn't say "fix" as it implies something is broken, but I wouldn't straitjacket us by saying we just build stuff. I would say we help businesses with their goals, leaning heavily on our design and engineering pedigree, using our versatility and experience, and not getting distracted by process BS.
3. Plain words for an outsider: Covered in answer 2.
4. One big idea: Outcomes over process.
5. Audacious vision: Redefining the category for Taiwan. A localised version of refining how agencies work. Note: I don't like the word "agency", it's a bit too mercenary.
6. Why it exists, the itch: Partly that agencies waste time on process, but mainly that others do not spend time learning about the business problem, they take the order and just build.
7. Origin story: By developing its own process that outperformed traditional methods. It wants to use this to help other businesses who otherwise struggle to meet their goals.
8. Why it matters to me personally: I like solving real problems.
9. Who it's for, and not for: Businesses with a real goal.
10. Client's world before: Companies who lack the skills, scale, or are otherwise unable to meet their goals within their constraints.
11. The unspoken problem: Probably a mixture of not knowing what they need, not trusting vendors, and lacking in-house skill, but leaning more towards not knowing what they need.
12. Real signals seen: So far it's been leveraging existing connections through Henry.
13. What's unique: A mixture of all three. An evolved process that is proportioned to the problem, by a versatile, creative and experienced team of skilled, bleeding-edge tool users.
14. Happy client quote: They got delivered on a budget that beat competitors, it was quicker than others quoted me, and they demonstrated something usable and not just back-and-forth meetings. They understood the problem quickly.
15. A real moment: Grasping the problem quickly.
16. AI's role: A tool we use.
17. Knowledge advantage: A combination of our way of working, with tool mastery thrown into it. Domain experience would only apply if we work with a client in the proptech sector. Tool mastery sits under our way of working, it is a part of it.
18. Making it repeatable: Similar to the above, codify the process, but part of that is reusing repeatable and scalable processes, tools/tech and artefacts.
19. Trust in AI use: Judge us on results. I'll add, I don't think clients ultimately care. They have a business problem that needs solving, and trust is earned on whether we can demonstrate we can do it within their constraints (time/budget/blockers etc).
20. Culture: Outcome-focused, low ceremony.
21. Good work, never ship: A mixture of never shipping what misses the goal, sloppy work, or process over substance. Sloppy work goes without saying, but I'll say we never ship waste, which could mean low quality or tasks that do not contribute to the overall goal.
22. How decisions are made: Fast and evidence-led.
23. The blend: Understand first, then build.
24. Getting to root cause: All three, and in that order (dig till we find the real cause, no blame just fix it, then learn and codify it). One caveat with the word "blame": if a team member is underperforming then blame can be justified, but it is an individual performance issue and not an open discussion.
25. Riskiest assumption: That clients will pay for this, and to expand: that they are expecting a team to just do what they are told, i.e. client-supplier. They forget that we have something they cannot do, do not want to do, or that we can do better. It must be an alliance/partnership, and customers are expecting a JFDI (just do it) operation.
26. Smallest version: A quick working demo.
27. Signal of success: We solved the real goal.
28. Growth engine: Clients bringing other clients.
29. Rules never to break: Slight amendment to "never just take the order": we never take the order, we solve the business problem, or we say no.
30. Reserve for hard times: Look at previous deliveries and see what scope there is to monetise, or apply the Google 10% rule to everyone as a matter of course. There may be a business in one of these ideas we could put our own money behind.
