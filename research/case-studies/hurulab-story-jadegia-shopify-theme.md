# Hurulab story: JADEGIA Shopify theme

*Extracted 2026-07-05. Evidence from project files and interview. Design perspective. Audience: owners and decision-makers at companies in Taiwan, weighing whether to trust a new studio with something that matters to them.*

## Part 1: evidence

### What does design do here

Design set the rules before it drew a single screen. It wrote a behavioural floor first, a set of documents defining how buttons, spacing, focus, and motion must behave on every phone, tablet, and desktop, then built the brand on top of that floor so quality could not quietly erode under deadline (showcase/design-decisions.md, showcase/project-copy.md). Brand visuals layer on the floor and never override it; when a rule has to be weakened, it is weakened on the record for one named case, not by accident (showcase/design-decisions.md).

Design led the decisions that usually get left to engineering. Price alone decides whether a jade piece is bought online or seen by private appointment, so the model cannot be set up wrong (showcase/features.md, docs/design-rules/STATUS.md). A full page-transition effect was built across several rounds and then deleted, because the house read cleaner without it (showcase/design-decisions.md, showcase/features.md). Design also owned the identity end to end: the warm cream, forest green, and brushed bronze palette, the small-caps editorial type, the trilingual navigation, the cinematic museum experience, and the quiet reading pages (showcase/project-copy.md, showcase/design-decisions.md).

### How we do these projects

Two directions were built in parallel on a shared, brand-neutral base, so they could be compared without re-arguing the fundamentals each time (showcase/project-copy.md, showcase/process-timeline.md). JADEGIA chose one. The project then folded onto a single control system where every colour, font, and spacing step lives in one place and can be re-tuned without a developer touching code (showcase/project-copy.md, showcase/features.md, showcase/technical-details.md).

Work moved surface by surface against designer review, and recurring quality problems were fixed as one site-wide pass at a time, each pass ending with the rule written down so the next page inherited it instead of re-deciding it (showcase/process-timeline.md). The work ran seven weeks, was directed by the designer, and was built with Claude (showcase/project-copy.md).

### Our value proposition

JADEGIA gets a storefront that finally matches the brands it is measured against, Cartier, Bulgari, and Tiffany, with detail that feels deliberate on every device (showcase/project-copy.md). It serves Taiwan and its Hong Kong, Singapore, Japan, and Malaysia markets in the right languages, sells everyday pieces online while routing museum-grade jade to private appointment, and can be re-tuned by the brand itself without paying a developer for every change (showcase/features.md).

The clearest proof it landed: JADEGIA reviewed the directions and approved the flagship one. The greenlight was the reaction (interview).

### How we do it differently

The realistic alternative was a Taiwan web shop dropping JADEGIA onto a standard template, or a design agency delivering handsome mockups and handing the build to someone else. Either way the result would be generic, impersonal, and not built around how JADEGIA's own customers actually shop (interview). Here the rules were written before the pixels, the commerce model was made impossible to misconfigure, and a working feature was deleted the moment it hurt the brand. None of that comes out of a template drop or a mockups-only handoff (showcase set, interview).

### Open gaps

- No word-for-word client quote. The approval is described, not quoted (interview).
- No before-and-after numbers. The old site's problems and any sales impact are not measured, and the store is not live yet, so value rests on client approval rather than market results (showcase/metadata.md, docs/design-rules/STATUS.md).
- The client's brief is reconstructed as "all of the above" (looked cheap, could not sell online, needed several markets, wanted a serious digital presence), not recorded in their own words (interview).

## Part 2: the story

### Headline

JADEGIA's jade belonged beside Cartier's. Now its storefront does too, governed by rules, not assembled from a template.

### Hero

The hero is JADEGIA, the Taiwan jade house. Its goal: an online home that carries the weight of the pieces themselves, jade worth as much as anything in a Cartier window. Hurulab is the guide, not the hero.

### Story structure (Pixar seven-step)

1. Once there was a Taiwan jade house whose pieces sat, in worth, beside Cartier and Tiffany, and wanted to be seen that way online.
2. Every day its jade sold through an old marketing site that could not take an order, spoke one language to a five-market audience, and looked ordinary, quietly underselling the very thing it existed to sell.
3. Until one day JADEGIA decided its online home had to match the pieces, and asked MoreHarvest to build it.
4. Because of that the studio wrote the rules before the pixels, how every button, space, and motion must behave on every phone, tablet, and desktop, then built two full directions on that shared base so JADEGIA could choose by seeing, not by guessing.
5. Because of that, when JADEGIA approved the flagship direction, the whole storefront could fold onto one control system, and design could make the hard calls on purpose: price alone decides whether a piece is bought online or seen by appointment, and a finished motion effect was deleted because the house read cleaner without it.
6. Until finally JADEGIA had a storefront that reads like the house it is, warm, deliberate, trilingual, everyday pieces bought online and museum-grade jade seen by private appointment, and the client greenlit the direction.
7. Ever since then the brand grows from one governed system instead of drifting screen by screen. The lesson: a luxury storefront is not assembled, it is governed.

### Three key points

1. Rules before pixels. Proof: a written behavioural floor plus a foundation layer that enforces it, so every "can we cut this corner?" became a decision on the record, not an accident under deadline (showcase/design-decisions.md).
2. A brand you can steer yourself. Proof: one control system where colours, fonts, and spacing live in a single place and are re-tuned without paying a developer for every change, and it is the direction JADEGIA itself approved (showcase/features.md, interview).
3. Design made the hard calls. Proof: price alone decides online versus appointment, so it cannot be set up wrong, and a fully built page-transition effect was deleted because it muddied the brand (showcase/features.md, showcase/design-decisions.md).

### Language notes

- Short-word flags: "consolidate" replaced with "fold onto one system"; "deliberate" and "deliberately" varied so they do not repeat in the same beat.
- Analogy: the single control system is like the lighting desk for a whole theatre. One board changes every light in the house at once, instead of climbing to each fixture. Familiar to any owner who has run a shop floor or a showroom.
- Anaphora opportunity, use once on the differentiation: "Not a template. Not a mockup handed to someone else to build. Not a site that looks the part but cannot take an order."
- Readability check: pass. Sentences are short; step 5 is the longest, so keep it tight on any edit.

### Visual notes

- Rules before pixels: a page of the written rule set beside the exact screen detail it produced, the rule and then the thing.
- A brand you can steer yourself: the theme's control panel next to the storefront shifting colour, one dial and the whole brand moves.
- Design made the hard calls: a split image, a museum piece reading "Available by appointment" beside an everyday piece with a buy button, plus a before-and-after frame of the transition that was cut.

### Delivery notes

- Surprise or reversal: the deleted feature in step 5. The reader expects a studio to add polish to impress a luxury client; instead the team removed a finished, working effect. That reverses the expected "more equals better" trajectory rather than adding a fact.
- Specific detail added: the vague "high-value pieces are handled separately" is replaced with the concrete rule, jade at or above NT$200,000 shows "Available by appointment" and cannot be added to the bag; and "built quickly" is replaced with the seven-week span, 14 May to 3 July 2026.
- Call to action for a Taiwan audience: close on what it means for the reader, not just for JADEGIA. A version they can act on: "If your product is worth more than your website admits, that gap is the first thing we fix." This turns the lesson into an invitation without overselling.
- Culture story, repeatable: "we built it, then deleted it." The page-transition system, made across several rounds and then cut because the house read better plain. It is a story Hurulab can retell to prove that restraint is a service, not a shortfall.
