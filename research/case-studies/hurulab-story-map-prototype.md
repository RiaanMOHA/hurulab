# Hurulab story: map-prototype

*Audience: a company tired of agencies that take orders and hand back an Excel sheet. Hero: the MoreHarvest team. Design perspective.*

## Headline
MoreHarvest was losing the room with slides. Now the team opens one live map that carries a buyer from a whole region down to one return, wi-fi or not.

## Confidentiality note
Real property names (Chateau Life Ozu, Kikuyo, Haramizu) and real financials live in the app. If this goes public, swap exact prices and projections for ranges first.

## Story structure (Pixar seven-step)
1. Once there was a team at MoreHarvest with a huge deal to sell: property next to Japan's new chip corridor, where the nation is betting billions and the world's biggest chipmaker is moving in.
2. Every day they sold it on slides. Flat numbers on a page. The slides never showed how the water, the government money, the global firms, and one real building all tied together.
3. Until one day the pitch had to win two different rooms, an investor deciding to fund and a buyer deciding to buy, often on meeting-room wi-fi that drops mid-sentence. Slides could not carry that.
4. Because of that, Hurulab refused to just make prettier slides. We found the real fault: the story fell flat because it was told, not shown. So we wrote the design system and named the feeling of every moment before we built one feature.
5. Because of that, the team got one live map that moves like a guided tour. It drops into Kumamoto, reveals the forces behind the boom one at a time, then flies down to a real building with its real drive time to the fab and its real numbers. All of it frozen inside the app, so it runs with the internet off.
6. Until finally the deck was dead. The team opens one map, in English or Traditional Chinese, and the deal unfolds in front of the person across the table instead of sitting still on a page.
7. Ever since then, MoreHarvest has a tool that sells with them, and Hurulab has the proof of how we work: find the real problem, design the system, build the real software, hand it over ready to use.

## Peter Guber check
- Opening challenge: present (losing the room).
- Struggle: present (two rooms, dead wi-fi, connections slides cannot show).
- Call to action: present. Closing line for the reader: "If your team is still selling a big idea on flat slides, that is the gap we close. We do not take the order. We find the problem, build the fix, and train your people to run it."

## Trigger structure check
- Trigger event: named. The pitch had to win two rooms, offline, and slides failed.
- Transformation: named. One offline, bilingual, live map replaces the deck.
- Life lesson: named. An agency that diagnoses and builds beats one that takes orders and ships a file.

## Three key points
1. We diagnose, we do not decorate. Proof: the design system and the beat sheet came before any feature. Nothing animates without a named beat and a target feeling. "The Approach" is written to feel like awe.
2. We build the real thing. Proof: 37,000 lines of working software, no framework, with real road routes to the JASM fab frozen into the app and real financials, live on the web and used daily.
3. We build for your real room and your real customer. Proof: it runs with the wi-fi off, so a dropped signal never kills a pitch, and it ships in Traditional Chinese for Taiwanese investors, for both the funder and the buyer.

## Language notes
- Short-word flags applied: "opportunity" is now "deal" or "bet"; "interactive" is now "live"; "commitment" trimmed.
- Analogy: the map is a tour guide who never needs wi-fi. The old deck was a brochure left on the table.
- Anaphora, drafted for the close: "We find the problem. We build the fix. We hand it over ready."
- Readability: pass. The one long sentence in step 5 is split into three.

## Delivery notes
- Surprise/reversal: sits on key point 3. The reader expects fancier software to be more fragile. The reversal is that this map is harder to break than the slides it replaced, because everything it needs is frozen inside it and it runs offline.
- Culture story for MoreHarvest: "the day we killed the wi-fi and the pitch still worked." One line the sales team can retell.

## Proof point gaps
