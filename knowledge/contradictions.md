# What the sources disagree on

Found by reading the whole corpus on 4 September 2026. Nothing here is settled by writing it
down. Each one needs a person to rule on it.

Three kinds: **open**, nobody has decided; **resolved**, decided elsewhere and recorded here
so it stays decided; **corrected**, a factual error already fixed.

---

## Open. Nobody has ruled.

### 1. What discovery costs

Five different answers exist:

| Source | The price |
|---|---|
| `research/strategy/funnel-and-pricing-one-pager.md` | Discovery fee is 5 to 15% of build value, 100% upfront |
| `meetings/2026-08-10-discovery-process.md` | About $3,000 per hour, quoted by estimated team hours |
| `research/strategy/go-to-market.md` | First working piece NT$80,000 to 250,000; full build NT$600,000 to 1,500,000 |
| `research/market/taiwan-market-research.md` | The jadegia engagement at NT$900,000 |
| `docs/evidence.md` part 9 | An hourly rate shown on the website with a floor of about $3k for a week |

The 10 August meeting also says price is about 20% negotiable. The funnel one-pager says never
discount, scope down instead. Both are dated policy.

### 2. Whether the business sells projects or subscriptions

`funnel-and-pricing-one-pager.md` makes subscription the default pitch: "A, subscription
(default pitch): setup fee + monthly retainer, we run operations and model swaps."

Every other document sells the opposite. The 6 July meeting records "Anti-subscription
positioning: build products clients own and control, staff trained to operate
independently". The coming-soon copy published
"No subscriptions. No lock-in. No fee you pay forever."

These cannot both be the offer.

### 3. What the demo is

Three incompatible descriptions:

- `go-to-market.md`: a free working redesign built in one to two days, sent as a single link,
  "no deck, no proposal, no meeting request."
- `funnel-and-pricing-one-pager.md`: a 60-minute in-person meeting, built live, "live only, no
  code, no links," and the artefact never leaves the room.
- `meetings/2026-08-10-discovery-process.md`: two calls, vetting then scoping, with no live
  build mentioned at all.

Related: `go-to-market.md` says the teaser is free. `competitor-analysis.md` and
`launch-roadmap.md` both say never free, invitation only, because free triggers a scam reflex.

### 4. What the client keeps

The 4 August meeting agreed: "Everything produced during discovery belongs to the client."
That promise was published in Chinese and English in the coming-soon copy.

The 10 August meeting says: "Client can keep it for the interim; full build is not handed
over."

This one matters more than the others, because the stronger promise is already written as
client-facing copy.

### 5. How many people do the work

The interviews are five people. The strategy documents are built on one.

- `competitor-analysis.md`: "One person is the product," "no developer payroll," a ceiling of
  "roughly 8 to 12 projects a year."
- `international-benchmarks.md`: "solo-ness sold as the feature," "one senior designer
  personally directs and signs off everything."
- `funnel-and-pricing-one-pager.md`: "Two operators, no exceptions: Henry interviews, Delta
  builds."

**Delta appears nowhere else in `knowledge/`.** Nobody has said who that is.

Note also that `trust-badges-plan.md` requires "at least one developer employed" to qualify
for a badge, while `competitor-analysis.md` says there is no developer payroll.

### 6. Who the client is

Every source names a different buyer, and the 12 August meeting recorded reconciling them as
an open next step that nobody has done.

| Source | The buyer |
|---|---|
| `docs/design-rules/brand.md` section 5 | Companies burned before, companies with the goal but not the means, companies spending on AI with nothing to show. Taiwan first, then APAC |
| `research/positioning-board/Foundation decisions/Who we go after.md` | "Established **Asian** companies, ambitious and quality-minded" |
| `research/strategy/the-twelve-steps.md` | "companies with a real goal, **big or small**" |
| `meetings/2026-08-04-meeting-2.md` | "Second and third generation owners taking over traditional **family businesses**" |
| `docs/gtos.md` | **Enterprises** |
| `docs/evidence.md` part 10 | A narrower cut: owner-led Taiwan businesses selling mainly offline |

Two of these conflict directly. **Asia against Taiwan:** brand.md sequences Taiwan first, the
board leads with Asia and no sequencing, and those are different market entries. **Big against
small:** the twelve steps say "big or small", while the 4 August meeting killed a factory
client because hurulab's offer was about seven times their budget.

`docs/evidence.md` part 9 records the two profiles as "named as needing reconciling". That
reconciliation is the open item.

### 7. The six positioning questions that were never closed

`research/positioning-board/Questions/` holds six decisions with options written out and no
answer marked: the emotional core, home versus international first, whether AI goes in the
headline, the growth engine, the north star metric, and how loud to be about enterprise
ambition.

### 8. Ed was never interviewed

Every strategy document describes the July decisions as provisional pending the CTO's input.
He has not been asked since. Either the decisions stop being provisional, or he gets asked.

### 9. The settled brand and the live site are two different pitches

Found 4 September 2026 while auditing. This is not a corpus problem: it is a gap between what
`docs/design-rules/brand.md` settles and what `site/index.html` actually says.

**The category.** brand.md section 3 settles "a consultancy", owner decision 30 July,
reversing "agency", and records that "Henry and BY have not been told." The live site uses no
category word at all. The research corpus still says agency in five places and "design and
engineering consultancy" in two.

**The single idea.** brand.md section 7 settles it: "Companies waste capital because they buy
software they have never used. **So you use it before you pay for it.**" It is also the only
claim `docs/evidence.md` part 4 confirms nobody else in Taiwan occupies.

**That promise appears zero times on the live site.** The site leads instead with judgement:
"Buying solutions you cannot judge is a gamble. hurulab makes it a decision, with work you can
see and numbers you can check." That line traces to `docs/evidence.md` part 10, a positioning
recommendations document whose own date and author evidence.md says are not known. It is a
reasonable line. It is not the one brand.md settles, and brand.md was never amended.

**The site form.** brand.md section 7 also settles that "the site is itself the first demo.
The visitor uses something real on the page", and names the failure mode: "a gimmick, an
interaction that demonstrates nothing... If it does not survive the question 'what did that
prove', it is wrong." The live site's only interaction is a rotating list of six phrases.

**One of those six phrases is a rejected framing.** The site says hurulab is your "technical
team". `docs/evidence.md` part 9 records: "The 'we are your tech team' framing was rejected as
role-based: positioning should focus on outcomes and decision confidence."

Either brand.md is amended to what the site says, or the site is rebuilt to what brand.md
settles. Nobody has ruled, and until someone does the brand foundation and the only public
artifact disagree about what hurulab sells.

## Resolved. Decided, and recorded so it stays decided.

### hurulab is not part of MoreHarvest

**Owner ruling, 4 September 2026.** They are separate companies. MoreHarvest is a client.

This needs stating because the corpus says otherwise in several places, and those documents
are still here:

- `meetings/2026-07-06-meeting-1.pdf` is titled "building the MoreHarvest agency brand from
  scratch," and all eleven of its steps say MoreHarvest.
- `meetings/2026-08-10-discovery-process.md` says Moreharvest throughout and never says
  hurulab.
- A MoreHarvest profile deck lists "HuruLab Inc., 自主 AI 研發與軟體團隊" (in-house AI R&D and
  software team) as one of four sibling brands sharing moreharvest.com. That deck is
  `plans/moreharvest-profile-deck-spec.md` in the Desktop archive and is not reproduced here,
  so this bullet cannot be checked from `knowledge/` alone.
- `projects/jadegia-storefront.md` credits MoreHarvest as the vendor.

Against that, the 4 August meeting has an open action item, "Confirm legality of carrying Moreharvest
credentials over to hurulab", glossed there as "Higher-tier badges already held by
Moreharvest; check if they can be shown under hurulab." Two divisions of one company do not
need to check that.

**What follows from the ruling:** four of the five write-ups in `projects/` are MoreHarvest's
own internal work, not hurulab client work. Only jadegia is an external paying client, and
that write-up names MoreHarvest as the contracting party. Whether any of it can be presented
as hurulab's own client work is a separate question, and it has not been answered.

### The brand system was replaced

accent, and Schibsted Grotesk, Instrument Sans and Sometype Mono.

All of it was retired on 30 July 2026 and none of it was reinstated. The current system is
`docs/design-rules/`. See `docs/design-rules/brand.md` section 15 for what was released.

### The Chinese name is undecided

`brand/superseded/coming-soon-copy-2026-08.md` ships **hurulab 胡了** as the hero wordmark.

The visual identity brief in the same folder says the Chinese name is undecided and explicitly
out of scope. Nothing records a decision between them.

## Corrected. Already fixed in the files.

Each of these was disproved by a later document in this same corpus, and the correction was
never carried back to the document that carried the error. Fixed on 4 September 2026.

| What was wrong | The correction |
|---|---|
| "Over 60 percent of SMEs receiving digital subsidies saw no improvement" | Not citable. No survey behind it. Replaced with PwC Taiwan 2022: 53.5 percent saw under 5 percent revenue increase |
| The NT$30,000 TCloud subsidy sold as live | Ended 15 March 2024. Successor programs cap at NT$20,000 |
| Four spellings of the company name | Normalised to `hurulab`. The pass also flattened one
counter-example in the visual identity brief, `Not "Huru Lab"`, which was restored |

Three further errors are flagged in `research/market/competitor-analysis.md` and were left in
place there, because that document exists to record them: the under-30-employee subsidy has no
co-payment rule, Japan's 12 trillion yen figure is from a 2018 report and has been criticised
as misleading, and the SBIR phase 1 ceiling is NT$1.5 million rather than NT$3 million.
