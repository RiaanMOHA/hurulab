> client keeps. Nobody has ruled between them.


* Two-call structure agreed:  
  * Call 1 (30 min): vet the client, extract one clear problem, confirm fit  
  * Call 2: decision makers in the room, deeper problem validation, scoping begins  
* First call is primarily about vetting, not selling  
* Decision makers must be present on the second call, otherwise the process stalls  
* Problem scope: one specific, well-defined problem per sprint, broken down from a larger area if needed  
  * If client can’t agree on a problem, send them away to align internally first

# **Quoting and Pricing**

* Rate: \~$3,000/hour (standard rate), not a flat fee  
* Quoted by estimated man-hours across the discovery team assembled for that engagement  
* Measurement unit: weeks (minimum one week, typically two to four)  
* Flexibility on price: up to \~20% negotiable depending on client and potential upside  
  * Below half the quoted rate: walk away, no hard sell

# **Discovery Sprint Delivery**

* Goal: deliver something tangible at the end of the sprint (mockup, screenshot, prototype)  
  * Client can keep it for the interim; full build is not handed over  
* Key message to clients: small upfront spend to validate before committing to a large build  
  * Framed as risk reduction vs. signing a long-term deal with uncertain ROI

# **Business Goal and Engagement Outcomes**

* Primary goal: win ongoing consultancy contracts off the back of discovery sprints, not volume of sprints  
* Four ways an engagement can end:  
  1. Client walks away after quote  
  2. Client buys sprint, takes deliverable, no further work  
  3. Client buys sprint, then contracts for further build work (ideal outcome)  
  4. Client buys sprint, then commissions a second discovery sprint for a new problem
