# hurulab stakeholder interview, combined answers

This file collects the answers from all five stakeholder interviews in one place, question by question. The questions are the 30 from the hurulab stakeholder interview prompt, in order. Where an answer ran long, it has been condensed to the point, in the person's own voice. Short answers are left as they were. Nothing has been added or invented.

Respondents:

- Riaan, lead designer. Interviewed 8 July 2026.
- Cal, product manager. Interviewed 8 July 2026.
- Henry, founder. Interviewed 8 July 2026.
- BY, team member. Interviewed 10 July 2026.
- Pedro, web developer. Interviewed 15 July 2026.

---

## Question 1. Who are you, and what is your role in hurulab?

**Riaan:** Riaan, lead designer.

**Cal:** Cal, product manager.

**Henry:** Henry, founder.

**BY:** BY, team member.

**Pedro:** Pedro, web developer.

## Question 2. In your own words, what is hurulab?

**Riaan:** A new kind of agency combining AI, tech and design to build products for companies big and small. We learn the whole business, build the thing, then train the team to run it, so you only really need us once.

**Cal:** A consultancy partner that solves other companies' business problems with product management, engineering and design in one team.

**Henry:** A design and AI agency.

**BY:** A design and AI agency.

**Pedro:** A technology partner.

## Question 3. If someone outside the industry asked what hurulab does, how would you say it in plain words?

**Riaan:** We build your product, help you put AI to work, understand your business, and train your team to run it themselves.

**Cal:** We help businesses reach their goals, leaning on our design and engineering pedigree and our versatility, without getting bogged down in process. Not fixing something broken, and not just building stuff, but getting them where they want to go.

**Henry:** We help companies put AI to work.

**BY:** We use design and AI to build software and apps for businesses.

**Pedro:** We build digital tools.

## Question 4. If hurulab stood for one single idea, what would it be?

**Riaan:** Once we are done we become useless, because we have given you everything to continue on your own, not just the product but the tools and training to keep doing it.

**Cal:** Outcomes over process.

**Henry:** A highly efficient method (business, design, technology) that helps companies get more out of their capital, their money, time and labour.

**BY:** Technology made human.

**Pedro:** Craft.

## Question 5. What is the audacious, almost unreasonable version of what hurulab could become?

**Riaan:** The go-to agency for every company, big and small, in Taiwan.

**Cal:** Redefining the category in Taiwan, a local version of rethinking how agencies work. Though I don't like the word agency, it feels too mercenary.

**Henry:** Default partner for multiple industries.

**BY:** To become a world-class studio.

**Pedro:** A global studio.

## Question 6. What question, or what itch, is hurulab actually trying to answer? Why does it need to exist at all?

**Riaan:** Why so much capital is wasted, why vendors just take the order without understanding the business, and why good design is so rare here. Also why companies struggle to scale outside Taiwan while keeping global standards. We want to guide clients through the whole process, not just take an order.

**Cal:** Partly that agencies waste time on process, but mainly that others don't spend time learning the business problem, they just take the order and build.

**Henry:** Why is so much capital wasted.

**BY:** Good work is too slow. Great products take too long and cost too much, and we want to change that.

**Pedro:** Good tech is out of reach for most businesses, hurulab changes that.

## Question 7. How did hurulab actually start? Who came together, and how?

**Riaan:** It is starting right now. We saw a possible need in the market, and it is beginning now, with Jadegia as the first case study.

**Cal:** By developing its own process that outperformed traditional methods, and wanting to use it to help other businesses who struggle to meet their goals.

**Henry:** Not answered.

**BY:** A small group who had worked together and knew each other decided to start it.

**Pedro:** It grew out of client work. Freelance or side projects grew until it made sense to form a company.

## Question 8. Why does this work matter to you personally?

**Riaan:** I like solving real problems.

**Cal:** I like solving real problems.

**Henry:** How fast AI is accelerating and changing how things get built, and the combination of business, design and technology.

**BY:** I want to grow. A chance to learn and be part of something new.

**Pedro:** I want real impact, work that makes a visible difference for real people and businesses.

## Question 9. Who exactly is hurulab for? And who is it not for?

**Riaan:** Companies sick of agencies that cost a lot, take a long time, and don't really deliver solutions. Not for traditional companies that won't accept change.

**Cal:** Businesses with a real goal.

**Henry:** Companies who want to use AI but have a hard time measuring the ROI.

**BY:** Ambitious teams that care about quality. Not those chasing quick, cheap work.

**Pedro:** Ambitious founders and startups building something new, not for slow moving organizations.

## Question 10. What does that ideal client's world look like before they find you? What are they stuck on?

**Riaan:** Burned by slow, costly agencies, and lacking the skills or scale to meet their goals within their own constraints.

**Cal:** Companies who lack the skills, scale, or are otherwise unable to meet their goals within their constraints.

**Henry:** Drowning in tools, no clear results, spending on AI with nothing to show.

**BY:** Burned by bad past results. They tried before and got poor work they had to redo.

**Pedro:** Big idea, no builders. A clear vision but no team that can actually design and build it.

## Question 11. What is the real problem they have that they might not say out loud?

**Riaan:** They do not know what they need.

**Cal:** A mix of not knowing what they need, not trusting vendors, and lacking in-house skill, mostly not knowing what they need.

**Henry:** The ROI on AI is not transparent, they don't know the difference between burning 100K or 5000 tokens.

**BY:** They fear being fooled, sold hype and made to waste money again.

**Pedro:** They feel out of their depth. Technology decisions feel too complex and they are afraid of choosing wrong.

## Question 12. What real actions have you seen from potential clients, such as a booking, a payment, or a referral, as opposed to polite interest?

**Riaan:** Jadegia is almost live, and there is genuine interest. I have done my own research and found actual people who need this.

**Cal:** So far it's been leveraging existing connections through Henry.

**Henry:** Not answered.

**BY:** Referrals. Clients have sent us to other people they know.

**Pedro:** None yet, still early. Mostly interest and conversations, no hard commitments yet.

## Question 13. What can a client get from hurulab that they cannot get anywhere else?

**Riaan:** Business, design and tech in one team that understands the business and builds it fast, and you can use the product before you pay.

**Cal:** An evolved process sized to the problem, run by a versatile, experienced team of skilled, bleeding-edge tool users.

**Henry:** Measurable ROI on every AI project, business, design and technology in one team, and a proven method, not one off experiments.

**BY:** Strong design and real AI skill in the same team.

**Pedro:** Design and AI in one team. Most shops do one or the other, hurulab does both deeply in the same small team.

## Question 14. What would a happy client say about hurulab to a friend, in a single sentence?

**Riaan:** They understood us fast.

**Cal:** Delivered on a budget that beat competitors, faster than others quoted, and showed something usable instead of endless meetings. They understood the problem quickly.

**Henry:** They made AI actually pay off for us, and they speak business, not just tech.

**BY:** The best team I have worked with.

**Pedro:** Worth every cent. Not cheap, but the best money spent on the business.

## Question 15. Describe one specific, real moment or scene that captures what working with hurulab is like.

**Riaan:** Using it before paying, tapping through your own working product early on.

**Cal:** Grasping the problem quickly.

**Henry:** Speaking the client's language, how much money they save, how many hours are cut, and how something impossible before is now possible.

**BY:** A demo that wowed. We showed a client the work and they were genuinely amazed.

**Pedro:** The first demo reaction, when a client sees the first working version and realizes it is real.

## Question 16. How central is AI to hurulab? Is it the product itself, a tool you use behind the scenes, your main point of difference, or something else?

**Riaan:** We actually know how to use AI, how to train people to use it, and how to build with it, and we use design taste so it doesn't look like AI slop.

**Cal:** A tool we use.

**Henry:** AI is a tool behind the scenes.

**BY:** We build software with AI and design the workflow, deciding for each part whether AI or ordinary code is the right tool, with good design on top.

**Pedro:** A tool behind the scenes. AI makes the team faster and better, but clients buy outcomes, not AI.

## Question 17. What knowledge or data advantage does hurulab have, or need to build?

**Riaan:** Our way of working plus tool mastery, and design taste on top of AI so it does not look like AI.

**Cal:** Our way of working plus tool mastery. Domain experience only applies for a client in proptech. Tool mastery is part of our way of working.

**Henry:** Using ourselves as proof of concept, but we still need to build it.

**BY:** Know-how on AI plus code. We know when to use AI and when to use normal code.

**Pedro:** Reusable playbooks and code. A growing library of patterns, tools, and code from past projects.

## Question 18. How will you turn projects done once into something repeatable, that works again and again?

**Riaan:** Both, codify the process and reuse scalable tools, tech, and artefacts.

**Cal:** Codify the process, part of which is reusing scalable processes, tools, tech and artefacts.

**Henry:** Not answered.

**BY:** A clear, set process we follow on every project.

**Pedro:** Internal tools and templates. Every project feeds a shared toolkit that makes the next one faster.

## Question 19. How do you want clients to trust the way hurulab uses AI?

**Riaan:** A hard one. Case studies, but also showing results quickly and understanding their business well with the tools we have.

**Cal:** Judge us on results. I don't think clients ultimately care about the how, they have a problem to solve, and trust is earned by showing we can do it within their constraints.

**Henry:** Let results speak, start small and prove it.

**BY:** Prove it with results. Let the quality of the work earn their trust.

**Pedro:** Humans check everything. AI drafts, people verify, nothing ships without human review.

## Question 20. What kind of culture do you want inside hurulab?

**Riaan:** Not answered. Riaan said this is not really an important question.

**Cal:** Outcome-focused, low ceremony.

**Henry:** Everyone is the CEO of their own project.

**BY:** Work smart with the best, newest technology, understand it deeply, and use it to bring real value to clients.

**Pedro:** Craft and calm. Focused, unhurried work by people who care about quality.

## Question 21. What does good work mean here? Put another way, what would hurulab never ship?

**Riaan:** Never ship anything that misses the goal, and never ship sloppy or slop-looking work.

**Cal:** Never ship what misses the goal, sloppy work, or process over substance. We never ship waste, whether that is low quality or work that doesn't serve the goal.

**Henry:** It needs to solve the client's problem and bring value.

**BY:** Never sloppy work, bad design, or hype.

**Pedro:** Never ship half finished. Nothing goes out that is buggy, untested, or visibly unpolished.

## Question 22. How do you want decisions to get made inside hurulab?

**Riaan:** Fast and evidence-led.

**Cal:** Fast and evidence-led.

**Henry:** Depends on the decision. Strategic ones sit with whoever is in charge and takes responsibility. (Answer incomplete.)

**BY:** Fast and clear. Decide quickly with a clear owner, do not overthink.

**Pedro:** Consensus among the core. The core team talks it through until there is broad agreement.

## Question 23. What is the blend of design, technology, and human understanding that defines hurulab's work?

**Riaan:** Understand the problem first, then build, with design taste holding it together so the tech and AI feel human, not generic.

**Cal:** Understand first, then build.

**Henry:** Start with the business problem, design the experience, build the tech.

**BY:** People first, then tech. Start with what people need, then use design and technology to serve it.

**Pedro:** All three, equal weight. Design, engineering, and human insight sit at the table as equals on every project.

## Question 24. When something goes wrong, how do you want to get to the real root of it?

**Riaan:** Ask why until the real cause shows.

**Cal:** Dig till we find the real cause, fix it without blame, then learn and codify it, in that order. One caveat on blame, if a team member is underperforming that is an individual performance issue, not an open discussion.

**Henry:** Ask why repeatedly until the real cause shows.

**BY:** Fix it, then write down the lesson so it does not happen again.

**Pedro:** Keep asking why. Do not stop at the first explanation, keep digging until the real cause shows.

## Question 25. What is the riskiest assumption behind hurulab, the one you most need to check before betting everything on it?

**Riaan:** That clients will pay for this.

**Cal:** That clients will pay for this, and that they expect a team to just do what they're told. It has to be a partnership where we bring something they can't or won't do themselves, not a client-supplier or just-do-it operation.

**Henry:** Whether everyone is ready to prove what deeply integrated business, design and technology really means.

**BY:** That enough clients will pay well for design plus AI work.

**Pedro:** Demand really exists. That enough of the right clients are out there looking for exactly this.

## Question 26. What is the smallest version of hurulab you could put in front of a real client, to learn whether this works?

**Riaan:** A quick working demo.

**Cal:** A quick working demo.

**Henry:** A single tool solving one clear problem.

**BY:** A quick prototype, a fast demo of what we can do.

**Pedro:** One paid pilot project. One real client through one small paid project, end to end.

## Question 27. How will you know it is working? What real signal are you watching for, rather than a number that just looks busy?

**Riaan:** First, whether our strategy lets us cold call or message potential clients (LinkedIn, 104, wherever). Then, whether we close contracts faster because we can explain and show what we do, at a better price than anyone else. Best of all, word of mouth, clients recommending us.

**Cal:** We solved the real goal.

**Henry:** We successfully use this method to help clients from at least three different industries.

**BY:** All of them, clients come back, clients refer us, and clients pay well.

**Pedro:** Unprompted referrals. New clients arrive saying someone told them about hurulab.

## Question 28. Which engine do you want to drive hurulab's growth: clients coming back, clients bringing other clients, or paid acquisition?

**Riaan:** Clients bringing other clients.

**Cal:** Clients bringing other clients.

**Henry:** Large companies for stable cash flow, smaller ones to keep us sharp, agile and close to the edge.

**BY:** All of them, clients coming back, clients bringing others, and paid acquisition.

**Pedro:** Clients bringing clients. Word of mouth and referrals as the main source of new business.

## Question 29. What are the few rules hurulab should never break, whatever happens?

**Riaan:** Never ship slop.

**Cal:** We never just take the order, we solve the business problem, or we say no.

**Henry:** Never take a client we cannot truly help.

**BY:** Always be honest, never drop quality, always respect people.

**Pedro:** Never sacrifice quality, and never burn out the team.

## Question 30. What would you keep in reserve, so a bad month cannot sink hurulab?

**Riaan:** Not answered. Riaan said this is not important at all.

**Cal:** Look at past deliveries for anything worth monetising, or apply a Google-style 10 percent rule across the team. There may be a business in one of those ideas worth backing ourselves.

**Henry:** Cash reserve, recurring revenue, a lean team with low fixed costs, and a fallback service, all of it, plus building the team with genuinely talented people.

**BY:** A few steady clients for steady income.

**Pedro:** Side income streams. Retainers or small recurring income that keeps money coming in between projects.
