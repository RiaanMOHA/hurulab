## Positioning statement

For established companies whose business is excellent but whose digital presence is not, hurulab is the consultancy that designs and builds working software fast, with AI doing the labor and senior design judgment making every call. Unlike agencies that show pictures and deliver months later, hurulab puts the real, working thing in the client's hands within weeks, and the client decides on what they can actually use.

## The one-line brand story

Use it before you pay for it.

Supporting line: one designer directing AI, delivering in weeks what used to take a team months, and some things no team would have delivered at all.

## Decisions, July 8, 2026 (provisional, revisitable)

Made by Riaan with three interviews in hand on July 8. BY (July 10) and Pedro (July 15) have since answered without contradicting them, so all three decisions hold unless Ed contradicts them.

1. Category word: agency, redefined. Keep the word the market searches and that Henry and Riaan use, but redefine it against the mercenary, order taking reputation Cal rightly dislikes. The redefinition line: the agency that learns your business, builds the working thing, and then makes itself useless.
2. AI framing: outcomes first, AI as a strong capability, not the headline. Lead with the business result and the working thing. Name AI as how the labor gets done, held to taste, never as the pitch.
3. Lead promise: use it before you pay, for now, explicitly open to change. The other two promises sit right behind it, in sequence.

The one line. We learn your business, build the working thing, and hand it over, so you use it before you pay and only need us once.

"See what it earns, before you commit." Support line, where AI appears as capability: business, design, and technology in one team, with AI doing the labor and taste making every call.

## The value proposition ladder

Three rungs, in ascending order of power. Lead with rung two in sales conversations, close with rung three.

1. **Speed without a team.** Days and weeks, not months. Three days to a live product hub. Twelve days to two complete, testable store designs. Three to four weeks to a full interactive pitch or a 3D property walkthrough. No developer payroll behind any of it.
2. **You decide on real software, not pictures of software.** Jadegia's team tapped through two finished versions of their own store on their own phones twelve days after kickoff, then chose a hybrid no mockup process could have produced. This rung is the emotional core of the brand because it removes the buyer's deepest fear: paying for months and hoping the result matches the slides.
3. **Possible versus impossible.** The value-add prototype, in the founder's own words, would not have been built at all the traditional way. An interactive investor journey with a live map, a 3D walkthrough, and animated financials was never on any agency's quote. This rung takes hurulab out of price comparison entirely.

## Message map (Gallo's technique: one headline, three messages, each with proof)

**Headline: use it before you pay for it.**

**Message 1: design directs, AI builds.**
Proof: a designer who writes no code shipped a Shopify Plus storefront, a 20-step interactive pitch, a 3D walkthrough, and a live product hub. Every technical decision was delegated to AI; every design decision was made by a human. The record shows it: walls named by what is on them, a banned grey, photos overruling blueprints.

**Message 2: the AI never works unsupervised here.**
Proof: five AI website audits came back wrong in the same two ways, and the designer caught it all five times and sent the AI back until every claim was observed, not guessed. When a blueprint said a window sill sat at 2 meters and the photos showed 0.85, the photos won. This message is the direct answer to the AI-skeptical buyer.

**Message 3: discipline is what makes the speed safe.**
Proof: locked source-of-truth files governing every word (roughly 360 strings, all 8 conflicts decided by a human), written handoffs after every session (40 on one project alone), layout specs that carry no color, font, or copy so nothing gets invented, and pre-execution reviews that caught three real blockers before a single file was touched.

## The brand narrative (Pixar seven-step spine, client as hero, hurulab as guide)

1. Once there was a company that was excellent at what it did. Decades of craft, serious customers, a real reputation.
2. Every day, its digital face let it down. The website looked ten years old, the pitch was a deck read once and forgotten, and the tools were spreadsheets and paper.
3. Until one day, the pressure became real: a competitor moved online, a successor took over, or a customer asked why they could not buy from their phone.
4. Because of that, they met a different kind of consultancy. Not a big agency with a big team and a big timeline. One senior designer, directing AI, who said: in two weeks you will hold the working thing in your hands.
5. Because of that, they did something no mockup ever let them do. They used their own future product, on their own phones, and changed it while it was still soft.
6. Until finally, the thing was live. Built in weeks, at a fraction of the old cost, and in some cases a thing no supplier had ever offered them at any price.
7. Ever since then, they own more than a website or a pitch. They own proof that their company can move fast, and a partner who showed them how.

Per Gallo: the client is always the hero. hurulab is the guide. Never open a pitch, a case study, or a web page with hurulab as the subject of the first sentence.

## Differentiation, by competitor category

- **Versus traditional Taiwanese web agencies (NT$50,000 to NT$300,000 for corporate sites, 1 to 3 months, mockups first):** they sell a process ending in a deliverable. hurulab sells a working result the client steers live. The client never approves a picture; they approve software.
- **Versus full-custom development houses (NT$300,000 to NT$1,000,000+, 3 to 6 months):** comparable or better fidelity, delivered in weeks, and the client tests real variants instead of signing off on wireframes.
- **Versus freelancers (NT$15,000 to NT$80,000):** a freelancer brings one skill. hurulab's one seat covers research, design, build, translation, QA, and documentation, with an auditable written record of every decision.
- **Versus template shops and DIY (Wix, generic Shopify themes, NT$20,000 to NT$50,000):** templates make everyone look the same. hurulab's work is built from the client's own brand truth, extracted page by page before anything is designed.
- **Versus big consulting AI practices:** they sell strategy decks about AI. hurulab ships working software made with AI, and hands over the keys with no vendor lock-in.
- **Versus other AI-native studios (the closest real competitor):** most sell speed. hurulab sells speed plus governance: the locked content files, the human conflict resolution, the reality-checked QA. The line that separates us: anyone can prompt an AI; almost nobody audits it five times until it stops guessing.

## Objection handling for the AI-skeptical traditional owner

- **"AI makes things up."** Correct, which is why nothing ships here without a human check against reality. Our record shows the AI being caught guessing five times in a row and being sent back each time. The AI never works unsupervised.
- **"We are not a tech company."** You do not need to be. You approve what you can see and tap. If you can judge a showroom, you can judge this.
- **"How can one person replace a team?"** One person does not do the labor. The AI does the labor of many. The person does the one thing AI cannot: know what good looks like, and say no.
- **"What if it breaks after you leave?"** Everything is documented as it is built: written handoffs, a locked source of truth, training included in scope. You own it. No lock-in is part of the offer, not a favor.
- **"It sounds too cheap or too fast to be good."** Fast here does not mean careless; it means no waiting between people. Judge the first two weeks. If what you hold in your hands is not better than what agencies show you after two months, walk away.

## Brand voice and language rules

- Sentence case everywhere. Never title case, never uppercase headings.
- Never use em dashes. Use commas, colons, periods, or parentheses.
- Plain words. Target grade 5 to 6 readability in all narrative text. Precise terms are allowed only in evidence sections.
- One number per claim, and only real numbers: twelve days, three days, 360 strings, 40 handoffs, NT$900,000.
- No hype vocabulary. Banned: revolutionary, cutting-edge, game-changing, seamless, empower, leverage (as a verb), synergy.
- The metaphor family is direction: the film director who never holds the camera, the head chef whose plates all pass their eyes. Use one per piece, never both.
- Two-thirds narrative, one-third data, per Gallo. Every story ends on ownership or proof, never on applause we do not have.
- Admit gaps openly. Honesty about what is unfinished is a brand asset in a market drowning in AI hype.

## How the positioning flexes across the four geographic rings

- **Ring 1, Taiwan:** lead with trust and tangibility. "Use the thing before you pay for the thing" lands hardest with buyers who fear being cheated by suppliers. Reference the Jadegia story: a 94-year-old Taipei 101 house chose its site by using two finished versions.
- **Ring 2, Chinese-speaking APAC (Hong Kong, Singapore, Malaysia):** same core, add the cross-border credential: we already work trilingually (zh-TW, English, Japanese) inside one workflow, and our best work sells Taiwanese capital into Japan.
- **Ring 3, rest of APAC, Japan first:** reframe around Japan's own language of urgency. Japan's government calls it the 2025 digital cliff, with estimated losses of 12 trillion yen a year for companies that fail to modernize. hurulab arrives with a Kumamoto track record, not as an outsider.
- **Ring 4, rest of world:** the method is the product. Sell the way of working (design-directed AI with governance) as the case studies, since local presence and guanxi advantages fall away.
