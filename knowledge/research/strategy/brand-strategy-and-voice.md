## Brand strategy in one paragraph

hurulab is a studio-brand wrapper around one senior designer directing AI (the Offgrid model), positioned as the only consultancy in Chinese-speaking APAC where the client uses their own working product before committing (confirmed unique, documents 4 and 5), where every AI decision has a named human supervisor of record (the Pentagram logic: the owner is the creator and the primary contact), and where results are published with numbers in a market that publishes poetry (documents 4 and 5). The client is always the hero; hurulab is the guide. The brand's job is to make one person credibly worth studio prices to a traditional Taiwanese family business, and the mechanism is proof over promise at every touchpoint.

## The brand idea

**The signed work.** An architect stamps drawings. A chef tastes every plate before it leaves the pass. hurulab signs everything the AI builds: one name, personally accountable, with the written record to show it. This idea unifies the three confirmed whitespaces (demo before commit, design-led governance, named supervisor of record) into one ownable thought, and it extends the direction metaphor family from document 1 without replacing it.

## Naming

hurulab stays. Rationale to publish: huru is freedom (from uhuru); the lab is where freedom gets built into working software. Open decision Riaan must make: the Chinese name. Three routes, with a recommendation:

1. Transliteration: 呼嚕實驗室 or similar. Warm, memorable, slightly playful; 呼嚕 is the sound of purring, which is friendly but may undercut premium.
2. Meaning-first: a name carrying 自由 (freedom) or 親手 (with one's own hands), for example 自研所 or 手作數位. Riskier, needs a native-speaker gut check.
3. Keep the English name only, styled as hurulab in all Chinese copy, the way premium Taiwan studios (block studio) keep mixed-script names.

Recommendation: route 3 for launch (lowest risk, matches market practice at the premium tier), with route 2 explored during phase 0 with native speakers. Do not launch route 1 or 2 without testing against the face-and-prestige dynamics in document 2.

## Voice

Four attributes, each with a test:

1. **Plain.** Grade 5 to 6 readability in narrative text. Test: can a sentence drop a grade level without losing meaning? Rewrite it.
2. **Specific.** One real number per claim: twelve days, 360 strings, 40 handoffs. Test: could a competitor paste this sentence into their site? If yes, cut it.
3. **Calm.** No hype, no urgency theater. Banned (from document 1, extended with document 5's cliché list): revolutionary, cutting-edge, game-changing, seamless, empower, leverage as a verb, synergy, AI-native, weeks not months, ship as a flex, world-class.
4. **Honest to a fault.** Gaps admitted openly; the record shows the AI being caught, not just succeeding. Test: does every case story contain at least one thing that went wrong and how it was caught?

Formatting rules carry over from document 1: sentence case everywhere, no em dashes, no emojis, Chinese and English as equals, never machine-parallel.

## Messaging architecture

**The headline (identity, six words or fewer, the Metalab pattern).** Options, English first, working zh-TW second:

1. "Use it before you buy it." / 先用，再決定。
2. "Working software, signed by a person." / 真的軟體，有人簽名負責。
3. "We build it. You try it. Then you decide." (longer, for the hero subhead rather than the headline)
4. "The agency that makes itself useless." (the make yourself useless idea as a headline)

Recommendation: option 1 as headline, option 2 as the about-page identity line, option 4 tested as a bolder alternative. Decide in phase 0, test with two trusted successors from the network.

**The message map stays as document 1 built it** (design directs AI builds; the AI never works unsupervised; discipline makes the speed safe), with the fourth proof rung from document 4 added: we show our numbers. New supporting line from the benchmark evidence, usable in sales conversations: nobody else in this market lets you use your own product before you commit. We checked.

**Work presentation format (from document 5, binding for the site):**

- Work index: one outcome sentence per client, the Work & Co format, maximum six featured cases.
- Case pages: impact numbers first, then the story in the Pixar structure, then a "how we worked together" section (the BASIC/DEPT relationship section, renamed plainly), ending with the owner's recorded sentence.
- Engagement models named in public (the Clay move): invitation demo, first working piece (NT$80,000 to 250,000), full build (NT$600,000 to 1,500,000), care retainer. Fear of open-ended billing is the buyer's bigger fear, not price.
- The method page publishes the pipeline the way Fantasy published LIV Golf, but with the real audit trail: the locked files, the handoffs, the five caught audits, and what was AI-made versus human-decided (honest provenance, unclaimed globally).

## Objection handling

Document 1's five objection answers stand unchanged. One addition from document 4: when the teaser demo triggers the free-means-scam reflex, the answer is selectivity, not persuasion: "we send a handful of these a quarter, to businesses we have already researched. It is not free work; it is our audition, and yours."

## Stakeholder input, July 8, 2026

Five of six interviews in: Henry (founder), Cal (product manager), Riaan (lead designer), BY (team member), and Pedro (web developer, answered 15 July). One still to come, Ed (CTO). What to carry into the verbal identity.

The order of the work is itself a message. All five agree: understand the business problem first, then design, then build. Henry, start with the business problem, design the experience, build the tech. Cal, understand first, then build. Riaan, understand the problem first, with design taste holding it together. BY, people first, then tech, start with what people need and use design and technology to serve it. Pedro, all three at equal weight, design, engineering, and human insight sit at the table as equals on every project. This is a clean, plain statement of method that belongs on the method page and in the headline test set, because it answers the buyer who fears tech for its own sake.

A return rung on the message map. Alongside the existing three (design directs AI builds, the AI never works unsupervised, discipline makes the speed safe) and the fourth proof rung (we show our numbers), Henry frames the whole company around the productivity of capital, money, time, and labor. The sales line, in his words, measurable ROI on every AI project. The buyer's unspoken problem is that the ROI on AI is not transparent, so the brand's job is to make the return legible, not just the work.

The idea the identity should carry: we make ourselves useless. Riaan's biggest single idea is that when the project ends, the client has the product plus the tools plus the training to keep going alone, so they only need hurulab once. This is ownable, it answers the fear of endless vendor dependence, and it should be tested as a headline and lived on the method page. It also connects to the anti subscription instinct already in the room.

Rules and culture, verbatim and usable. Henry: everyone is the CEO of their own project, and never take a client we cannot truly help. Cal: outcome focused and low ceremony, we never ship waste, and the amended rule, we never take the order, we solve the business problem, or we say no. Riaan: never ship slop, and never take a company that will not accept change. BY: work smart with the best and newest technology, understand it deeply, and use it to bring real value to clients, and never ship sloppy work, bad design, or hype. Pedro: craft and calm, focused unhurried work by people who care about quality, never ship half finished, never sacrifice quality, and never burn out the team. These belong on the about and method pages.

The tension the identity must resolve. Two words still split the room, but the balance has shifted further. On the category label, Henry, Riaan, and BY are comfortable with agency (Riaan uses it freely, BY calls hurulab a design and AI agency), Cal dislikes it and prefers consultancy partner, and Pedro calls hurulab a technology partner, which sits nearer Cal. On how central AI is, Henry centers measurable ROI on AI, Riaan and BY center AI capability plus design taste so the output never looks like slop, Cal keeps AI subordinate to the method, and Pedro is blunt in the same direction, AI is a tool behind the scenes, clients buy outcomes, not AI. The emerging lean, now backed by three of the five on the label and all five on outcomes-first, is to keep agency and to lead on the outcome and the make yourself useless idea, with AI as a strong capability, not the headline. Test the headline options above in two flavors, one that names AI and one that leads on the outcome. One phrase from BY worth testing in the AI-naming flavor: technology made human. One from Pedro worth testing on the method page: humans check everything.

Engineering view, now largely captured. BY and Pedro have both answered, and both technical reads reinforce rather than contradict the lean: know when to use AI and when to use ordinary code, design the workflow deliberately, keep design taste on top, and, in Pedro's words, AI drafts, people verify, nothing ships without human review. Pedro also adds the most concrete repeatability engine yet, reusable playbooks, internal tools and templates, every project feeding a shared toolkit, which strengthens the method page. Ed (CTO) is still to come and stays an open assumption. Riaan and Henry keep the provisional call on the agency and AI question, revisitable if Ed weighs in.

Decision, July 8, 2026 (provisional). Agency, redefined: the agency that learns your business, builds the working thing, and then makes itself useless. Outcomes first, AI as a strong capability, not the headline. Lead promise, use it before you pay, open to change, with make ourselves useless right behind it. For the headline set above, this picks the outcomes flavor: "Use it before you buy it." leads as the identity line, and the AI naming line becomes a support line, not the headline. Add make ourselves useless as a headline candidate to test: "We build it, you use it, then we go." and "The agency that makes itself useless." Keep AI out of the top line, name it in the support copy as how the labor gets done, held to taste. BY's answers, added July 10, and Pedro's answers, added July 15, support this direction and do not change it.

## Creative brief for the visual identity

Working visual reference, July 8, 2026 (provisional). No brand style is locked. There is no palette, type system, or logo decided yet. For now the direction is cold neutrals only, black, white, and cold grey, stark and minimal, with Balenciaga (balenciaga.com) as the working reference for tone: austere, high fashion, generous space, restraint over decoration. Treat the earlier warm palette (the sand and amber in the old brand system notes) as one discarded exploration, not the house style. Anything built now, on Miro or the site, stays in cold neutrals until Riaan sets a real direction.

**What the identity must do.** Make one person feel like an institution without pretending to be big; feel premium to a 60-year-old founder and current to a 35-year-old successor; work bilingually as equals; and carry the signed-work idea visibly.

**Direction references (from the benchmarks, for study not imitation):**

- Restraint tier: Pentagram's typography-first museum restraint; Metalab's three-word confidence; Offgrid's logo-wall minimalism and the self-branding rigor of a custom typeface.
- Personality tier: Sublayer's broadcast-station identity as proof that distinctiveness beats polish; BASIC/DEPT's registered-mark styling (the ® system) as an ownable typographic tic.
- Avoid: dark-mode glow gradients, bright Framer-template look, stat-bar-and-logo-wall sameness, anything that reads "AI startup 2025."

**Motifs worth exploring (Riaan's call):** the stamp or seal (the signed work; seals carry weight in Chinese business culture and connect to the supervisor-of-record idea), the audit trail as a visual system (timestamps, record lines, version marks as graphic elements), and the two-state motif (before and after, picture versus working thing, mockup versus tappable).

**Typography note.** The Jadegia system proved the pairing logic (a distinctive display face plus a quiet body face, Chinese display chosen with the same care as Latin: JF LanYangMing over Noto Sans headings). The hurulab identity should apply the same standard to its own face choices; the site is case zero and the typography is the first exhibit.

**The site as proof.** Every benchmark set agrees the studio's own site is the audition. The hurulab site must be the fastest, most tappable thing a prospect has touched: mobile-first, one moment of surprise (per the Gallo rule), the method page as the trust engine, and the whole thing built the way client work is built, with its own audit trail published.

## What gets designed in phase 0, in order
