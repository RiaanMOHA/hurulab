# hurulab trust badges, how to get each one

Researched July 10, 2026, from the official program pages. Eight badges, ranked by what they cost, how long they take, and whether hurulab can realistically get them. One is out of reach and one is a decoy, the rest are gettable.

## The short answer, in order of attack

1. Free and fast, start this week: NVIDIA Inception, Dell Technologies partner, HPE technology partner, and an AIEC evaluation enquiry (Taiwan government, testing currently free).
2. Real money and months, start when there is budget: ISO 27001 first, then SOC 2.
3. The long game: ISO 42001 (AI management, rare and very on-brand), Google Developer Expert (personal, 12 to 24 months), Dell AI ecosystem certified and HPE Unleash AI (both need a real software product plus a vendor relationship).
4. Skip: NVIDIA Cloud Validated. It is for companies operating GPU cloud data centers, not consultancies.

---

## 1. NVIDIA Inception (free, about 1 month)

What it is: NVIDIA's free program for AI startups. Membership includes the member badge, cloud credits, hardware discounts, and free deep learning institute training. No fee, no equity, rolling admission.

The catch: consulting and outsourced development firms are explicitly excluded. hurulab must apply as a company building its own AI product, not as an agency. If the website reads as an agency site, expect rejection.

Steps:

1. Decide which owned product to apply on (a productized tool, platform, or SaaS hurulab is developing) and make it visible on the hurulab site before applying.
2. Confirm the basics: incorporated entity, under 10 years old, at least one developer employed, working public website.
3. Create an NVIDIA developer account with a hurulab or moreharvest email, not a personal one.
4. Apply at programs.nvidia.com/phoenix/application with a 3 to 4 sentence product description that has a clear AI angle. A short deck helps.
5. On acceptance, download the badge from the member portal marketing kit and add it to the site.

Cost: zero. Timeline: 1 to 2 hours to apply, 2 to 4 weeks for review.

Fallback: if there is no ownable product story yet, apply to NVIDIA Connect instead (nvidia.com/en-us/programs/isv), which is free and explicitly open to service providers. Note you can be in Inception or Connect, not both, and Connect may not include a public badge.

## 2. Dell Technologies partner (free, about 1 week)

What it is: there is no published badge literally named "Dell AI ISV partner". The competitor most likely displays either the generic Dell Technologies partner badge (easy) or a Dell AI ecosystem certified designation (harder, solution level, from the 2026 Dell AI Factory ecosystem program).

Steps for the easy version:

1. Register at dell.my.site.com/PartnerEnrollment, country Taiwan, track solution provider (the open track). Needs the business registration number, proof of registration, and a company website with a matching email domain.
2. Approval is quoted at 5 to 7 business days. The authorized tier is free.

Steps for the AI ecosystem version, later: it needs an actual software product validated against Dell AI Factory reference architectures (compatibility, security, reliability tests run in Dell's self-validation lab). Entry is through the Dell Taiwan partner team, not a self-service form. Expect 1 to 4 months of engineering effort once engaged.

Cost: free at entry. Timeline: about a week for partner status.

## 3. HPE technology partner (free entry, days to weeks)

What it is: HPE's route for ISVs. Note the timing: HPE is unifying everything into Partner Ready Vantage on November 1, 2026, so mechanics change this year. The "AI ISV" style badge at HPE is the Unleash AI program, which is curated and effectively invitation only (members like CrowdStrike and Trend Micro, vetted, with solutions validated on HPE Private Cloud AI).

Steps:

1. Register on the HPE partner portal at partner.hpe.com/web/prp, country Taiwan, technology partner path. The entry tier has historically been free.
2. Sign the technology partner agreement to get baseline partner status and portal access.
3. If a validated badge is wanted later, engage the technology validation center to test a product on HPE infrastructure (historically about USD 5,000 per year at the tier that includes testing).
4. Unleash AI is the long game: build joint customer stories first, then ask HPE's AI business development team for nomination. Realistically 6 to 18 months for a small unknown ISV.
5. Reconfirm program mechanics after November 1, 2026.

Cost: free at entry. Timeline: days to weeks for baseline status.

## 4. AIEC (Taiwan AI evaluation center, currently free, weeks to months)

What it is: the AIEC badge is from Taiwan's artificial intelligence evaluation center (aiec.org.tw), a Ministry of Digital Affairs initiative run with the national institute of cyber security and ITRI. It evaluates AI products and systems (accuracy, reliability, fairness, privacy, security) and issues test reports, certificates, and a usage mark. For a Taiwan AI company this is the most on-brand badge of the eight, and testing is currently free during the pilot phase.

The catch: it evaluates a specific AI product or system, not the company. hurulab needs a deployed AI system or an API endpoint to submit.

Steps:

1. Pick the product or system to submit. Online models need an accessible API endpoint, offline models a download link.
2. Read the submission instructions at aiec.org.tw and download the application documents.
3. Email AIECService@aiec.org.tw or call +886 2 6631 6463 to scope the evaluation and confirm current fees and certification status (the scheme is transitioning from pilot to formal certification, so verify before promising the badge).
4. Submit the testing request, pass the pre-check, and let the testing laboratory run the evaluation.
5. Apply to the certification body with the test report for the certificate and usage mark, then display it per their rules.

Cost: testing currently free per the national science and technology council page. Timeline: not published, expect weeks to a few months.

## 5. ISO/IEC 27001 (roughly USD 10,000 to 30,000 year one, 6 to 12 months)

What it is: the international information security management certification. A real certification: an accredited body audits the company and issues a certificate valid three years, with its own certification mark (the round certified badge on the competitor site is a certification body's mark; check the certificate number on it and look it up in that body's public directory). Any size of company can be certified.

Steps:

1. Scope the ISMS to the whole company, simplest at hurulab's size.
2. Pick a route: compliance platform (Vanta, Drata, Secureframe, roughly USD 10,000 per year, least manual work) or a DIY toolkit (a few hundred dollars, more work).
3. Run a gap analysis against the 93 controls of the 2022 standard, then build the ISMS: risk assessment, policies, asset inventory, incident management, training.
4. Operate it for a few months, run the mandatory internal audit and management review.
5. Get quotes from accredited bodies in Taiwan: BSI Taiwan, SGS Taiwan, TUV Rheinland Taiwan. Audits run in Chinese or English.
6. Pass the stage 1 audit (documentation) and stage 2 audit (implementation). Certificate issued, valid 3 years with annual surveillance audits.
7. Display that body's certification mark per its brand rules.

Cost: small-scope audits in Taiwan commonly run NTD 200,000 to 400,000, plus the platform or consultant. Timeline: 3 to 6 months achievable with a platform, 6 to 12 typical.

Add-on worth knowing: ISO/IEC 42001, the AI management system standard from 2023. Still rare, so more differentiating than 27001 for an AI consultancy, and much cheaper when bolted onto an existing 27001 ISMS with the same certification body. Recommended sequence: 27001 first, 42001 second.

## 6. AICPA SOC 2 (roughly USD 20,000 to 40,000 year one, 6 to 12 months)

What it is: not a certification but an attestation. A licensed CPA firm audits the company's controls against the AICPA trust services criteria and issues a private report. Companies with at least one clean SOC report may register with AICPA to display the official SOC for service organizations badge, linked to aicpa.org/soc4so. Type II (controls observed over 3 to 12 months) is the one buyers take seriously.

Steps:

1. Scope it: which system, and start with the security criteria only.
2. Use the same compliance platform as for ISO 27001 (Vanta, Drata, and Secureframe cover both, so doing 27001 and SOC 2 together is much cheaper than separately).
3. Implement the controls: MFA and SSO, access reviews, device management, vendor management, incident response, an annual pen test.
4. Pick a licensed CPA firm. Remote US firms are the standard route for non-US companies (startup-focused ones include Prescient Assurance, Johanson Group, Insight Assurance). Big 4 Taiwan firms also do it at higher cost.
5. Optionally do a fast Type I first, then open the Type II observation window (3 to 6 months for a first report).
6. On a clean report, register via the AICPA logo registration form, download the badge, display it with the link. Renew annually with each new report.

Cost: audit USD 7,000 to 20,000 for Type II via a startup-focused firm, plus platform and pen test, so USD 20,000 to 40,000 all in for year one. Timeline: 6 to 12 months to a first Type II. Wording rule: never say "SOC 2 certified", it is attested, not certified.

## 7. Google Developer Expert (free, 12 to 24 months, personal)

What it is: individual recognition, not a company badge. It cannot be bought and belongs to the person, not to hurulab. A referral from a current expert or a Google employee is mandatory to even get the application form. Categories are technology based (AI and machine learning, web, Flutter, cloud); there is no design category, so the realistic candidate is a hurulab engineer.

Steps for that person:

1. Pick one Google technology and commit, most on-brand: AI and machine learning, or web.
2. Start a contribution log now: every talk, article, video, and open source contribution with dates, links, and audience size. Reviewers ask for exactly this.
3. Build the public track record over 12 to 24 months: talks at GDG Taipei and DevFest Taiwan, technical articles (bilingual works well, English strengthens the application), open source, workshops. Roughly one substantial contribution every month or two.
4. Meet the Taiwan GDEs and Google Taiwan developer relations people at those events, and ask for a referral once the track record is real.
5. Pass the application, the community interview with a current expert, and the product interview with Google. The title lasts one year and renews on continued activity.

Cost: free, paid in time. Timeline: the process is weeks once referred; the track record is the year-plus part.

## 8. NVIDIA Cloud Validated (skip)

What it is: a mark for cloud service providers whose NVIDIA GPU data center infrastructure passed NVIDIA validation. The current versions are NVIDIA Cloud Partner and Exemplar Cloud, held by the likes of Lambda, Nebius, and Oracle. It requires operating a commercial GPU cloud at data center scale, tens of millions of dollars of hardware. The exact "cloud validated" phrasing no longer appears on nvidia.com at all, so the competitor's badge is likely legacy.

Verdict: out of reach for any consultancy without its own GPU cloud, and a category error to chase. If the competitor showing it does not operate GPU infrastructure either, the badge is inherited or displayed outside its intended scope, which is worth knowing when selling against them. The Inception badge is the attainable NVIDIA mark.

## Honest reading of the competitor's badge wall

Of their eight badges, only three cost real money and effort (SOC 2, ISO, and the cloud validation, if it is even theirs). The vendor partner badges are largely free registrations, and the strongest signals for a Taiwan buyer are the government-backed AIEC mark and the audited ones. hurulab can match the free tier in weeks, the AIEC mark as soon as there is a submittable product, and the audited tier within a year on a modest budget. That ordering also matches the brand: proof over hype, one real number per claim.
