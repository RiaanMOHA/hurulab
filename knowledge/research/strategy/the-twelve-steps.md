# hurulab strategy

> **Read with `../../contradictions.md`.** This document is built on a one-person
> studio model and treats its conclusions as provisional pending the CTO's input,
> which was never given. The interviews describe a team of five. Its market claims
> are sourced; its claims about hurulab are not.


The complete strategy, built as twelve standard steps, in order. Steps 1 and 2 are the inputs. Steps 3 to 8 are the core analysis. Steps 9 to 12 turn it into action. This document called itself the single source of truth when it was written; it is not one now, and `docs/design-rules/brand.md` holds that role. The Miro board mirrors these same twelve steps. Updated July 15, 2026. Provisional until Ed (CTO) has added his input.

## Step 1. Internal audit

What the people inside think hurulab is, in their own words, gathered through the stakeholder interviews. Status: done for five of six. Henry (founder), Cal (product manager), Riaan (lead designer), BY (team member), and Pedro (web developer). Ed (CTO) still to go.

Where they agree: we solve the client's real business problem, we understand it first and then build, AI is a capability not the whole story, outcomes over process, referrals drive growth, and we never ship slop. Founder's sharpening: measurable return, the productivity of capital. Cal's sharpening: a partner, not an order taker. Riaan's sharpening: we make ourselves useless, the client gets the product plus tools plus training and only needs us once. BY's sharpening: technology made human, proof over hype, and the first technical read, know when to use AI and when to use ordinary code. Pedro's sharpening: craft, reusable playbooks and code that make every next project faster, and humans check everything, AI drafts, people verify, nothing ships without review. Open tensions: the word agency (Henry, Riaan, and BY keep it, Cal prefers consultancy partner, Pedro says technology partner), how central AI is (leaning: keep agency, lead on outcomes, AI as a capability, Pedro reinforces, clients buy outcomes not AI), and now decision-making (Riaan, Cal, and BY want fast with a clear owner, Pedro wants consensus among the core).

## Step 2. Market and competitor research

The field, who else is out there and how they win. Status: drafted, in the competitor analysis and benchmarks. Four occupied corners in Taiwan: prestige design studios (Block, Bito, JL), enterprise UX shops (AJA, UserXper), engineering and dev houses (Duotify, 5xRuby), and AI-first volume shops (Tenten, NSS). Pricing runs from template shops at the bottom to full-custom houses at the top. They win by referral, portfolio, and process. To watch: Tenten moving down-market, the FraserTec model crossing from Hong Kong, and the global fast-build wave.

## Step 3. Target audience

Who it is for: companies with a real goal, big or small, tired of agencies that overcharge and underdeliver, that want AI to actually pay off. Who it is not for: traditional companies that will not accept change, and buyers who just want a supplier to take orders.

## Step 4. The problem

The client's world before us: burned by slow, costly agencies that deliver meetings and pictures, money spent on tools and AI with nothing to show, and a lack of the skill or scale to reach their goals. The problem they will not say out loud: they do not actually know what they need, they feel out of their depth, and they are afraid of choosing wrong. The question behind the company: why is so much capital wasted.

## Step 5. Value proposition

What the client gets. You use the real working product before you pay for it. We hand it over with the tools and training, so you only need us once. We show you the return, what it earned and what it saved. Business, design, and technology in one team, delivered fast.

## Step 6. Differentiators

What only we offer, and what is hard to copy. Business, design, and technology in one team. We understand the business problem first, then build. Design taste, so the output never looks like AI slop. A proven, repeatable method, not one-off experiments. And the client uses a working version before committing, which nobody else in this market offers.

## Step 7. Market positioning

Where we sit against everyone else. The four corners are crowded; the open lane nobody holds is working software the client uses before committing, with senior design judgment and governed AI, sold to companies that need a real result. We keep the word agency but redefine it: the agency that makes itself useless. AI is a strong capability, not the headline. Provisional decision, revisitable when Ed weighs in. BY and Pedro have since answered and both support the lean.

## Step 8. SWOT

Strengths: the only demo-first motion, use it before you commit; AI governance shown as evidence, not claimed; business, design, and tech in one team; a warm referral network. Weaknesses: no published proof yet; heavy reliance on one or two people; no presence in the channels this market checks; no engineering bench yet. Opportunities: publish real outcome numbers, which no agency here does; own the succession lane; convert buyers burned by cheap AI. Threats: Tenten and FraserTec moving in; price compression from cheap AI builders; the fast-build wave; the free-means-scam reflex if the demo is ever framed as free.

## Step 9. What we stand for

The one core idea the brand is built on. We learn your business, build the working thing, and hand it over, so you use it before you pay and only need us once. The agency that makes itself useless.

## Step 10. Voice and messaging

How we say it. Plain, specific, calm, and honest to a fault. One real number per claim. No hype, no AI buzzwords. Lead promise: use it before you pay, with make ourselves useless right behind it, and proven return as the proof. Headlines to test: use it before you pay for it; we build it, you use it, then we go; the agency that makes itself useless. AI named as a capability in the support line, never the headline.

## Step 11. Go-to-market

How we reach clients and grow. The engine is referral: clients bringing other clients, starting from Henry's network. Convert skeptics with an invitation-only working demo, never free. Publish every early win as a case study with real numbers. Large clients for stable cash flow, small clients to stay sharp.

## Step 12. Goals and the long game

What success looks like and how we last. The real signal it is working: the method delivers measurable results across at least three different industries, and clients start referring us. Rules never to break: never take a client we cannot truly help, never ship slop, never just take the order, and never burn out the team. Reserve so a bad month cannot sink us: cash, low fixed costs, recurring revenue, a lean team of real talent. The riskiest assumption to check first: that clients will pay for this, and will work as a partner, not expect an order taker.
