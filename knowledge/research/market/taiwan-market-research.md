## 1. Taiwan: the home market

### The size of the pond

- Taiwan has more than 1.6 million small and medium enterprises, over 98 percent of all enterprises, employing about 9.2 million people (over 80 percent of the workforce). Source: Ministry of Economic Affairs SME white paper data, https://www.sme.gov.tw/article-en-2819-9515 and https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/biz/archives/2022/10/31/2003787988
- SME revenue exceeded NT$26 trillion, with almost 90 percent of sales generated in the domestic market. Source: same MOEA white paper data.
- Family ownership dominates. Around 54 percent of Taiwan's listed companies are family businesses, and the proportion is far higher among unlisted SMEs, where roughly 97 percent of enterprises are SMEs and family-run structures are the norm. Sources: EY and NCCU survey reported by Economic Daily, https://money.udn.com/SSI/2023/topic_enterprise_second_generation_2/ and NTUST thesis data, https://etheses.lib.ntust.edu.tw/thesis/detail/7338753fde3c94781ae92422c219d716/?seq=2

### The two forces that create hurulab's moment

**Force 1: the succession wave.** The average age of founders of Taiwan's top 100 companies is over 68, and PwC data shows 54 percent of Taiwanese family businesses are still managed by the first generation, well above the international average. Succession is expected to peak now, within the next two years. Source: Economic Daily succession series, https://money.udn.com/SSI/2023/topic_enterprise_second_generation/index.html. Roughly 65 percent of Taiwan's SMEs are facing second-generation succession. Source: Sunrise Media on the Taiwan digital enterprise association, https://sunrisemedium.com/p/391/tdea. The successor generation is the natural buyer: they inherit the credibility problem of an outdated digital presence and need visible modernization wins that do not require firing anyone or betting the company.

**Force 2: the digital gap.** The MOEA's own survey found that more than half of Taiwan's SMEs still use simple word processors as their primary digitalization tool. Source: https://www.sme.gov.tw/article-en-2819-9515. This is the Excel-and-paper reality the brand must speak to. **Corrected.** An earlier version of this paragraph claimed that over 60 percent of SMEs receiving digital transformation subsidies reported no measurable improvement. That figure is not citable: no MOEA survey, academic study or government report was found behind it, and it circulates verbatim in vendor marketing without a source. The citable version is PwC Taiwan's 2022 SME survey: 53.5 percent of digitally investing SMEs saw less than a 5 percent revenue increase, and about 70 percent of SMEs use only basic digital tools. Source: https://www.pwc.tw/zh/publications/topic-report/sme-digitalisation-survey-2022.html. The argument survives, tools without judgment fail, but it must be made with the PwC numbers.

### Government subsidies hurulab can ride

- **TCloud cloud marketplace (臺灣雲市集): ended, do not cite.** This entry previously described NT$30,000 in digital points. The official site states the point subsidy was exhausted and stopped on 15 March 2024 (https://tcloud.gov.tw/about). TCloud now operates as a SaaS catalogue with occasional themed subsidies. The 2026 successor point-style programs cap at NT$20,000 per micro-business (https://metabiz.tw/smb-ai-digital-transformation-subsidy-guide-2026/).
- **Digital transformation subsidy for companies under 30 employees:** up to NT$100,000 with a 30 to 50 percent co-payment. Source: https://actgsys.com/en/blog/taiwan-sme-ai-subsidy-guide-2026
- **Diversified revitalization program (多元振興):** proposal-based tracks for AI adoption and multi-store upgrades, where quantified outcome targets score well. Source: https://metabiz.tw/smb-ai-digital-transformation-subsidy-guide-2026/
- **SBIR and SIIR:** R&D and service innovation grants up to NT$3 million for individual applications. Sources: https://sbir.org.tw/en and https://startup.sme.gov.tw/en/incentive
- Note: 2026 program rules ban Chinese-mainland-brand ICT products in subsidized purchases, which favors hurulab's stack. Source: metabiz guide above.

The strategic use is not the money itself (the amounts are small relative to a Jadegia-scale project). It is the door: a subsidy the client can claim reduces the perceived risk of saying yes, and positions hurulab as the guide who knows the paperwork.

## 2. Competitor landscape and pricing

Where hurulab sits: the Jadegia engagement at NT$900,000 for a full Shopify Plus storefront with trilingual setup, payment integration, an owned AI content workflow, SEO migration, QA, and training sits at the top of the market on price but delivers what the NT$1 million-plus full-custom tier delivers, in a fraction of the time, with a decision process (live variants) that no tier offers at any price. hurulab does not compete on price. It competes on certainty and on things the other tiers cannot produce.

### The specific weakness of every traditional tier

Every tier above shares one process: the client approves pictures (mockups, wireframes, style decks) before code exists. The prior agency that Jadegia fired delivered exactly that: a style deck. hurulab's process inverts it, and no incumbent tier can copy this quickly, because their economics depend on billing the team that mockups exist to coordinate.

### AI-native studios (the closest true competitors)

Globally, a wave of AI-first product studios now sells "prototype in days" services, and AI-assisted development is compressing everyone's timelines. Two things keep hurulab differentiated for now:

1. Almost all AI-native studios target startups and tech-adjacent buyers in English-speaking markets. Nearly none target traditional Chinese-speaking family businesses, in their language, through their trust networks.
2. Almost all sell speed alone. hurulab's documented governance (locked source-of-truth files, human conflict resolution, reality-checked QA, written handoffs) answers the exact failure mode behind the weak returns PwC measured, where 53.5 percent of digitally investing SMEs saw under a 5 percent revenue increase. Speed is copyable; a documented method plus local trust is not.

The window matters: this advantage is a 2 to 4 year head start, not a permanent moat. The go-to-market plan should assume competitors with similar tooling appear in Taiwan within that window.

### Big consulting, for contrast

The global firms (McKinsey, Accenture, Deloitte and peers) sell AI transformation strategy to enterprises at fees starting where hurulab's whole projects end. They do not serve the NT$100,000 to NT$2,000,000 project band, and they deliver reports before software. Useful only as a contrast in sales conversations: they advise about AI; we ship with it.

## 3. Buyer psychology: the traditional Taiwanese owner

Research-backed patterns, each with a direct implication for hurulab:

- **Guanxi and referrals rule.** Foreigners and outsiders seeking partnerships with Taiwanese family SMEs are explicitly advised to build a relationship with the owner and family first; deals follow relationships, not proposals. Source: Association for Asian Studies overview of Taiwan SMEs, https://www.asianstudies.org/publications/eaa/archives/taiwans-smes/. Implication: cold outreach will underperform; introductions and lighthouse clients are the channel.
- **Face (mianzi) shapes every meeting.** An outdated website is quietly embarrassing; pointing at it directly costs face. Implication: never open with what is wrong. Open with what the business deserves ("a site worthy of that room," as the Jadegia story puts it), and let the demo make the gap obvious without saying it.
- **Seeing beats hearing.** The same cultural literature stresses tangible results and long courtships before trust. Implication: the demo-first motion is not just differentiation, it is cultural fit. A live link the owner can tap is proof in a form this buyer already trusts.
- **The generational triangle.** Succession research describes first-generation owners who avoid naming successors and successors who cannot push change without seeming to audit their parents. Third parties are explicitly recommended as coaches and bridges to defuse father-son tension. Sources: https://money.udn.com/SSI/2023/topic_enterprise_second_generation/index.html and the NexTech Academy successor communication curriculum, https://startup.sme.gov.tw/ntacademy/. Implication: hurulab can be the neutral third party through whom the successor modernizes without confronting the founder. Sell to the successor; give the founder the face of the final decision (which the live-variants process does naturally: the founder chooses).
- **AI hesitancy is really risk hesitancy.** The documented failure of tool-first subsidy spending shows these buyers have already been burned by technology purchases that changed nothing. They do not fear AI as such; they fear another useless purchase. Implication: lead with supervision and outcomes ("the AI never works unsupervised, and you approve only what you can use"), never with AI capability claims.
- **Triggers that convert hesitation into budget:** succession (new decision-maker needs a visible win), a competitor moving online, prestige mismatch (a Taipei 101 address with a 2010 website), cross-border ambition (selling to or buying from Japan and Southeast Asia), and available subsidies that make the first step feel discounted.

## 4. The four rings: market sizing and expansion logic

### Ring 1: Taiwan (year 1 focus)

- Serviceable market: the heritage and premium slice of 1.6 million SMEs. Even a narrow definition (established firms with premium products, wealthy customers, and weak digital presence, concentrated in Taipei, Taichung, Tainan, Kaohsiung) yields thousands of qualified targets, of which hurulab needs perhaps 8 to 12 per year.
- Advantages: physical presence, the Jadegia lighthouse, existing MoreHarvest network, language, subsidy literacy.
- Entry cost: near zero; this is home.

### Ring 2: Chinese-speaking APAC (from month 9 to 12, opportunistic earlier)

- Hong Kong: comparable pricing (canned sites HK$5,000 to 15,000, custom e-commerce HK$100,000 to 300,000, source: https://www.hellotoby.com/zh-hk/c/香港網頁開發價格資訊), dense heritage retail (jewelry, tea, tailoring), same language, same face-and-guanxi dynamics. Enters through referrals and the Jadegia story, which translates culturally with zero adaptation.
- Singapore: wealthier buyers, English-plus-Chinese, strong government digitalization grants (PSG and related schemes) that mirror the Taiwan subsidy door-opener. Gateway to Southeast Asian Chinese business networks.
- Malaysia: large Chinese business community, price-sensitive but relationship-driven; treat as referral-fed, not actively hunted.
- Logic: same buyer psychology, same language, same story assets. The cost of entry is travel and one local introducer per city, not a new playbook.

### Ring 3: rest of APAC, Japan first (from year 2)

- Japan is the standout because hurulab already operates there (Kumamoto) and the macro tailwind is documented: METI's 2025 digital cliff warns of losses up to 12 trillion yen (about US$79 billion) per year from unmodernized systems. Sources: https://www.weforum.org/stories/2024/04/how-can-japan-navigate-digital-transformation-ahead-of-a-2025-digital-cliff/ and https://www.cio.com/article/4074930/japans-digital-transformation-in-crisis.html
- Japan's DX services market is growing from about 1.8 trillion yen (2019) toward a projected 5.4 trillion yen by 2027. Source: https://resources.nihonium.io/glossary/digital-transformation-dx-japan
- Subsidy landscape: the IT introduction subsidy (IT導入補助金) covers up to about 4.5 million yen per SME, cutting client out-of-pocket cost by 50 to 75 percent, with 2026 revisions emphasizing AI adoption; the broader SME productivity program carries a budget of roughly 340 billion yen. Sources: nihonium above, https://scalingyourcompany.com/guide-to-startup-subsidies-in-japan/, https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/sme-technology-adoption-in-the-united-kingdom_cecfb794-en/japan-s-sme-productivity-revolution-programme_4b64c40d-en.html
- Reality check: vendor registration for Japanese subsidies requires Japanese documentation and registered consultants, and Japanese buyers are even more relationship- and process-bound than Taiwanese ones. Enter through the existing MoreHarvest and Kumamoto relationships, and consider a Japanese partner for subsidy registration.
- Rest of ring 3 (Korea, Thailand, Vietnam): defer; no structural advantage yet.

### Ring 4: rest of the world (year 3+, or opportunistic inbound)

- Here the guanxi advantage disappears and the method becomes the product. The sellable asset is the documented way of working: design-directed AI with governance, proven on heritage businesses. Channel: published case studies, the hurulab site itself as a demo of the method, and inbound from the global conversation about AI-native services. Do not spend outbound effort here before rings 1 to 3 produce referenceable external results.

## 5. The single most important research finding

The market's largest documented failure (PwC Taiwan 2022: 53.5 percent of digitally investing SMEs saw under a 5 percent revenue increase, and about 70 percent use only basic digital tools) is caused by exactly what hurulab's method fixes: tools without judgment, purchases without strategy, and no one checking the output against reality. Every other finding in this document is context. This one is the wedge.
