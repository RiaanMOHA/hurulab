## The four deliverables

All four are required. None is optional.

**1. Logo mockups.**

Three distinct mark directions for hurulab, each generated by a stated rule, not by styling. For each direction show:

- the mark alone, at 24px and at 200px
- the mark in lockup with a piece of body copy
- the pattern extension the rule produces when it keeps running

Show every direction on light ground and on dark ground. Deliver rendered images plus editable source.

Hard constraint, this has failed before: do not draw letterforms as SVG paths, rectangles or arcs from scratch. It never produces a real wordmark. Set the name in a real typeface and build the mark by operating on those letterforms: repetition, concentric offset, contour, masking, stroke expansion, subtraction. The rule acts on real type. If a direction cannot be made this way, describe it in words for Riaan to draw in vector instead of faking it.

This deliverable comes first. Stop after it. Nothing else starts until Riaan has chosen a direction.

**2. The chosen direction added to the coming soon preview.**

Update the coming soon page so it carries the chosen mark and any pattern or motion the rule implies.

Preserve exactly: one continuous passage of text, word by word darkening as scroll focus reaches each word, 24px gap between sentences, "coming soon" held as its own moment, email capture CTA, progress hairline, reduced motion fallback. Do not restructure the page.

Blocking dependency: the coming soon palette was never chosen. The live page is monochrome. A warm amber variant exists in `huru-lab-coming-soon-compare.html`. Ask Riaan to choose before editing. Do not guess.

**3. A design direction tracking file.**

Filename `huru-lab-design-direction.md`, saved in the same folder as the other hurulab markdown files.

It records, for every direction explored: the rule it runs on, what was made, why it was kept or killed, and what is still open. It is updated at the end of every working session, not at the end of the project. Sentence case, no em dashes, no emojis, sentences end with periods.

**4. The work posted to Miro.**

Not a dump. Build it as frames on the board, in this order:

- Frame 1: the reference reading. One paragraph stating the rule Mexico 68 runs on, in Cowork's own words.
- Frame 2 to 4: one frame per mark direction. Rule stated at the top, then the mark at both sizes, the lockup, the pattern extension, on both grounds.
- Frame 5: the palette test. Warm greige and amber against whatever the direction demands, side by side.

Leave a sticky on each direction frame asking for one vote and one objection, so Cal and Henry can respond.

## Scope

Cowork owns the visual identity: the mark, the type system, the color system, layout and grid rules, motion rules, the asset library, and eventually the published guidelines.

Cowork does not own strategy, positioning, messaging, or case study copy. Those are written and locked in the source files. Read them as constraints. Do not rewrite them. Do not put strategy inside brand guidelines.

Out of scope for this brief, do not attempt: the Chinese-script treatment of the mark (the Chinese name is undecided), and the Storybook build (it comes after a mark is signed off).

## Fixed facts, do not change

- The wordmark is hurulab. One word, no space. Not "hurulab". Body copy may still read "Huru lab" where the strategy files use it, but the mark is one word.
- Type trio, already chosen and tested: Schibsted Grotesk (display, 600 to 800), Instrument Sans (body, 400 to 600), Sometype Mono (record marks, 400 to 500). No serif. No italics anywhere. No fourth face. Hierarchy comes from weight and size, never a font swap.
- Color, current state: warm greige and sand base. Brand ink #1a1714. Secondary sand #e8e2d6. Accent amber #e0742a. Paper #f7f3ea. Panels #efe9dd. Semantic: success #3f7d52, warning #c08a2d, danger #b23b2e, info #3d6b8f. States: hover #c9631f, active #b0551a, focus ring #f4c79e, disabled #cfc7b8. The accent appears once per view, on the one thing that matters. This palette is open to challenge by the design direction, but only with both options shown.
- Spacing: hard 24px maximum on every vertical gap. Explicit pixel values, never clamps.
- Layout: text runs full width to the container margins. No narrow reading column.
- Writing rules, applying to every asset including labels inside guidelines: sentence case only, no title case, no uppercase, no em dashes, no emojis, sentences end with periods.
- Banned words anywhere: revolutionary, cutting-edge, game-changing, seamless, empower, leverage as a verb, synergy, AI-native, world-class.

## The reference: Mexico 68

The attached image is the Mexico 1968 Olympics identity by Lance Wyman, with Pedro Ramírez Vázquez and Eduardo Terrazas. Study it as a system, not as a picture.

- It is not a logo applied to things. It is a rule that generates things. The Olympic rings are absorbed into the numerals, the ring geometry propagates outward as concentric repeated strokes, and that same repetition builds the letterforms, then the pattern, then the signage, then the stadium. Identity and typography are the same object.
- The energy comes from repetition and optical vibration. Flat line, no gradient, no illustration, no ornament. Every effect is the rule running.
- It is rooted in local culture (Huichol yarn work, pre-Hispanic pattern) without being literal, folkloric or touristic.
- It survives at every scale, from a lapel pin to a building, because the rule does not care about size.

## Why it fits, and where it fights

It fits because:

- A rule that propagates outward from a source, and keeps running once it has left the source, is the identity of a studio whose promise is handover. The mark radiates and does not need the center to continue. That is "we make ourselves unnecessary" drawn.
- Machine repetition under a human-set rule is the supervised AI position exactly. A person draws the line. A machine draws the thousand.
- Local root without kitsch is the Taiwan problem. This market's premium design goes either global-generic or literal-heritage. Mexico 68 proves a third road exists.

It fights because:

- The locked direction so far is Swiss grotesque, restrained, quiet, warm neutral, accent used once. Mexico 68 is high-vibration op art. These do not automatically coexist.
- Resolve this before anything is drawn. The likely answer is that grotesque restraint holds for typography and interface, while the Mexico 68 logic lives in the mark, the pattern system and motion, appearing at full strength rarely. Propose the resolution. Do not assume it.

## Definition of done

Before showing anything to Riaan, check each item and fix what fails:

- Every mark direction has its rule written as one sentence. A direction without a stated rule is styling, and is thrown away.
- No letterform was drawn from scratch as a path or a primitive shape.
- Each direction was seen at 24px. Anything that dies at 24px is not a mark.
- No vertical gap exceeds 24px.
- No italics, no serif, no fourth typeface, no uppercase, no title case, no em dash, no emoji, no banned word.
- No strategy text sits inside a visual guidelines artifact.
- The tracking file records why the killed directions were killed, not only what survived.
