Hurulab plan

Step 1. Gather everyone's perspective

What it is. A short set of questions, sent to each stakeholder, that captures how each of us sees the agency and where it should sit, in our own words.

Why it needs doing. Positioning only holds if the whole team stands behind it. Before I write it, I need everyone answering the same questions, so we start on the same page instead of pulling in different directions.

How I will do it. I will send everyone the same prompt, ask each person to answer it in their own words, and send their answers back to me. I will read every response and use them as the raw material for the positioning.

What I expect. One set of answers from each stakeholder, in their own words, to build the positioning on.

Step 2. Research the market and competitors

What it is. A study of the studios and agencies that build high-end digital products, how they work, and what they charge.

Why it needs doing. We cannot say what makes us different until we can show where every competitor sits and where the gap is. Our home market is not known for design or software, so the benchmark is the global standard, not the local one.

How I will do it. I will list each competitor, record their prices, and note how buyers choose, then find the one space nobody has taken. I will also study why the best studios read as clear: their storytelling, their defined voice, and their consistent identity.

What I expect. A clear picture of the market, with named competitors, price ranges, and one open space for us.

Step 3. Positioning and strategy

What it is. The one thing that makes us different, and the plan to make it ours.

Why it needs doing. It turns the research and everyone's answers into a decision, and keeps every later choice pointing the same way.

How I will do it. I will write down what makes us different, the plan for winning clients, and a step-by-step timeline. Our difference is that design, engineering, and business work on the same problem together, so the client sees results in the room and decisions get made live. One recent build already shows this: it was delivered in about half the time and half the cost of a competitor's quote. We will not name the tools we use; we will translate the result into time, money, and manpower saved. And we will stand against subscription lock-in: we build products the client owns and controls, we train their staff to run them, then we step away.

What I expect. A short written strategy and a timeline everyone agrees on.

Step 4. What the brand stands for

What it is. The foundation of the brand: its name, its main idea, its personality, its headline, and the promise it makes.

Why it needs doing. Every font, color, and line of copy needs one clear idea to serve, or the brand pulls in different directions.

How I will do it. I will write the main idea, the promises we make, the headline, and the personality the brand should have, benchmarked against the strongest independent creative studios, for example Sagmeister.

What I expect. A short summary of what the brand stands for, that the rest of the work builds on.

Step 5. Fonts

What it is. The fonts and the rules for using them.

Why it needs doing. A small, consistent set of fonts does most of the work of making the studio look established and high-end.

How I will do it. I will choose three fonts, each with one job, and set the sizes and spacing for headings and body text.

What I expect. A clear set of fonts and rules anyone can follow.

Step 6. Color

What it is. The colors and the rules for using them.

Why it needs doing. A calm palette with one highlight color looks controlled and confident. Color used everywhere cheapens the work.

How I will do it. I will set a warm neutral base, one highlight color used on just one thing at a time, and colors for buttons, warnings, and success messages, all easy to read.

What I expect. A complete set of colors that everyone finds easy to read.

Step 7. Logo and page layout

What it is. The logo and the rules for laying out a page.

Why it needs doing. A consistent logo and layout make the brand easy to recognize and give every page the same order.

How I will do it. I will set the working logo now, plan the final logo, and write the rules for spacing and page layout.

What I expect. A finished logo and a set of page templates.

Step 8. Brand voice

What it is. How the brand sounds when it writes.

Why it needs doing. A set way of writing keeps everything we publish sounding like one studio. This has been a recurring weakness for us: no rules, no banned words, and no consistent tone.

How I will do it. I will set the tone, agree the traits, and turn each one into a rule anyone can check their writing against. I will settle one spelling standard, British or American, agree the approved phrasing, and write the list of banned terms.

What I expect. A clear way of writing with simple rules.

Step 9. Case studies

What it is. A short, honest write-up of each project we have finished.

Why it needs doing. Case studies are the proof that the studio delivers. Without them, buyers only have our word.

How I will do it. I will answer the same fixed set of questions for each past project, covering why it was done, what it proved, and how it proved it, then write each one up the same way. Our own site stands for the traditional approach; the other projects, JJ, GK, Map, and Value Add, show the new way of working, where design, engineering, and business run together. JJ is the clearest proof: it was delivered in about half the time and half the cost of a competitor's quote. I will also run a small number of free projects, such as a clinic website, to build the portfolio without revenue pressure, and record every project as it is made.

What I expect. A finished set of case studies.

Step 10. Design guidelines

What it is. The rulebook that holds the whole brand in one place.

Why it needs doing. Rules only hold when they are written down, so anyone can use the brand without guessing.

How I will do it. I will write down the logo, fonts, colors, layout, and voice, with examples of right and wrong use, and put it all in one place online that anyone can use, for digital work, print, and consulting.

What I expect. One complete rulebook.

Step 11. Build and launch the website

What it is. The studio's website, its first public home.

Why it needs doing. The website is the first place the market sees who we are and what we do.

How I will do it. I will start with a text-only coming soon page that leads with the client's pain: you paid a lot, and now it is broken or locked behind a subscription; we build products you own, train your staff to run them, then step away. Then I will design the full site to the new brand and work with our web developer to build and launch it. The first design cycle is complete once the coming soon page is live.

What I expect. A live website.

Step 12. Set up our public pages

What it is. The places online where people check us out before they trust us.

Why it needs doing. Buyers and referrers look for a real, current footprint before they reach out.

How I will do it. I will set up a portfolio and the accounts this market uses, and keep them up to date.

What I expect. A credible set of public pages.
