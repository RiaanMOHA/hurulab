01 How it starts

It starts with watching how you work.

A short, defined piece of work in four parts, sized to the problem. On your site when that is the right place, remote when it is not. You will not read a proposal a fortnight later.

The four parts.

  1. 01

    We interview you

    The people who own the problem, and the people who live with it. They describe it differently, and the difference matters.

  2. 02

    We watch you work

    Wherever the work happens. What your team does differs from what the process document says, and that gap is usually where the money sits.

  3. 03

    We look at your systems

    What you run today, what it costs, what it will not do, and what your team has built in spreadsheets to work around it.

  4. 04

    We build it in the room

    A working version of your problem, changed in front of you as you react to it. The person doing that is a forward deployed engineer who sits with you instead of taking notes back to an office.

Why a day is enough

We build it while we are there. Nothing to specify, review, revise and re-agree, which is where the months go.

05 Where the return comes from

The problem is rarely only the technology.

Most companies we meet pay every year for software that fits them approximately, that they cannot change, and that they cannot leave.

Three things you stop paying for.

Faster build, testing and delivery has changed what a bespoke system costs. Work that used to justify a licence fee, because commissioning your own was unthinkable, is now short enough to be worth owning outright. That takes away three bills at the same time.

The licence. Gone, with the annual rise and the seat count you keep explaining to somebody else's account manager.

The long tail. No eighteen-month project billing steadily before anything usable exists. The first working version arrives on day one.

The perpetual contract. Support and maintenance because you want it, not because your system is untouchable by anyone else.

Three ways to solve the same problem
Dimension Off-the-shelf or white label A traditional development house A system built around you
What it costs you A subscription or licence fee, every year, forever, rising as you grow. A large one-off build, then a support contract you cannot easily exit. Paid once. From then on you own it.
How well it fits Roughly. The last twenty per cent is the part your business competes on, and it is the part the product will not do. As well as the spec was written, which is as well as anyone could imagine it before seeing it. Built around the way you work, because we watched you work before we built anything.
Who changes to fit whom You reshape your process around the product, then keep paying for the privilege. You pay for every change request, so most of them go unmade. The system fits the business. That is the entire point of spending the day first.
Who can change it later The vendor, on their roadmap, if enough other customers want the same thing. Them, on their rates and their availability. Your team, once we have shown them how. Or us, if you prefer. It is your choice.

The arithmetic you have not been shown.

Between a subscription that runs forever and a system paid for once, there is a point where building costs less than renting.

For most companies we meet it arrives sooner than they assume, because the licence they measure against keeps rising while the thing they would own does not.

We will not put a payback period on your business before we have seen your numbers, and you should be wary of anyone who does. Inside the day we work out where that point sits for you, using what you pay today. If renting turns out to be cheaper in your case, that is a real answer, and you will get it from us.

Independence is the thing you cannot buy off a shelf.

Changing your own systems when the business changes. Keeping the lights on with the team you already have. Paying once for something built to fit, instead of every year for the eighty per cent that nearly does. That is what owning it gets you, and it is why we hand you the everyday work and ask to be called back for the jobs that need us.
