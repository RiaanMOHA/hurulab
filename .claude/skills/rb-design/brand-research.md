# Brand authenticity: the grounding research

This is the verified foundation the brand loop stands on. Read this file before
running the loop. Do not run the loop from a remembered version of the framework,
it drifts. Everything below was confirmed against primary and credible secondary
sources, cited at the end. Where a detail could not be verified, it is flagged as
a gap rather than invented.

## The core principle (this drives everything)

Authenticity is never something a brand declares about itself. It is attributed
by the audience, and only when there is real evidence behind it. The word
"authentic" in a sentence does no work. A specific, verifiable fact on each
dimension does all the work. The entire job of the loop is to replace claims with
proof.

The research (tracing back to Beverland, and to Grayson and Martinec) says people
read three kinds of cue when they judge a brand as genuine:

- **Indexical cues.** Actual, verifiable evidence. A real date, a real maker, a
  documented decision, a recorded number, a named case. This is the gold standard.
- **Iconic cues.** The brand merely resembles something authentic (looks vintage,
  sounds heritage, is styled to feel hand-made). Resemblance, not proof.
- **Existential cues.** The brand connects to the person's own sense of self.

The loop privileges indexical proof and refuses to let iconic dressing-up stand
in for it. That single distinction is the spine of the whole method.

## The framework: two validated models

### Napoli, Dickinson-Delaporte, Beverland and Farrelly (2014)

"Measuring consumer-based brand authenticity," Journal of Business Research,
67(6), 1090 to 1098. The first validated consumer-side measure of brand
authenticity. Finding: authenticity is not one thing. It is 14 measured items
loading onto three first-order factors that roll up into a single higher-order
authenticity construct. The factors, with their real weighting and example items
(verbatim wording recovered from the published scale):

**1. Quality commitment (7 items, the heaviest factor). Proof of excellence.**
Uncompromising devotion to craft, standards, and outcomes. The continuous promise
to keep making to the same or a better standard. Example items:
- "made by a master craftsman who pays attention to detail and is involved
  throughout the production process"
- "committed to retaining long-held quality standards"
- "has a mark of distinction that signifies quality"

**2. Heritage (5 items). Proof of origin and continuity.**
Historical roots, tradition, cultural anchoring, a line held over time. Example
items:
- "a strong link to the past, which is still celebrated to this day"
- "a strong connection to a historical period, culture or region"
- "exudes a sense of tradition"
- "builds on long-held traditions"

**3. Sincerity (2 items). Proof of principle.**
Genuine intent, honesty, holding to founding values rather than chasing the
purely commercial. Example items:
- "remains true to its espoused values"
- "refuses to compromise the values upon which it was founded"
- "has stuck to its principles"

### Morhart, Malar, Guevremont, Girardin and Grohmann (2015)

"Brand authenticity: an integrative framework and measurement scale," Journal of
Consumer Psychology. A parallel measure with four dimensions (a 15-item scale).
This is where the optional fourth axis, symbolism, comes from. The dimensions,
with item wording as operationalised in applied studies:

- **Continuity** (Morhart's version of heritage). "remains faithful to its
  values; survives time; stays consistent with its products and image."
- **Credibility.** "will not betray you; accomplishes its value promise; honest;
  reliable and trustworthy."
- **Integrity.** "gives back; true to a set of moral values; cares about its
  customers; transparent."
- **Symbolism.** "adds meaning to people's lives; reflects values people care
  about; connects people with their real selves; connects people to a cause."

## The four dimensions the loop uses

The loop works the three Napoli factors as its core, plus the Morhart symbolism
axis as an optional fourth, added when the category is crowded or identity matters
to the audience:

1. **Quality commitment.** Proof of excellence. Named methods, a signature
   process, credentials, real results with real numbers and named cases.
2. **Heritage.** Proof of origin and continuity. The real catalyst moment, the
   formative why, the line from past to present.
3. **Sincerity.** Proof of principle. Values shown through an action, a trade-off
   named, work turned down, something sacrificed for a belief.
4. **Symbolism (optional).** Proof of meaning. How the brand helps its audience
   express who they are or connect to what matters to them.

## Why it is worth building on

The payoff is established across multiple studies: higher perceived authenticity
reliably tracks with stronger brand equity, loyalty, and willingness to pay. So a
true, proof-backed brand foundation is not decoration. It earns its place as the
thing every other design decision sits on.

## The honest gap

Napoli's exact 14 survey sentences sit partly behind a paywall. The definitions,
the factor structure, the item counts (7 quality commitment, 5 heritage, 2
sincerity), and several verbatim items were recovered and are quoted above. A few
of the precise quality-commitment sentences were not recovered. This does not
block the method, the dimensions and their meaning are what the loop needs, but it
is recorded here rather than papered over.
