Seven areas, and most clients need three or four of them. Each one starts from something we recognise on the day rather than from a list you have to choose off. We would rather narrow it down at the start than bill you for the rest.

Discovery and strategy

You know what you want built, but not yet what it should change. Who the customer is, what the commercial bet is, and who you are really up against are all still open.

What we do
  • Market and competitor research
  • Strategy and positioning
  • Modelling how the business would actually run
Brand and design

You look different in every place a customer already finds you, and nothing decides how a page should be laid out. What you have does not fit the people you are trying to reach.

What we do
  • Brand and identity
  • Interface and experience design
Build

What you need does not exist yet, or what you already have cannot do the job. This starts once we both know what the business is trying to change.

What we do
  • Architecture
  • Development
  • Integrations with what you already run
  • Testing
  • Accessibility
Configuration

You have a catalogue already, or new products to add, and you want to sell or operate in more than one market.

What we do
  • Data and catalogue setup
  • Localisation
  • Multiple languages
Discoverability and analytics

You have a site with search rankings worth protecting, you are planning to run ads, or you cannot say much about what customers are doing on it today.

What we do
  • Search visibility, in Google and in AI answers
  • Analytics and tracking
  • Product feeds
Launch and handover

Nobody inside your business controls your own domain or your hosting, and there is no one on your side who could run the platform once it is live.

What we do
  • Hosting and your domain, set up in your name
  • Training and documentation, including managing your own content
Ops and support

Nothing is set aside to keep it running after launch. It is the part left out of almost every plan, so we raise it whether or not you ask.

What we do
  • Maintenance and fixes
  • Small improvements
  • A response agreement if you want one
Changed: the service list now matches the Miro board

The site was showing nine invented areas (Strategy, Solution design, Branding, UI and UX design, Engineering, Testing and quality, Configuration, Deployment, Handover and support) with sub-lists written for the site. Those did not match the seven buckets Ed endorsed on 22 July, so the published site was describing a different service model to the internal one. Now taken from "Huru Lab - How we approach the client".

Titles are Ed's seven, unchanged. The lead paragraph in each card is the board's "triggered when we learnt" column, turned from "they" into "you", because that column is the only client-facing writing on the board: it describes what the client feels rather than what we deliver. The "what we do" list is the board's description column in plain English, so a technical reader who gets forwarded the link still sees the actual work.

Two triggers had to be written fresh because the board versions are internal notes rather than client situations. Build was "Almost always. Begins once the business aims are clear", which describes frequency. Ops and support ended "Always raise it, because they will not", which is an instruction to whoever is selling. Both need Ed's eye. Three wordings were also softened out of board vocabulary: infrastructure, hosting and DNS became hosting and your domain set up in your name; SEO and GEO became search visibility in Google and in AI answers; SLAs became a response agreement if you want one.
